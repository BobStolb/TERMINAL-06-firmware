## What the product is, and where it stands
* TERMINAL-06: an eight-tube nixie clock, to be built as a small batch (about ten units) and sold as an option-rich kit.
* Three boards, 191.4 mm wide: **TS06-DISP** (tubes, 191.4 x 44), **TS06-DRV** (Nano module, 12 V -> 185 V boost with LM393 +
  IRF840, 3 x K155ID1 cathode decoders, 7 x TLP627 anode opto-couplers, RTC), **fascia R rev B** (191.4 x 40; MODE rotary, FIELD
  and SUB levers, - and + buttons; two resistor ladders on A6 and A7). DRV plugs in 11 mm behind DISP on pin strips.
* **Rev B is drawn, checked by scripts, never built, never ordered** (owner, 2026-10-14: "the boards are not ordered yet"). Fab
  zips: `fab/` (`ORDER.md`, `README.md`). No bench measurement of a built board exists.
* **Rev C is planned, not routed:** `PCB/TS06-routing-plan.md` (LED-filament lights on DISP and behind the fascia, two 16-channel
  PWM chips, high-side P-FETs, a 7-way fascia connector). Six grills of the art faces: `PCB/art/*-grill.md`.
* The boards are **generated by scripts**, not drawn by hand in KiCad: `tools/mksch*.py`, `tools/mkpcb*.py`, `tools/mkpcb_*_routes.json`,
  `tools/astar.c`. Checks: `bash tools/verify_pair.sh` (here: 16 PASS, 0 FAIL, 5 SKIP; the KiCad-in-Docker checks skip in this
  container), `tools/checkpcb.py`, `checkcopper.py`, `checksch.py`, `checkmatch.py`, `dfm_check.py`, `kicad_checks.py`, `audit.py`.
* Earlier reviews (cite them, check whether their findings were closed, do not redo them): `grill.md`, `Claude outputs/TS06-pair-review.md`,
  `Claude outputs/TS06-routing-study.md`, `Claude outputs/TS06-split-study.md`, `Claude outputs/TERMINAL-06-anode-driver-study.md`,
  `PCB/TS06-pair-testing.md`, `PCB/README.md`, `PCB/TS06-DISP/bom.md`, `PCB/TS06-DRV/bom.md`, the owner's measurements

## Method (all runs)
1. Judge two things: **rev B as it is in the repo**, and **the rev C plan**. Say which a finding is about.
2. Number the findings. Severity: **Stop** (unsafe, or will not work), **Fix before order**, **Should fix**, **Note**.
3. Evidence class on every finding: *measured* (you computed it from the files; give the command or script), *inferred*, *assumed*.
   Give the source as `file:line` or a coordinate. Part data from memory is *assumed, not re-checked* unless you fetched it.
4. **Reconsider:** end with a table of the big choices in your area: what it is today, what you would pick starting today, the cost
   of changing, and **keep / change in rev C / change later**. Be willing to say "keep".
5. Web: read-only, at most 10 fetches, for part status or a datasheet value that decides a finding. No accounts, no purchases.
6. Large files: never read a whole `.kicad_pcb` or `.kicad_sch`. Use the repo's check scripts, or a short Python scan that prints
   a summary. Put your scripts in the scratchpad folder `audit/<run>/`, not in the repo.
7. **Docs only, no pictures.** Plain words, short sentences. Layout of your doc: the verdict in 3 lines, then a table with one
   line per finding (number, severity, finding, fix), then the detail, then Reconsider, then "Not checked". 3500 words at most.
8. Read and write rules: no edit to board, schematic, fab, tools, 3d, firmware, spec or page files. Write only your own doc in

## Run audit-bom -> `PCB/audit/TS06-audit-bom.md` (the BOM and the circuit, as a product)
* Every part against its stress: voltage, current, power, temperature. Start with the 185 V side (boost switch, diode, inductor
  saturation, output cap, divider and anode resistors' voltage rating and power, opto-coupler ratings, decoder outputs' off-state
  voltage), then the 12 V input and the 5 V rail (budget today, and with rev C's lights: up to 872 mA).
* Protection: input fuse, reverse polarity, over-voltage if the feedback opens, bleed-down time of the HV cap, touchable HV.
* Parts that are old, new-old-stock, single-source or module-only (K155ID1, the tubes, TLP627, the Nano module, the buttons,
  levers and rotary): risk for a batch, and a second source for each.
* Completeness: is everything needed to build a unit on a BOM (connectors, pin strips, wire, hardware, case parts, the rev C
  PWM chips, FETs, filaments, resistors)? Count and rough cost per unit and per batch (mark prices *assumed*).
* Life and use: tube life and the anti-poisoning routine, HV cap life, heat in a closed case, what fails first.
* Product safety and EMC in plain terms for a kit sold to hobbyists: what a seller should do at the least.
* Firmware match: the pins and nets the firmware uses (`ts06pair*` sketch folder) against the schematic.
* Reconsider at least: K155ID1 + opto-couplers against a high-voltage serial driver; the discrete boost against a controller IC
  or a module; the Nano module against a bare MCU; the 12 V input against USB-C; through-hole against SMD for a batch.

## Run audit-layout -> `PCB/audit/TS06-audit-layout.md` (the boards as built objects: DISP, DRV, fascia R rev B)
* Run the repo checks and report what they say. Then measure what they do not: the smallest distance from every 185 V net to
  every other net and to board edges and holes (clearance, and creepage along the surface), per board, with the worst five places.
* The boost converter's layout: the hot loop (switch, diode, output cap), its area, where the feedback divider and the ground
  return run. Decoupling: distance from each IC's supply pin to its capacitor. Ground: how returns flow with no plane.
* Track width against current for 12 V in, 5 V, HV, and the multiplexed anode lines. Thermal: parts that run warm, and their copper.
* Mechanics: the DISP to DRV pin strips line up (coordinates), part heights in the 11 mm gap, mounting holes and keep-outs,
  connectors' reach, the order of soldering (can every joint be reached?).
* Fab: the fab's minimums (`tools/dfm_check.py`, `fab/README.md`), silkscreen (polarity, pin 1, values, test points, HV warning),
  and **whether the fab zips in `fab/` match the board files in the repo today**. Is fascia R rev B's KiCad source in the repo at all?
* Reconsider at least: the zero-via rule, two layers with no plane, the board split and the pin-strip stack, test points.
* Leave route-level statistics and the routing method to audit-routing.

## Run audit-evidence -> `PCB/audit/TS06-audit-evidence.md` (what is proven, and how)
* List the claims the product stands on (it works; it is safe; it fits its case; it can be assembled; the order files are right;
  the firmware matches). For each: what evidence exists, of which kind (a script check, a calculation, a drawing, a bench
  measurement, nothing), and how strong it is.
* `tools/verify_pair.sh`: list every check, what it proves, and what it cannot prove (for example: does any check know which nets
  are 185 V? does any check run KiCad's own DRC with real rules?). What do the 5 skipped checks cover?
* Sample 12 claims with numbers from the docs (pair review, testing plan, routing plan, the grills, the page) and re-derive each
  from the files. Report: holds, does not hold, cannot be checked.
* Stale and missing: docs or the page that no longer match the repo; sources that live only outside the repo; whether the fab
  zips can be regenerated the same from the repo (`tools/mkfab.sh`); whether anything in the build depends on files that are gone.
* What has been physically measured by the owner (`TERMINAL-06-measurements-*.md`, `qa/`) against what is only assumed. List the
  assumptions that would be cheapest to turn into measurements.
* End with an **evidence ladder**: the bench tests and coupons to do before any board order, in order, each with its pass mark.
* Reconsider at least: script-generated boards with custom checkers against a normal KiCad project with a rules file and ERC/DRC.

## Run audit-routing -> `PCB/audit/TS06-audit-routing.md` (the routing methods, and their results)
* The method: how tracks came to be (`tools/mkpcb_drv.py`, `mkpcb_disp.py`, the routes JSON, `astar.c`, hand-typed coordinates, the
  alt-layout comparison in `Claude outputs/TS06-routing-study.md` and `routing-baseline/`). Can it be repeated? Can a person review
  it? What errors can it make that nothing catches?
* The results, measured per board: tracks, total length, vias, layers used, widths in use, the spread of clearances (how many
  places sit within 10 % of the minimum), unconnected or dangling copper, acute corners, neck-downs, long parallel runs of HV
  next to signal, the loop area of the boost and of the multiplexed anode and cathode lines.
* Compare with what a normal flow would give: KiCad's interactive router with net classes, or an autorouter. Say where the
  scripted result is better, and where it is worse.
* Fit for rev C: `PCB/TS06-routing-plan.md` adds about 21 light channels, FETs and a new connector, and was not routed. Can this
  method deliver rev C? Name the method you would use for rev C, the rules it needs on day one (net classes, HV clearance and
  creepage, widths), and the checks to add.
* Leave placement, mechanics and fab questions to audit-layout.
