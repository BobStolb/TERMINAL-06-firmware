# TERMINAL-06 — six-digit nixie clock, production spec (Rev D.4)
 
Ten AlexGyver NixieClock v2 boards → a six-digit instrument: 4× ИН-12А (HH:MM),
2× ИН-17 (SS), one two-lamp ИНС-1 colon, one ИН-15Б/ИН-15А pair that shows AM/PM
and idle-cycles through its full symbol set, a fixed amber LED "m" beneath it,
six-position rotary selector, two МТ1 levers, two КМД-1 buttons.
 
Deliverables: `terminal-06.html` (dossier), `TERMINAL-06-parts-list.xlsx` (sourced
BOM, live formulas), firmware source at
[github.com/BobStolb/TERMINAL-06-firmware](https://github.com/BobStolb/TERMINAL-06-firmware)
(**display layer only — see §5**).
 
**Rev C changes:** control scheme replaced entirely (rotary + 2 levers + 2 buttons,
all software-read); one colon instead of two; input architecture moved to A6/A7
resistor ladders; no hardware power or backlight cut; costing refreshed.
 
---
 
## Rev D.4 (09.09.26) — a second build of the fascia, and J1's pin order becomes a specification
 
**1. TS06-FASCIA-THT — the through-hole build, fully routed.** The same board: same
outline, same artwork, same netlist, same connector. It is built with through-hole parts
instead of surface-mount ones, and it is routed — 34 tracks, no vias. The two builds
answer one question differently: *where is the solder?*
 
- **TS06-FASCIA** (surface-mount) hides every joint on the back face. The customer sees
  black mask, white silkscreen and the decorative gold, and no solder anywhere.
- **TS06-FASCIA-THT** puts the **A6 divider on the face**: five axial resistors sit above
  the rotary's seven landing holes, and the copper between them is left **bare** — mask
  opened, ENIG gold, a solder fillet at every joint. The divider reads as a circuit
  rather than as decoration. Every other part stays behind the panel **with its front
  mask closed**, so those holes show a bare drilled hole and no gold ring.
They are **alternatives, not a pair.** One of them gets fabricated. Nothing else in the
product changes either way — same cable, same firmware, same enclosure.
 
**2. Two rules the through-hole build made explicit.** Both are now written into the
generator, and both constrain any future revision of this board:
 
- **A plated through-hole is copper on BOTH faces.** A part mounted behind the panel
  still has to clear the decorative gold on the front. This is the same class of fault
  Rev D.3's KiCad work caught, arriving from the other side.
- **The panel parts have bodies behind the board.** Nothing can sit inside those
  envelopes, which puts every back-mounted passive in the strip below y 38.5 — the only
  band on this board clear of all five control bodies.
**3. J1's pin order is fixed at 1 +5V, 2 GND, 3 A6, 4 A7, 5 D7, 6 D8, on both builds.**
It used to be whatever the surface-mount routing happened to prefer (D8, D7, GND, A7,
+5V, A6), which was an artefact of one layout. The through-hole board cannot route to
that order, and **two builds of one product must not need two different cables**, so the
pin order stopped being a layout convenience and became a specification. The legend is
printed on the back silkscreen of both boards.
 
**4. CORRECTED — the panel connector is JST-PH (2.0 mm), not JST-XH (2.5 mm).** The
footprint, the parts already being ordered (`S6B-PH-SM4-TB` at the board, `PHR-6`
housings and `SPH-002T-P0.5` contacts at the cable) and the physical part have been PH
since the connector was chosen. Only this document and the schematic symbol still said
XH. Both are corrected. **XS1–XS4 on TS06-SEC are unaffected** — those are separate
connectors and stay as §4 specifies them.
 
**5. The board files are checked by five tools before KiCad ever opens them** —
placement, copper clearance, per-net connectivity, ground-pour fill, and, new this
revision, **schematic-against-board**. That last one was written after the J1
disagreement above, because every other check can pass while the two files disagree
about *what* they are connecting. `PCB/README.md` in the firmware repo says how to run
them; `tools/render.py` draws either board to SVG, front face and back face, so it can
be looked at without opening KiCad.
 
**Nothing in this revision changes a value, a threshold, or a part** beyond the
connector's family name. The netlist, the ladder, the control scheme and the artwork are
exactly as Rev D.3 left them.

## Rev D.3 (08.09.26) — three measurements corrected, control scheme revised, identity moved off the fascia
 
All of this came out of designing **TS06-FASCIA** in KiCad. Four changes, two of them
reversals of things this document has been asserting confidently.
 
**1. The parametric FreeCAD models in `3d/` are now the authoritative dimension
source.** The owner built them and re-calipered every dimension after the first
measuring pass used bad technique. Where a model and an older hand reading disagree,
**the model wins**. Dimensions in this revision were read straight out of the STEP
B-reps rather than transcribed.
 
**2. WITHDRAWN — the rotary body is 25.00 mm, not 26.94 mm.** The 28.08.26 "headline
finding" that the measured body matched neither Ozon listing variant was a technique
error. Re-measured, it is a real **25.00 mm**, which means the listing was right about
that dimension after all. Every downstream statement built on 26.94 mm is corrected in
this revision (§2, §6's panel table, §8). **The lesson the old finding taught is not
withdrawn** — a listing figure still is not a measurement — but the specific reversal is
worth keeping visible: *a bad measurement is not more trustworthy than a datasheet just
because you took it yourself.*
 
**3. CLOSED — the step angle is exactly 30.00°, and six positions span 150°.** Not
measured: **counted**. The detent ring carries **twelve divots**, and a count is an
integer, so 360 ÷ 12 = 30.00° exactly. This also agrees with the twelve outer taps
already documented on the pin ring — the same claim arriving from a second, independent
direction. The earlier ~32°/~160° figure was an eye estimate of a swept arc and is
withdrawn. **The dial artwork can now be drawn from an exact number rather than a good
guess.**
 
**4. Control scheme rev B — two changes to §1**, both applied below.
 
- **INFO and FORMAT/DATE swap places.** §1 already identified positions 1 and 5 as the
  two screens that ignore every input — "safe parking positions where a knock does
  nothing" — but rev A left one of them stranded in the middle of the arc. Putting both
  on **end stops** means the knob cannot be knocked past either end into a live editing
  screen, because past either end there is nothing. Costs one row of the A6 threshold
  table; the tap voltages do not move, only the screen each maps to.
- **Lever B (SUB) gains the glitch rate on position 3.** Rev A read it on one screen out
  of six, which is hard to letter honestly and reads as broken hardware. It now also
  picks Transition ↔ Glitch on Display, **read only while Lever A points at Effects**.
  The point is not the extra feature — it is that SUB now obeys a **rule** rather than an
  exception: *SUB is read only when FIELD is thrown to its second position*, true on both
  screens that use it. A rule is learned once; an exception has to be printed.
**5. The fascia carries no maker's mark and no wordmark.** §6's "hexagon bisected by a
breaking bar, 06 inside" was never a decision the owner made — it entered this project
through an earlier session's document, and nobody could account for where the hexagon
came from. Rather than inherit it, **the face now carries controls and their names and
nothing else**, and identity moves to the enclosure and the packaging where it is not
competing with a dial for the same eye. The colour palette in §6 is unaffected.
 
**6. КМД-1's bushing is independently sourced at last.** `3d/KMD1.step` gives it as
**7.82 mm**, and `3d/MT1.step` gives МТ1 the same 7.82 mm. This closes the
"carried over from МТ1 by eye" gap open since 27.08.26 — the button now has its own
value, and the two agree exactly. Both fascia holes stay 8.0 mm.
 
**7. TS06-TUBE is deferred, not cancelled** — see §3b.

## 1. Control scheme — rev B (Rev D.3, 08.09.26)
 
**The rotary picks the screen.** A lever answers one question and only when that
question is relevant to the current screen — otherwise it is not read. Buttons are
always − / + on whatever the levers point at.
 
| # | Rotary | Lever A — FIELD | Lever B — SUB | Buttons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Normal | not read | not read | not read — display just runs, ИН-15 pair idle-cycles (Rev D, see §3) |
| 2 | Set Time | Hour ↔ Minute | not read | adjusts selected field |
| 3 | Display | Brightness ↔ Effects | Transition ↔ Glitch *(read only when FIELD = Effects)* | adjusts the selected setting; glitch runs **off** at the bottom of its range |
| 4 | Ambient | Colon ↔ Backlight | not read | cycles that item's styles |
| 5 | Format / Date | Format ↔ Date | Day-Month ↔ Year *(read only when FIELD = Date)* | Format: either flips 12h/24h. Date: adjusts SUB's selection |
| 6 | Info | not read | not read | not read — read-only glance screen |
 
**Positions 1 and 6 are both parking screens, and both are now end stops.** Every
input is ignored on either one, on purpose, and because they sit at the two extremes
of the 150° arc the knob cannot be knocked *past* either of them into a screen that
changes a setting — past either end there is nothing. This is the rev B swap: rev A
had INFO at position 5, stranded between two live editing screens.
 
**SUB has exactly one rule, and it holds on both screens that use it:**
 
> **Lever B is read only when Lever A is thrown to its second position.**
 
Effects on Display, Date on Format/Date. No special case, nothing to letter as an
exception. The fascia draws this as a supply rather than a caption — see §6.
 
**Position 5 is the only screen holding two different *kinds* of setting** — a binary
toggle and a multi-field value — which is what originally justified a second lever.
Position 3 now uses the same structure for a different reason: brightness, transition
style and glitch rate are three settings, and two levers plus two buttons address
three settings only if one lever nests under the other.
 
**Rev D addition to position 1:** while parked on Normal, the ИН-15 pair idle-cycles
through some or all of both tubes' 18 glyphs as an ambient animation, in the same
spirit as the existing anti-poisoning sweep and glitch effect — between glimpses it
still shows the correct A or P. Exact cadence and trigger is a firmware decision, not
fixed here — see the AM/PM subsection of §3.
 
**No hardware power switch.** Both levers are software inputs. Defensible (desk
clocks are always-on) but decide it deliberately and say "always-on, no switch" in
the listing.
 
## 2. Input wiring — costs no real GPIO
 
A6/A7 on the Nano's TQFP-32 ATmega328P are ADC inputs with no digital driver and no
pull-up. They can never do anything else, which is exactly why ladders suit them.
 
**A6 — rotary, tapped divider.** Five 4.7 kΩ 1% in series across +5 V, wiper
picks a node. String 23.5 kΩ, 213 µA, worst source Z 5.64 kΩ (ADC wants <10 k).
 
| pos | screen | V | ADC | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Normal | 0.00 | 0 | — |
| 2 | Set Time | 1.00 | 205 | 205 |
| 3 | Display | 2.00 | 409 | 204 |
| 4 | Ambient | 3.00 | 614 | 205 |
| 5 | Format / Date | 4.00 | 818 | 204 |
| 6 | Info | 5.00 | 1023 | 205 |
 
1% parts shift a tap ~2 codes. Hundredfold margin. **The 1% tolerance is not there for
accuracy — the gaps are enormous. It is there so one firmware threshold table fits all
ten units without calibrating each clock by hand.**
 
*(Rev D.3: the screen column reflects the rev B swap. The voltages did not move; only
which screen each tap maps to. Firmware cost is one row of the threshold table.)*
 
**"SR26" is a working name, not a fixed part number**, and as of Rev D.2 not tied to a
specific seller. **Rev D.3 — the part is in hand and fully characterised from the
parametric model in `3d/SR25.FCStd`, every dimension re-calipered:**
 
| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Body / wafer ⌀ | **25.00 mm** *(supersedes the withdrawn 26.94 mm)* |
| Bushing ⌀ | **8.62 mm**, **7.00 mm usable** |
| Shaft ⌀ | **6.00 mm** |
| Outer tap ring | **12 pins on ⌀ 19.99 mm**, at 15° + n×30° |
| Inner wiper commons | **2 pins on ⌀ ~10.90 mm**, at 0° and 180° |
| Lug ⌀ | **1.00 mm**, 10 mm free length |
| **Detents** | **12 counted → step is exactly 30.00°; 6 positions span 150°** |
 
No washer trick is needed — it is a native 6-position part. Only one pole (one common
+ 6 taps) is wired; the second is spare.
**A7 — two МТ1 levers, binary-weighted.** 10 k pull-up; 20 k and 10 k to ground.
States at ADC 1023 / 682 / 512 / 409, worst gap 103 codes. **Both levers switch to
ground, never to the rail** — a closed switch then clamps the pin through a low
resistance, the state most immune to what a metre of cable picks up running past a
converter chopping 185 V at 31 kHz.
 
**Three things the ladders need:**
1. 100 nF from A6 and A7 to ground **at the board end** — holds the last value when
   a non-shorting rotary floats its wiper, and filters what the cable picks up. **At
   the board end specifically:** put them on the fascia and the whole cable still hangs
   off the ADC pin unfiltered. With 5.64 kΩ of source impedance the cap forms a 564 µs
   low-pass — slow against noise, fast against a hand turning a knob.
2. Five consecutive identical readings at ~10 ms before acting. The rotary is the
   master mode selector; one spurious sample must never jump into Set Time.
3. Contact-clean both levers as a documented refurb step regardless of the rotary's
   source — oxidised wipers shift the divider, the failure that appears in month
   three. Worth doing on the rotary too.
**Panel cable — one 6-pin JST-PH (2.0 mm), pin order fixed:**
 
| J1 pin | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net | +5 V | GND | A6 | A7 | D7 (−) | D8 (+) |
 
No power path, no LED rail. Ladder resistors on the fascia at the switches; caps at the
board end. *(Rev D.4: **PH, not XH** — the footprint and the parts on order have always
been PH 2.0 mm; only this document said otherwise. The pin order is now a specification
rather than a routing artefact, and is identical on both builds of the fascia, so one
cable fits either. It is printed on the back silkscreen.)* Board-side part
`S6B-PH-K-S` (through-hole) or `S6B-PH-SM4-TB` (surface-mount); cable side `PHR-6`
housing with `SPH-002T-P0.5` contacts.
 
**Landing points on the main board:** signals to the **S1 / S2 tactile pads**
(D7, D8) with stock buttons unpopulated; +5 V and GND from the **unpopulated RTC
footprint**. Both plated, labelled, identical on every board. Confirm with a meter —
silkscreen is not evidence. **The buttons need no pull-ups on the fascia** — the
ATmega supplies its own and the firmware already enables them.
 
**Full pin map:** D2 seconds-tens anode · D3–D6 HHMM anodes · D7 button − ·
D8 button + · D9 HV oscillator · D10 colon (MPSA42 base) · D11 backlight PWM ·
D12 free · D13 seconds-units anode · A0–A3 К155ИД1 BCD · A4/A5 I²C (DS3231 RTC +
two MCP23017 expanders, Rev D) · A6 rotary · A7 levers · D0/D1 serial.
**D10 and D11 are the board's only fixed jobs outside the digit multiplex.**
 
**No I/O expander for the panel controls.** It would solve a shortage that does
not exist there: five controls cost two real GPIO plus two pins that could never
drive anything else. Six resistors and two caps, ~30 ₽, still beats putting the
panel behind an I²C transaction. **This verdict is specifically about reading the
rotary and levers — it does not apply to switching HV cathodes, which is a
genuinely different problem and exactly why Rev D reaches for an expander there
(see §3).**
 
## 3. Display and colon
 
**One colon, between HH and MM only.** No MM:SS separator — the ИН-12 envelope is
~22 × 24 mm against the ИН-17's 15 × 20 mm, so the size step already states the
group break. It also lands on the main board's existing centre indicator pad.
 
**Each lamp gets its own 220 kΩ ballast. Never shared.** A neon has negative
resistance once struck: the first lamp to fire drags the shared node to its
sustaining voltage, below the other's striking voltage, and the second dot never
lights.
 
```
185 V ──┬── 220k ── ИНС-1 upper ──┐
        └── 220k ── ИНС-1 lower ──┤
                                   ├── MPSA42 collector
D10 ── 10k ── MPSA42 base          │
              MPSA42 emitter ──────┴── GND
```
 
Low-side switch, not an opto: grounds are common so isolation buys nothing, and a
TLP627 Darlington's turn-off would smear D10's 31 kHz PWM fade. **300 V rating is
mandatory** — with it off the full rail stands across it. 2N5551 (160 V) works on
the bench and fails in a customer's house.
 
Select the two lamps as a **matched pair** on a bench rig. A brightness mismatch
between the two dots of one colon is the most visible defect on the product.
 
Backlight: six amber 595 nm LEDs on D11 PWM, diffused, behind 2 mm printed shield
rings. Amber only — blue/white fights the tube glow.
 
### AM/PM and idle symbol cycling (Rev C.3, expanded Rev D)
 
Two symbol tubes, same size class and socket family as the ИН-12А, mounted on
TS06-SEC alongside the seconds tubes. Verified against rudatasheet.ru, eandc.ru and
two tube-collector references: ИН-15А and ИН-15Б do **not** display the words
"AM"/"PM" — they're Soviet multimeter symbol tubes, one glyph lit at a time.
 
- **ИН-15А** — μ, −, +, %, n, m, M, K, Р, П (metric prefixes, 10 glyphs)
- **ИН-15Б** — A, W, F, Hz, H, V, S, Ω (electrical units, 8 glyphs)
The **'A'** (Ampere) on ИН-15Б means AM, the **'Р'** (reads as Latin P) on ИН-15А
means PM. **Rev D wires every cathode on both tubes** — all 18 — because the brief now
includes an idle-time animation that cycles through the whole symbol set.
 
**Why this needed a redesign, not just more wire.** A single Nano pin can only ever
select between 2 states without help. Eighteen independently selectable cathodes need
eighteen independently selectable control lines, and there were zero free Nano pins
left after Rev C.3.
 
**Circuit — one low-side switch per cathode, driven from two MCP23017 I²C GPIO
expanders instead of Nano pins directly.**
 
```
                    +--- A0/A1/A2 -> GND          (U3, addr 0x20)
Nano A4/A5 (I2C) ---+
                    +--- A0 -> +5V, A1/A2 -> GND  (U4, addr 0x21)
 
U3 GPA0..GPA7        -> 8x  [10k base R -> MPSA42 -> ИН-15Б cathode]  (VT2-VT9)
U4 GPA0..GPA7+GPB0-1 -> 10x [10k base R -> MPSA42 -> ИН-15А cathode]  (VT10-VT19)
```
 
Each MPSA42 is the same 300 V low-side switch used everywhere else — off-state sees
close to the full 185 V rail, so the 300 V rating is still mandatory, still no
substituting 2N5551.
 
**Why two chips, and why that's still free on the Nano.** ИН-15Б's 8 cathodes plus
ИН-15А's 10 don't fit in one MCP23017's 16 lines, so it's one chip per tube. Neither
collides with the DS3231's fixed address (0x68). Both ride the SAME two wires — SDA
and SCL, already brought out for the RTC — so going from 2 switched cathodes to 18
cost **zero** additional Nano pins.
 
**The anode resistor is still one per tube, not one per cathode.** R38/R39 (8.2 kΩ)
limit the shared anode current regardless of which single cathode is grounded — only
one cathode per tube is ever lit at once.
 
**Placement — trailing, after the seconds tubes:** `[HH]:[MM]  [SS]  [A/P]` reads in
AM/PM order for the American buyers this feature targets. Give the pair its own gap
and a slightly recessed mount.
 
**Still open:**
1. **ИН-15Б** — a handful secured for one prototype unit; the *ten-unit-batch depth*
   question is a separate, later sourcing task, tracked in §8. ИН-15А is sourced
   (Begrant 35 ₽ ×283, yg-radio 30 ₽ ×25).
2. **Does idle cycling ever compromise AM/PM legibility?** Two honest paths, both
   firmware decisions: (a) cycle only after N seconds of no input, snapping back to
   the correct glyph at the first sign of activity, or (b) accept the trade, since the
   real time-of-day reference is the digit tubes.
3. Two MCP23017s per unit are a real BOM addition (~440 ₽/unit before spares). See §7.
4. XS3 grows from 7 pins to 8: AMPM_SEL retired, replaced with SDA + SCL. This also
   resolves the long-standing XS3-vs-`terminal-06.html` "J3" pin-count discrepancy.
5. Brightness/dimming parity at night is still unsolved for both AM/PM and the
   idle-cycling glyphs.
6. If using an MCP23017 already in hand rather than fresh stock, check it against a
   known-good part first — counterfeit/relabelled expander ICs are a known problem on
   grey-market listings, and this is going into ten units.
### "m" indicator (Rev C.4, lamp swapped Rev D)
 
The AM/PM pair reads as "A" or "P" alone, which is legible but terser than "AM"/"PM".
Rev C.4 added a fixed "m" glyph beneath the pair using a printed stencil. **Rev D
reconsiders the lamp, not the idea.** The stencil carries the letterform entirely, so
the lamp never needed to be a neon on the 185 V rail: **a plain 5 mm amber LED does
the same job for less money, less routing, and zero HV exposure.**
 
- Drops ИНС-1 count back to **2 per unit** (colon only).
- Removes the 220 kΩ HV ballast; replaces it with an ordinary 220 Ω 0.25 W at 5 V.
- Removes the lamp from the HV rail altogether.
- Keeps the same amber colour as the backlight LEDs rather than the neon's orange-red,
  so it reads as one light family with the backlight — a genuine, minor aesthetic trade.
**Wiring.** HL3 and R26 (220 Ω) in series across +5 V and GND, **specified as always
on whenever the clock is powered**. **Rev D.2 flags this as disputed** — see the header
note. If the 12h-mode-conditional answer wins instead, HL3 moves onto a transistor
gated by a diode-OR of whichever two expander lines mean "showing A" and "showing P".
 
**Mounting.** A small printed stencil (PETG) in front of the lamp, recessed under or
beside the A/P pair.

## 4. Seconds and colon module (TS06-SEC)
 
46 × 34 mm, 1.6 mm, matte black. Mount at the same 14.5 mm standoff as the tube
board so both tube planes are level. This board carries the AM/PM pair (V9
ИН-15Б, V10 ИН-15А), both MCP23017 GPIO expanders (U3, U4) and their eighteen
switching transistors, the colon driver, and the "m" indicator LED (HL3).
 
**It also carries the two TLP627 optocouplers for the ИН-17 anodes (D2, D13)** — the
main board has only four opto channels and they serve the ИН-12s. The ИН-15 pair has
**no** optocouplers: it is not in the multiplex at all, and is cathode-switched by the
expanders against a shared anode resistor per tube.
 
Anode resistor at 185 V, 1/6 duty: **12 kΩ start** (6.6 mA peak / 1.10 mA avg);
15 k soft, 10 k bright, 8.2 k floor. 0.5 W for voltage rating, not power. The stock
board has **no** per-tube anode resistor — fine with four matched ИН-12s, fatal
with an ИН-17 (sustains ~105 V vs ~140 V and hogs current).
 
**1 MΩ 0.5 W anode bleed per ИН-17** for ghost suppression, retained on its original
merit. **Do not confuse this with the 12 kΩ-class anode *series* resistor above** —
different part, different job; the series resistor limits current and is not a
ghosting fix.
 
*(Rev D.3 note: the 01.09.26 caveat that the main board's ИН-12 anode drivers might
need the same class of fix was **withdrawn 06.09.26** — see §5a-pre. The bleed
footprints elsewhere drop to DNP insurance.)*
 
Connectors: XS1 2-pin JST-VH (HV) · XS2 10-way IDC (cathodes) · XS3 **8-pin**
JST-XH (GND, +5V, BACKL, DOT, SEC_A0, SEC_A1, SDA, SCL) · XS4 2-pin JST-XH (colon
leads). Three keyed families so 5 V cannot be plugged into 185 V at 11 pm.
*(Rev D.4: these four are unaffected by the fascia connector's correction to PH —
XS1–XS4 are on the SEC module and stay as written.)*
 
**Cathode routing:** ИН-17 cathode for digit N → the same К155ИД1 output the ИН-12s
use for digit N, so one `digitMask` covers all six tubes. Measure the digit→pin
table once with `secCathodeMap`. Never from a datasheet — surplus markings lie.
 
## 5a-pre. GHOSTING ROOT CAUSE — empty multiplex slots (06.09.26)
 
The ИН-12 ghosting that blocked P2 was **not** an anode-driver fault. The ISR writes a
digit to the shared cathode bus inside the same guard that turns a slot's anode on;
with `NUM_INDI 6` but no SEC module fitted, slots 4 and 5 put a digit on the bus with
**no anode driven anywhere on the board**, and the real tubes' decaying anodes light
it. Stock AlexGyver firmware cannot create this — it rotates through exactly as many
slots as it has tubes.
 
**Consequences:**
 
- **§4's caveat about the main board's anode drivers is withdrawn.** The ИН-12 anode
  drivers are fine. The **1 MΩ bleed remains specified for the ИН-17s** on its original
  merit; the four ИН-12 bleeds are **not required**.
- **TS06-TUBE loses its bleed-footprint justification entirely** (§3b).
- **The anode-driver study's hardware options (MPSA92 restage, HV shift registers) are
  not needed for this product.** They remain the right direction for a future design
  that wants high refresh with certainty.
**Proper firmware fix, better than the `MUX_SLOTS` hack:** the ISR should never leave a
digit on the cathode bus without a driven anode. When the changeover guard fails it
should write a blanking code (К155ИД1 inputs 10–15 select no cathode). With that, the
rotation is safe at any `NUM_INDI` regardless of which slots are populated.
 
## 5a. Display timing
 
**The camera-banding requirement is dropped by owner decision.** Visible ghosting is a
product defect; 60 fps video flicker is not.
 
**Shipped display timing:** Timer2 prescaler **8** (AlexGyver's original), 26-tick slot,
**`INDI_BRIGHT 21`**, `NUM_INDI 6`. Gives 640 µs of dead time, 50 Hz frame, 13.5 %
per-tube duty — measured clean, with unlit cathodes fully dark. Stock's `INDI_BRIGHT 24`
is **not** sufficient at six tubes; three brightness steps is the entire cost of six
pristine tubes. Prescaler 8 also drops ISR load from a measured 33.5 % CPU to roughly
4 %, which is headroom the MCP23017 driver at P6a will want.
 
**Worth re-opening once the SEC module is fitted:** if empty slots were the dominant
ghost source all along, a much higher refresh may be clean with all six tubes present.
Not a promise — re-run the sweep before accepting the 50 Hz frame as final.
 
## 5. Firmware status — control scheme NOT implemented
 
The firmware source is the **display-layer port only**: 4→6 tubes, ISR rewrite,
anti-poisoning and glitch extension, and the superseded rev B control scheme
(2 buttons + 1 lever). Flashing it gives a working six-digit clock with the *old*
controls.
 
**"Six tubes" here means only the anode-multiplexed digit tubes (4× ИН-12А +
2× ИН-17), not the finished instrument's full glass count.** The colon (2× ИНС-1) and

Done and verified:

- Buzzer removed (`tone()` and the multiplex share `__vector_7`)
- Anti-poisoning and glitch across six tubes; seconds excluded from effects
- **BOARD_TYPE corrected, 01.09.26** — the shipped `#define BOARD_TYPE 1` was wrong

**Correction (28.08.26): `DOT_BRIGHT` was never actually retuned.** Direct inspection

Still to write: A6 mode state machine with the agreement filter; A7 lever decode;

- **The A6 threshold table maps position 5 → Format/Date and position 6 → Info**

## 6. Enclosure
 
176 × 96 × 78 mm wedge, front raked 12°, top deck overhanging a recessed tube
trench. Printed: chassis (matte black PETG, 0.20/4 walls/20% gyroid, no supports),
brow (safety orange, 0.16), base plate, colon holder (press-fit sockets for the two
ИНС-1s with a lead channel), and the "m" stencil. One filament batch for all ten units.
 
### Brow reveal — why the case "hides the tubes"
 
**The brow was sized to clear the digit, not the envelope.** `TERMINAL-06-concept-plates.md`
set the brow geometry (y 9.5–22) so that a 10° viewing angle still clears the **full digit**
— assumed 18 mm, since measured at **18.63 mm**. But the ИН-12 envelope is **28.70 mm** on
its wide axis. **So roughly 10 mm of glass — the top dome, the getter flash, the shoulder —
sits above the feature the clearance criterion was ever written against, and the overhanging
top deck takes exactly that band.**
 
That is the whole of the "case hides the tubes too much" complaint, stated geometrically:
the trench does not hide the *numbers*, it hides the *glass*.
 
**Three levers on the tube deck:** raise it; move it forward toward the 12° raked front;
or rake the tube deck itself. The third is the highest-value — the ИН-12 is an end-view
tube, so tilting it toward the viewer improves digit legibility *and* presents more
envelope profile at once.
 
**All three are constrained by the inherited board's tube pitch (23.36 / 27.42 / 23.36 mm)
and mounting.** Whether that constraint is worth 1 425 ₽/unit to remove is now a **case**
decision, settled by a printed brow test-fit — see §3b.
 
**Sequencing note:** none of this can be finalised before the socket capture sheet exists
(`TS06-LIB-SOCKET`) — seat height above the PCB, the tip-off pip's projection, and the
front-glass-to-digit-plane parallax depth under the 12° rake are all still uncaptured.
 
### TS06-FASCIA — 176 × 52 mm, 2.0 mm FR4, matte black mask, white silk, ENIG
 
Recessed into the chassis so bushing torque lands on plastic.
 
**Controls need no additional board — this one is it.** The rotary, both МТ1 levers and
both КМД1 buttons are **panel-mount hardware with solder lugs, not PCB-mount parts.**
**Rev D.3 confirms this geometrically from the models:** on every one of them the lugs
sit *behind* the body, which itself sits behind the panel. On the rotary that is
**≥11.3 mm behind the fascia's rear face, pointing away.** Nothing can be board-mounted;
every control is hand-wired to landing pads.
 
TS06-FASCIA earns its place for three reasons: it is the **printed product face**; it is
**structural**; and it **anchors the ladder** at defined pads rather than in free air,
where the spec already fears drift by month three. Panel-to-board is **one 6-pin JST-PH**
(§2 has the pin order).
 
Layout left→right = **pick the screen, pick the field, adjust the value**: rotary left
(six labelled positions arced around the shaft), levers centre, buttons right.
 
**Lettering, Rev D.3.** Rotary positions are lettered with their six fixed names:
`NORMAL · SET TIME · DISPLAY · AMBIENT · FORMAT/DATE · INFO`, on a **150° fan at exactly
30.00° per step**. The levers are lettered **FIELD** and **SUB** — their invariant role,
true on all six screens — because their per-screen meanings change and cannot be printed
honestly. Buttons are **−** and **+**.
 
**§1's SUB rule is drawn, not captioned.** A gold ENIG trace ends directly beneath FIELD,
at the point its second throw reaches, and runs from there into a box enclosing SUB; two
more leave that box for positions 3 and 5. Nothing reaches SUB except through that point.
The real lever closes the gap, so the rule is stated as geometry and completed by
hardware. Nothing is drawn in the space the lever physically occupies.
 
**Bushing stack — open.** The rotary bushing is 7.00 mm usable and the fascia is 2.0 mm
recessed into the chassis, so nut, washer and recess depth must all fit in the remaining
**5.00 mm**. A depth question against the printed chassis, not a diameter question.
 
**Worth the PCB?** Not as a wiring loom — solder lugs, not PCB pins, six conductors total.
**The rotary settles it:** six labelled positions is the most information-dense thing on
the product and exactly what FDM cannot print. Without a dial the rotary is a mystery
knob. ~1 050 ₽/unit = 3.9% of retail for the most-photographed surface.
 
### Two builds of the fascia — pick one (Rev D.4)
 
Same outline, same artwork, same netlist, same connector, same firmware. They differ in
one decision: **where the solder is.**
 
| | TS06-FASCIA | TS06-FASCIA-THT |
|---|---|---|
| Parts | surface-mount, all on the back face | through-hole |
| The A6 divider | behind the panel, invisible | **on the face**, five axial resistors above the rotary's seven landing holes |
| Copper between them | under mask | **bare** — mask opened, ENIG gold, a solder fillet at every joint |
| Everything else | behind the panel | behind the panel, **front mask closed** — a bare drilled hole, no gold ring |
| Routing | shelved (placement and artwork only) | **routed: 34 tracks, no vias.** Ladder on F.Cu under opened mask; six signals on B.Cu under mask; GND is a pour over the whole back face |
 
**The through-hole build is not the cheap option, it is the other aesthetic.** The
surface-mount board says *sealed instrument*; the through-hole board says *this is the
divider that reads the knob, and here is the solder*. Both are honest; they photograph
differently, and #2 in the listing is the dead-on front shot.
 
**Two rules the through-hole build makes explicit, true of any future revision:**
 
1. **A plated through-hole is copper on both faces.** A part mounted behind the panel
   still has to clear the decorative gold in front of it.
2. **The panel parts have bodies behind the board.** Nothing sits inside those
   envelopes, which confines every back-mounted passive to the strip below y 38.5 — the
   only band on this board clear of all five.
Board files, generators and the five checkers live in the firmware repo under `PCB/` and
`tools/`; `PCB/README.md` explains them. `tools/render.py` draws either board to SVG,
front face and back face.

**Every ZS-042 DS3231 module needs a fix:** it has a charging circuit for a
rechargeable LIR2032. Fit a plain CR2032 with that intact and you trickle-charge a
non-rechargeable cell inside a sealed product. Remove the charging resistor or its
diode. Revisions differ — check, don't assume. The same "check, don't assume" applies
to the ZS-042's SDA/SCL pull-ups before adding a second set for the MCP23017s.
