PLATE 00

What the answer killed, and what it freed

Corrections

Three things in Rev 1 were wrong, and only one of them was the obvious one. Getting the viewing axis back did not just close the wrist question — it invalidated an entire section of assembly reasoning that had been written for a tube lying on its side.

Rev 1 saidTrueConsequence
Side-view is "the version that works" — tube lies flat, 14 mm sets case thickness End-view only. You look down the long axis at the ~20 mm face; the barrel runs back into the case. Case thickness would be ~30 mm on a wrist. The watch is dead on ИН-17, exactly as Rev 1's branch B predicted.
"Rotate the rear pair 180° so its leads point away" There is no rear pair. In a 2×2 of end-view tubes all four face the viewer and all 48 leads leave straight backwards into one board. The whole §3 lead problem was written for a layout that does not exist. No riser board, no flex, no lateral dressing.
≈60–75 min per unit in tube leads alone; "budget 2.5–3 h" Four tubes into one flat board with no interference. Call it 25–35 min until a jig exists. The labour argument that forced a $700+ price evaporates. This can be a $180 object.
The rule survives, restated correctly Rev 1 proposed: for any wearable, the tube's shortest dimension is the case thickness. That is still right — it just needs the half nobody wrote down: you do not get to choose which dimension faces you. The tube's viewing axis is fixed by how its cathodes are stacked, and case thickness is whatever lies behind that face. Ask which face you look at before asking how thick anything is.

The object

Hero · 33° yaw, 13° elevation

Four glass barrels pointing at you in a square, in a black block you could close a hand around. The thing that made ИН-17 wrong for a wrist — you can only look into one end — is the thing that makes this read as an instrument rather than a clock face: four little windows, each with a number burning at the bottom of it.

Always on changes one thing that matters A watch lights its tubes about two minutes a day, so Rev 1 could call poisoning a low risk. A desk object runs them continuously, which puts this product back under exactly the same anti-poisoning discipline as TERMINAL-06 — a full cathode sweep on a timer, not a decorative slot-machine at each glance. The animation catalogue already has the constants: every 15 minutes, 0→9 three times, 10 ms a step.

PLATE 01A

Four faces in a dark room

night · 3/4 front

The desk miniature makes sense at night in a way the daylight drawing cannot show. All four ИН-17 are end-view — you read them straight on, which is the constraint that killed the wristwatch and produced this object instead — so the entire display is one flat plane of glass 46 × 42 mm behind a single recessed window, and the case around it has nothing to do but disappear.

What is interpretation here The 2 × 2 arrangement and the 20 × 15 mm visible face are taken from the measured ИН-17 envelope; the window reveal and the bezel glow are design intent. The colon is drawn as two small neons on the centreline — that pair is specified but not yet bench-matched.

The design rule this settles

An end-view tube gets a window, not a plinth

TERMINAL-06 and MIMI-06 put their tubes proud on an inset deck because an ИН-12 is a side-view tube — there is a whole glass envelope to show, and hiding it in a trench wasted the product. An ИН-17 seen end-on has nothing to show but its face. Standing it proud would display a glass barrel and a set of leads. So it gets a recessed square window with a 2 mm reveal, and the open-deck rule stays where it belongs.

What it does not need

No sapphire, no Qi, no interlock

The wrist version needed a flat rectangular sapphire (possibly the most expensive part in it), a charging scheme, a battery rated ≥2C, a hard-gated converter and a back-off HV interlock against skin contact. A mains-powered desk object needs none of that. What survives is the 170 V discipline the shop already practises on every other product.

PLATE 03

Drive, and why it got easier

40 cathodes

Four tubes × ten cathodes is still forty lines, and static drive is still the right answer — but the reason has changed. On a battery it was about the 4 mA overcurrent limit against a 25% duty cycle. On mains it is simply that static is what a permanently-lit display should be: no multiplex, no refresh rate, and therefore none of P2's ghosting problem, which is a multiplex artefact and cannot occur here.

Matching is the cost that did not go away All four tubes are lit simultaneously and permanently, so brightness mismatch is the first thing the eye finds — and unlike a watch that shows them for two seconds, this one shows them for a year. Expect to bin NOS ИН-17 into matched quads at 60–70% yield, ~6 tubes bought per 4 shipped. At 150 ₽ that is ~900 ₽ of glass per unit including losses, which is nothing. Depth is the question for s_svshkin and Avito Seller B, not price.

PLATE 04

The wrist, on the tube that actually works

Sibling concept

The thesis was never about ИН-17. It was that every nixie watch on sale reads the time sequentially — press, wait, hours, wait, minutes — because two tubes is all that fits, and that this is the most-complained-about thing in the category. That argument survives the tube being wrong. It just needs a tube that is right, and the market has already proved which one.

Why ИН-16 and not a guess Both competitors put ИН-16 on a wrist and ship it. That is not a datasheet claim or a listing photo — it is two products in the market proving the tube's viewing axis and its shortest dimension both work in a ~17 mm case. And the shop already holds the supply: Begrant, 250 ₽, 500 in stock — the deepest listing of any product tube on the whole board, eighty-three six-digit clocks' worth, and it is EMBER-06's tube so it is already a line the shop restocks.
What is still unmeasured, and it is the same mistake if skipped We have never calipered an ИН-16. The 10.5 mm figure quoted in Rev 1 came from reasoning about why NIWA works, not from a tube on a bench — which is precisely the class of assumption that just cost this concept a revision. Buy six, measure all three axes, and confirm which face the digits are viewed through before drawing a case. Same for ИН-19 (40 ₽, 101 in stock), which would make the watch smaller again if its short axis comes in under ~11 mm.
Sequencing Build the desk miniature first. It uses tubes already in hand (ten ИН-17 bought from yg-radio at 870 ₽), it has no unmeasured part in it beyond the ИН-17 axes, and it proves the 2×2 layout, the static driver and the matched-quad binning process — all three of which the wrist version needs and none of which are cheaper to learn on a $700 object with a sapphire in it.

PLATE 06

Open, in order

Rev 2
  1. Three caliper readings on one ИН-17, all three orthogonal axes, plus the 19.30 / 17.50 pair re-taken on two more samples. The viewing axis is settled; the numbers behind it are not, and every dimension on plates 01–02 rests on them.
  2. Lead exit geometry — circle, comb, or two rows, at what pitch. Now a board-layout question rather than a packaging crisis, but it still sets the footprint.
  3. Bin six ИН-17 by eye at 1.5 mA and find out whether 60–70% matched-quad yield is pessimistic or optimistic. Ten tubes are already on the bench; this costs an evening.
  4. Depth at s_svshkin and Avito Seller B. Twelve and eighteen tubes is two or three units after binning, not a run.
  5. Caliper six ИН-16 for QUADRANT-W, and confirm the viewing face — do not repeat the mistake this revision exists to correct.
  6. Decide the sixth glyph position. Four digits shows HH:MM only. Seconds would need two more tubes and a 3×2, which stops being a square and stops being the idea. Recommendation: leave it at four — the squareness is the product.
