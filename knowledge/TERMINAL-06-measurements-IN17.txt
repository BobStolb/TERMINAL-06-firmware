## ✅ Row 13, CLOSED in Rev 5: the envelope is FLATTENED
 
A factory outline drawing surfaced on 9 Sep 2026 — four views: cathode order, base lead
circle, side elevation, end elevation. It settles the question Rev 2 raised and Rev 4 could
not answer from vendor listings.
 
**The envelope is a round Ø20 stem that flattens toward the display end.** It is not a
cylinder and it is not a flat rectangular tube. The three unequal numbers the vendors quote
are all real:
 
| Axis | Value | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Along the axis | **22 mm** | glass only, base to dome. Leads are a further 35 |
| Across, major | **20 mm** | the stem. `Ø20` on the drawing |
| Across, minor | **14 mm** | the face, at the flattened display end |
 
End-view, confirmed in Rev 3, is unchanged. What is new is that the face you look into is a
**narrow lens, 14 across**, while the stem behind it is the full Ø20.
 
### Rows 1-2 re-labelled, not deleted
 
Rev 2 guessed that the two readings might be the major axis and an off-axis chord of one
oval section. **That guess was right.**
 
- Row 1, **19.30 mm** — the major axis. Reads 0.7 low against the drawing's Ø20, which is
  ordinary tolerance on a hand-worked envelope.
- Row 2, **17.50 mm** — **not an axis at all.** An intermediate chord, taken part way down
  the flatten where the section is neither 20 nor 14. It is a real reading of a real place on
  the glass, and it is not a dimension of anything.
 
**Neither reading is the face.** The face was never calipered — the photo meant to show it had
the jaws off the glass. That is why row 2 fitted nothing.
 
### What it cost, and the rule that follows
 
For two days TERMINAL-06 was drawn with the ИН-17 face at **17.58 × 19.20, portrait** — both
numbers taken from rows 1-2 and used as though they were the width and height of one
rectangle. They are two chords of a curved surface. The row closed at 208 instead of 204.3.
 
> **Rule, added to this sheet:** two caliper readings on a curved body are two chords until
> proved otherwise. Do not pair them into a width × height. Row 13's orthogonal-pair
> procedure exists precisely to prove otherwise, and it was skipped.

## Rev 5: what this changed downstream, same day
 
`claude/TERMINAL-06-concept-plates.md`, Rev F:
 
- ИН-17 face **14 × 20**, was 17.58 × 19.20. Digit **9.00** against ИН-12's 18.63, so the
  seconds read at **48%** the height of the hours on **half** the glass area. That is now a

This is the second time this sheet has moved TERMINAL-06's overall width. It is the sheet
that most needs the caliper pass.

## ✅ Gating question, CLOSED in Rev 3: end-view
 
Rev 2 asked which axis the digits are viewed down, and said the answer decided whether the
QUADRANT wristwatch was a ~17 mm product or a ~30 mm brick.
 
> **Answered 2 Sep 2026, by looking at the tube: end-view.** You can only look into one end, and
> it is the ~20 mm face. The long axis runs back into the case.
 
Two things follow, and Rev 2 got one of them wrong:
 
1. **The ИН-17 wristwatch is withdrawn.** Rev 2's branch B, as expected — 22 mm of tube
   perpendicular to a watch face is a ~30 mm case.
2. **Rev 2 said "QUADRANT dies". It did not.** The concept moved to a **desk miniature**
   (`claude/QUADRANT-D-spec.md`), where end-view is not a defect but the whole idea: four barrels
   pointing at you, each a little window with a number burning at the bottom of it. **Do not read
   Rev 2's banner as a live warning — it is history.**
**Row 9 is re-datumed, not deleted.** Rev 2 said the parallax number was "only meaningful if the
gating question resolves to side-view". That was wrong. For an end-view tube it is still the number
that matters — it is just measured **from the viewed end face to the digit plane**, not from a flat
side. It sets how deep the digit sits inside QUADRANT-D's recessed window and what the collar has
to clear.

## ⚠ Rev 4: the lead geometry is not on the internet. Stop looking for it.
 
Rows 5–7 — lead pitch, lead diameter, free length, exit pattern — block `claude/QUADRANT-D-spec.md`
(§11.1) and therefore its PCB order. **They were searched for properly and they do not exist in
public.** Sources checked, all of which give electricals and none of which give lead geometry:
 
| Source | What it has | Lead geometry? |
|---|---|---|
| Dieter's archive, `IN-17_01/02/03.pdf` (original ТУ scans + translation) | full electrical table, 12-pin assignment, handling limits | **no drawing** |
| rudatasheet.ru | mass 7 g, digit 9 mm, "glass, miniature", full pin table | no |
| tec.org.ru | pulsed current and скважность, «бесцокольное с гибкими выводами» | no |
| eandc.ru | ТУ electricals, mass, 12 leads | no |
| swissnixie.com | "about 15 × 20 mm", 9 mm digit, 39 kΩ suggested | no |
| soviet-tubes.com | 22 × 20 × 14 mm, 9 × 5.5 mm digit | envelope only |
| neonixie.com | "one of the smallest ever made", ~8 mm digit, sold with a plastic base | no |
| A commercial ИН-17 clock kit's own assembly manual | trim leads to 15–20 mm, tack two opposite leads first | **no spacing given** |
 
**Conclusion to act on:** the caliper pass is not a shortcut being skipped, it is the only path to
these numbers. **Twenty minutes with the tube beats another hour of searching.**
 
**The bound the sweep does give, which is enough to keep drawing against.** Twelve leads inside a
~14–17.5 mm minor envelope put lead-to-lead spacing at roughly **2.5–3 mm** on any plausible
pattern — an ~11 mm circle gives 2.9 mm, two rows of six give ~2.5 mm. So one tube's lead cluster is
**≈11–13 mm across**. At QUADRANT-D's 26 mm horizontal pitch that leaves a **~14 mm channel between
adjacent clusters**, enough to fan ten cathodes out of each. **The board is routable at the assumed
pitch.** That is a bound, not a measurement, and it is what `claude/QUADRANT-D-spec.md` §5.1 is
drawn on.
 
## Rev 4: the orientation key — two conventions are documented, and they disagree
 
- A commercial ИН-17 kit's assembly manual: **the anode lead is marked with a white bar**, and it
  goes into the hole marked on the silkscreen.
- tec.org.ru's catalogue entry: one lead is **«вывод обрезан (ключ)»** — physically cut short as an
  index.
Both cannot be true of one batch. **Look at the ten tubes on the bench and record which convention
they use** — it decides the silkscreen, and it decides whether J-QLEAD needs a keyway or a marked
slot. Four tubes soldered in rotated is not a recoverable mistake on a wire-ended part.
 
## Rev 4: handling limits, from the ТУ and from a kit that ships
 
Not dimensions, but they constrain the jig and the build step, so they live here:
 
- **Solder no closer than 8 mm from the glass. Bend no closer than 3 mm.** (ТУ scan.)
- Trim leads to **~15–20 mm** before insertion; flush after soldering. (Kit manual.)
- Straighten each lead with long-nose pliers before insertion; **tack two opposite leads first** to
  hold the tube square, then do the rest from the far side. (Kit manual.)
- **Burn each cathode in at 1.5–2 mA for 1–2 minutes before final soldering.** (ТУ scan.)
## Resolved: it's a lead spacer, not a base
 
The cream part slides along the leads rather than being fused to the glass — confirmed.
That's not a socket or a factory base; it's close kin to the **J-LEAD comb** already
planned in the build spec for holding IN-17 leads at final spacing while soldering. The
spec's "wire-ended, no socket" line stands as written. Worth dimensioning the spacer on
its own — its slide positions may double as a reference for the J-LEAD jig design, or at
minimum tell you the target lead spacing directly.
 
**Rev 3: QUADRANT-D raises the stakes on this.** Its build jig **J-QLEAD** has to hold four tubes
coplanar with all 48 leads at final spacing at once, and rows 10–12 below are the only data that
jig can be designed from.
 
The 19.30 mm reading was taken glass-only (confirmed). The 17.50 mm reading, by photo
composition, also looks like jaws-on-glass at a different point along the body, not on
the spacer — treating both as clean glass diameters, at two different axial positions.
 
**Rev 2 note:** the external listings below give the envelope as *three unequal numbers*
(≈22 × 20 × 14), i.e. a flattened rather than round envelope. If that is right, the 19.30 and
17.50 readings may not be two axial positions on a round body at all — they may be the major
axis and an off-axis chord of the *same* oval cross-section. Row 13 is added to settle it.

Consolidated envelope, **closed Rev 5 by the outline drawing**: **22 long × 20 stem × 14 face**, digits
9 mm, 12 flexible leads, 7 g. Our own 19.30 mm sits neatly on the "wide" figure, which is the
first external corroboration this sheet has had.
 
The **4 mA maximum overcurrent** figure is new and load-bearing: it rules out anode
multiplexing at 25 % duty for any 4-tube product, since that needs ~6 mA peak. Static drive
only. Recorded here because it changes a circuit decision, not just a dimension.
 
### Rev 4: a second source that looks like a contradiction and is not
 
tec.org.ru lists **ток в импульсе 3,5…7 мА** — pulsed current well above the 4 mA ceiling above —
alongside **минимальная длительность импульса 80 мкс** and **максимальная скважность 25**.
 
**Read the condition before reading the number.** Скважность 25 means the on-time is 1/25 of the
period — a **~4 % duty cycle**. The 7 mA allowance is granted at 4 % duty, not at the **25 % duty**
(скважность 4) a four-tube anode multiplex actually runs at. The two sources agree: **4 mA is the DC
ceiling, 7 mA is a 4 %-duty pulse ceiling, and a four-tube multiplex fits neither.** Static drive
stands, now on two independent sources instead of one.
 
### Rev 4: three source disagreements, recorded not resolved
 
| Parameter | Value A | Value B | Does it change a decision? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sustaining voltage | **≥105 V** (rudatasheet, swissnixie, ТУ scan) | **>160 V** (tec.org.ru) | **Yes.** QUADRANT-D's 56 kΩ anode resistor is (185 − 105) / 1.43 mA. At 160 V it would be badly wrong. Settle it with a meter across a lit tube — five minutes |
| Minimum life | **≥7 500 h** (soviet-tubes) / **≥9 000 h** (rudatasheet) | **1 000 h** (tec.org.ru) | Not today, but the whole 100 %-duty risk assessment (`QUADRANT-D-spec.md` §11.2) sits on the higher figure |
| Digit height | **9 mm** (four sources) | **~8 mm** (neonixie) | No — 1 mm does not move the window aperture past its tolerance |
 
### Rev 3 — two more electrical figures that turned into product decisions
 
- **≥200 V supply (eandc ТУ) against a 185 V shop rail.** QUADRANT-D runs the same 185 V as
  TERMINAL-06 for consistency, with the trimmer free to go to 200 V if a batch strikes reluctantly.
  That headroom only exists because its cathode driver was specified at **300 V (HV5630)** rather
  than the 220 V part originally proposed. **The tube's supply line is what sized the driver.**
- **7 500–9 000 h minimum life** is unremarkable at TERMINAL-06's 1/6 multiplex duty and is
  **ten to twelve months** on QUADRANT-D's static, 100 %-duty drive. It is now a commercial risk
  with its own mitigation list (`claude/QUADRANT-D-spec.md` §11.2), and this row is where the
  number came from.
## Still needed
 
**In the order QUADRANT-D needs them** — rows 5–7 are the half-hour that unblocks a PCB order:
 
1. **Lead pitch, diameter, free length, and exit pattern** (circle / comb / two rows) — confirm
   by hand, not photo-count. **This is the board footprint. Nothing can be ordered without it.**
2. **Row 13** — two orthogonal caliper readings at one axial position. Settles round vs oval,
   retro-labels rows 1–2, and sets the window aperture.
3. **Overall length**, real caliper reading laid flat along the tube's own axis (visual
   estimate above is a placeholder only).
4. **Row 9, from the end face** — how deep the digit sits behind the glass you look through.
5. **Lead spacer, dimensioned as its own small part** — outer size, hole/lead pitch, slide
   range along the leads. This is the J-QLEAD jig's data.
6. **Row 16, `cathodeMask`** — one `secCathodeMap` session, serves both products.
7. Two more full samples for the two diameters already in hand, per the min/mean/max rule.
8. **Digit centre height above the eventual jig seating plane** — needed before the SEC module's
   geometry (currently IN-17 centres at 136 / 152 mm per the concept plates) is anything more
   than a drawn assumption.
9. **From QUADRANT-W:** buy six ИН-19 (40 ₽, Begrant) and run this same sheet on them in the
   same session. If their shortest axis is under ~11 mm they replace ИН-16 in the wristwatch and
   the whole thickness problem goes away.
