TERMINAL-06 — building a 3D component library

Purpose: a parametric library of the physical parts so enclosure revisions are a boolean operation instead of a re-measurement. Open-source toolchain, STEP as the interchange format, git as the history.

0. What you are modelling (and what you are not)

You are not making replicas. You are making fit solids. Every part in the library carries two solids:

Solid	Contains	Used for
BODY	Simplified real geometry — envelope, bushing, base, leads	Renders, sanity, section views
KEEPOUT	Worst case swept volume: body ⊕ lever swing ⊕ plunger travel ⊕ tool access ⊕ finger access	The boolean the enclosure is cut with

Clearance lives in KEEPOUT, never in the enclosure. When you find out the МТ1 lever fouls the brow, you fix it in one file and every downstream part updates.

The third thing each part carries is a published hole diameter. The fascia sketch should reference INS_MT1.HOLE_D, not a number you typed from memory.

1. Toolchain
Tool	Role	Why this one

KiCad 8/9 + StepUp	Export TS06-FASCIA and the tube board as STEP	Makes the PCB the single source of truth for hole X/Y. You already have Gerbers; hole positions should never be typed twice
OpenSCAD	Optional, for parts that are pure numbers (neon holder, J-LEAD comb, print jigs)	Plain text → diffs cleanly in git, trivially parametric
Blender 4.x	Listing renders only	Import via FreeCAD → glTF/OBJ. Do not model dimensioned parts here
Inkscape	Trace scanned silhouettes → SVG → FreeCAD sketch	For irregular metal bodies (КМД1-1 box, lever mounting plate)
git + git-lfs	History	.FCStd is a zip; LFS it. Commit measurement tables as plain text so they diff

Skip photogrammetry. Meshroom on a nixie tube is the worst case in the discipline: transparent envelope, specular metal cathodes, no texture, and no scale reference. You get an unusable mesh after an hour. Calipers take four minutes and are more accurate.

2. Conventions — decide once, before the first part
Origin rules

These matter more than modelling skill. Get them right and placing a part in an assembly is one drag, no arithmetic.

Panel-mount parts (levers, buttons): origin at the centre of the mounting hole, on the front face of the panel. +Z points out of the panel toward the user. +Y up. The bushing straddles the origin — hardware in front, body behind.
Socketed board-mount parts (IN-12): the tube's own origin is on the tube axis at the pin-exit shoulder — the plane where glass meets the pin comb, i.e. where it seats into a socket. That's intrinsic to the tube and doesn't move if the board layout does. The socket is a separate part, origin on the tube axis at the PCB top surface; its height is what actually places the tube above the board. Digit-centre-above-PCB is therefore always socket.seat_h + tube.pin_exit_to_digit, never a number either part owns by itself — don't let it drift back into being "the tube's height." +Z along the viewing direction.
Wire-ended parts (IN-17, ИНС-1): origin at the seating plane your jig defines, not at the glass. The J-LEAD comb sets the real Z — so model the jig and let it own the datum, and the tube references it. The samples on the mat came with a loose moulded spacer that slides along the leads rather than a fused base — that's not a socket, it's close kin to the J-LEAD comb the build plan already calls for. Worth photographing it dimensioned on its own; it may save you designing the jig from scratch.

Spreadsheet-driven

Every part document opens with a dims spreadsheet. Alias every measured number: env_w, env_h, pin_pcd, bush_d, bush_len, body_l, swing_deg, clear_r, panel_t. Nothing typed into a sketch dimension box. When you re-measure a better sample, you edit one cell.

Also alias the derived values you want published: HOLE_D = bush_d + fit_clear.

3. Measuring

Ground rules for Soviet stock:

Digital calipers at 0.01 mm. Measure three samples minimum, record min / mean / max. Design against max + clearance. Your own spec already flags batch variance in envelope tint — dimensional spread is the same story.
Never clamp glass. Set the jaws slightly open and slide the part until it just drags.
Threads: pitch gauge plus major diameter. Record as M12×0.75, not "about 12".
Flatbed scanner trick — part flat on the glass, black card draped over it, 600 dpi, steel rule in frame. You get a 1:1 silhouette. Verify against the rule, trace in Inkscape, import the SVG as a FreeCAD sketch and dimension over it. Best method there is for the КМД1-1's stamped box and the toggle's mounting plate.
Photograph alternative: maximum working distance plus zoom, ruler in the same plane as the feature. Your cutting-mat shots are good documentation but too close for tracing — perspective on the near corner will cost you a millimetre.

4. FreeCAD build recipe (per part)
New document. Add a spreadsheet named dims, alias everything from the capture sheet.
New Body. Sketch on an origin plane or a datum plane — never on a face of an existing feature. Face-attached sketches are what break when you change a dimension. FreeCAD 1.0 is better here but not immune.
Revolve / pad the BODY. Simplify aggressively:
No modelled threads. A plain cylinder at major diameter. Cosmetic thread only if a render needs it, in a separate body you can suppress
No knurling
Skip chamfers under ~0.3 mm Threads and knurls destroy rebuild time and bloat STEP files for zero fit information.
Second Body named KEEPOUT, same document, red at 60 % transparency:
Body silhouette + radial clearance
Lever swing cone / plunger travel
Tool access. A nut driver on a 13 mm bushing nut needs roughly 17–19 mm across flats plus wall — model the socket OD as a cylinder from the panel face forward. This is the classic panel-mount failure: five controls that individually fit and collectively cannot be tightened
Finger access on anything the customer operates
Export both to lib/step/.
Assembly document: App::Link each part in, attach to the fascia face by LCS.
Enclosure document: boolean-subtract the union of all KEEPOUT solids, then Part → Check Geometry, then take a section along the digit centreline and check it against the numbers already committed in the spec (trench 166 × 21, floor 32 mm back, digit centreline 17 mm down, brow y 9.5–22 / z 74.5–79).
5. Verify before spending money
Fascia proxy: print 176 × 52 × 1.6 mm in PETG with the exact hole pattern and fit the real switches. Two hours of print time against a 15-board Rezonit order in black mask with a surcharge and a long lead time.
Trench coupon: one tube position, full rake, real IN-12 seated in its actual socket — not floated at an assumed height. A tube held loose at "roughly where it'll sit" confirms nothing about the real digit centreline, since that number is a socket + tube stack-up, not a tube property. Confirms the 17 mm centreline and the brow clearance at a 10° viewing angle before you commit ten hours of chassis print.
Interference check in the FreeCAD assembly, then a visual pass at the listing camera angle.

7. Pitfalls
Datasheet dimensions are a starting hypothesis, not a measurement. 1984 stock from Meshok is not held to the drawing.
Never use STL between your own tools. It is a lossy export format. STEP in, STEP out.
Don't put clearance in the enclosure. It belongs in KEEPOUT, in the part file.
Don't attach sketches to faces. Datum planes only.
Glass gets max-of-sample + 0.4 mm, minimum.
One part per file. The temptation to build the whole clock in one document ends in a 40-second rebuild and a dependency graph you cannot reason about.
Open flag on the photographed parts

The spec currently fixes the fascia at ⌀12.2 mm for 12 mm anti-vandal buttons and ⌀13.2 mm for the levers. The button in these photos is a КМД1-1, which is not that part — different bushing, and a stamped rectangular body rather than a round barrel.

Three things to settle before the fascia goes to Rezonit:

Re-derive HOLE_D for both controls from measured bushings, plus the anti-rotation flat geometry.
Body depth behind the panel against the 12° rake — the wedge gives you depth at the bottom of the face and takes it away at the top.
Lug and body footprint on a 176 × 52 mm board. SET at X = 22 and ADJ at X = 154 put a rectangular body close to the board edge; the levers at 64 / 88 / 112 sit on a 24 mm pitch, which is where the nut-driver keepout earns its place.

Modelling those two parts first, from the samples in front of you, answers all three.
