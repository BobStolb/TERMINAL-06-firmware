## P2 — flash the six-tube firmware onto the four-tube clock ← BLOCKED, 01.09.26
 
**What this step exists to prove:** that the six-tube multiplex firmware — specifically
the ISR/prescaler rewrite that takes the display refresh from the stock board's ~50 Hz up
to ~400 Hz — actually works correctly on real hardware, using the four ИН-12 tubes already
built and working, *before* any new hardware (SEC module, colon, AM/PM pair) is added. It
is a gate: nothing after P2 starts until it passes clean. It does not test the rotary, the
buttons, the lever, the colon, or anything from Rev D — purely the digit multiplex.
 
Pass criteria, unchanged from the original plan:
1. All four ИН-12 digits display correctly, no cathode swaps.
2. D13's onboard LED flashes once for about a second at boot, then stays off.
3. No HV glow on the two unpopulated channels (D2, D13 — no tube there yet).
4. Filmed at 60 fps for 5 s, no banding/flicker on playback — the actual point of the
   prescaler rewrite (the stock 50 Hz rate beats against camera shutters).
The display-layer port (Timer2 prescaler 8→1, 401 Hz, buzzer removed, rev B controls) lives
at **[github.com/BobStolb/TERMINAL-06-firmware](https://github.com/BobStolb/TERMINAL-06-firmware)**
(HEAD `5b5b29f` as of the libraries update; bench-testing below happened on top of that,
see the BOARD_TYPE and ghosting findings for what's changed locally and not yet pushed).
 
### Bench result, 01.09.26 — two separate findings
 
**Finding 1 — BOARD_TYPE was wrong. Fixed, solid, unrelated to finding 2.** The firmware
shipped with `#define BOARD_TYPE 1` ("IN-12 upside down — AlexGyver IN-12 board as
shipped"). The actual physical board needs **`BOARD_TYPE 0`**. This was the direct cause
of the wrong/scrambled-looking digits seen in early bench testing — a single-digit,
wrong-cathode-mapping problem, not the ghosting described below. **Commit this fix to the
repo regardless of how the ghosting question resolves** — it's independently confirmed
correct and doesn't depend on anything else in this section.
 
**Finding 2 — real ghosting at the target refresh rate, root-caused to the anode driver's
quench speed, not fixable in firmware alone.** With BOARD_TYPE corrected, a second and
separate symptom remained: at ~400 Hz, multiple digits partially light at once on the same
tube — confirmed by eye, not a camera artifact, and persistent (not the ~300 ms power-on
anti-poisoning sweep, `burnIndicators()`, which was ruled out early on since this doesn't
clear after boot).
 
The team bracketed the only firmware lever available — dead-time forced between tube slots
before the next tube's anode turns on, traded directly against refresh rate, since both draw
on the same fixed per-tube rotation-time budget — at both extremes:
 
- **~400 Hz, `DEAD_TIME_TICKS = 0`:** clean on camera by design (the original point of the
  prescaler-1 rewrite), but severe ghosting.
- **~75 Hz, `DEAD_TIME_TICKS = 182`, `NUM_INDI = 4`:** ghosting reduced to near-
  imperceptible, but noticeable flicker at 60 fps returns — confirmed on camera.
**There is no middle setting to go hunting for.** A camera-safe refresh rate and enough
dead-time to let a tube fully quench pull in directly opposite directions on this hardware,
because both draw from the same rotation-time budget. This is a real ceiling on what
firmware timing alone can do here, not a "try a few more numbers" problem.
 
**What this points to:** the anode driver/tube isn't quenching fast enough on its own, so
firmware can only trade one symptom for the other, never eliminate both. This is exactly
the failure mode the spec already anticipated a bleed resistor for on the SEC module's
ИН-17s (`claude/TERMINAL-06-spec.md` §4: "1 MΩ anode bleed... ghost suppression") — an
active discharge path that speeds up the actual physical quench time, rather than firmware
just waiting longer for a slow passive one. With that in place, a fast rotation (400 Hz)
could plausibly stay clean without needing any `DEAD_TIME_TICKS` margin at all, satisfying
both criteria simultaneously instead of trading one for the other. **This is now flagged
as a possible requirement on the MAIN board's ИН-12 anode drivers too, not only the SEC
module's ИН-17s** — see the caveat added to spec §4.
 
**Scorecard against the four pass criteria:**
1. Correct digits — **pass**, via the BOARD_TYPE fix, solid and unrelated to finding 2.
2. D13 boot-flash behaviour — unaffected, not explicitly re-verified after the fixes, but
   no reason to expect a regression.
3. No HV on unpopulated channels — unaffected, same as #2.
4. No camera banding — **fail**, at the only setting (~400 Hz) where digits are clean.
   This isn't a tuning gap, it's a confirmed conflict between criteria 1/4 and the
   ghosting symptom.
**P2 status: BLOCKED, not just incomplete.** Root cause identified (anode driver quench
time); firmware-only mitigation proven insufficient in both directions; a hardware fix
(active anode discharge / bleed resistor, or a faster-quenching driver stage) is needed on
the main board before re-test. **The BOARD_TYPE fix should go into the repo regardless** —
it's solid and unrelated. Whether to leave `DEAD_TIME_TICKS`/`NUM_INDI` at their current
diagnostic bracketing values or revert them to production defaults (`0` / `6`) while the
hardware fix is pending: **recommendation is to revert to `0`/`6`** — neither bracketed
extreme is a viable shipped configuration, and leaving an experimental diagnostic value in
place risks it being mistaken for a real setting later. Commit the BOARD_TYPE fix with the
values reverted, and leave this writeup (not a code comment) as the record of what was
tried and why.
 
**Nothing downstream of this gate (P3 onward) should start until a hardware fix is
bench-tested and this scorecard re-run clean.**
 
### Finding 2, refined — 04.09.26: the quench limit is the **TLP627's own turn-off time**, and it gets *worse*, not better, as tubes are added
 
The 01.09.26 writeup above called this "anode driver quench time" and assumed the slow
element was **anode-node capacitance** discharging through the tube. That framing was
directionally right but not precise enough to specify a fix. Inspecting the board's actual
BOM/schematic against the observed behaviour puts the dominant term somewhere else:
 
**The TLP627 is a photo-Darlington optocoupler.** A Darlington output stage has a
**storage / turn-off tail** — after the drive to its internal LED is removed, the output
transistor keeps conducting for a further period while the base charge clears. Working
figure: **~100 µs** (order: tens to a couple hundred µs, load-dependent — *verify against
the Toshiba TLP627 datasheet before committing to a design*). This is a fixed physical
property of the part. It is **not** a capacitance effect, and no amount of anode-side
capacitance reduction removes it.
 
That single number explains the whole bracketing result, quantitatively:
 
| Configuration | Frame rate | Slot per tube | ~100 µs tail as % of slot | Observed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock, 4 tubes | ~50 Hz | ~5000 µs | **2 %** | invisible — never a problem on the stock firmware |
| Stock, 4 tubes | ~400 Hz | ~625 µs | **16 %** | visible |
| **Target, 6 tubes** | ~400 Hz | ~416 µs | **24 %** | **glaring — this is P2's symptom** |
| 8 glass (6 digits + 2 more in the chain) | ~400 Hz | ~312 µs | **32 %** | worse still |
 
Mechanism, precisely: the opto is still conducting into the *previous* tube's anode while
the К155ИД1 has already switched cathode selection to the *next* digit. The stale tube
therefore faintly shows the **new** digit. This is why forced dead-time helped — it was
buying time for the tail to clear — and why it could only ever trade against refresh rate:
the tail is a constant, so the only firmware lever is to make the slot longer.
 
**Tube-count scaling — the question this raises, answered.** The intuition that adding four
more tubes will *increase current draw and therefore leave less charge behind to ghost* is
a coherent mechanism in general, but it does **not** apply to this circuit, for two reasons:
 
1. **The display is multiplexed — only one tube conducts at any instant.** Each tube's anode
   sits behind its own TLP627. When that opto turns off, that anode node becomes an
   electrical island: the *only* path off it is through its own tube. The other tubes cannot
   drain it, because their own optos are open. The shared cathode bus is not a relief valve
   either — it is the **destination** of the ghost current, not an escape from it. This is
   exactly why a bleed resistor is required: it is the only *deliberate* path off that
   island. Adding tubes adds more islands; it does not connect them.
2. **More tubes divides the frame into more slots.** At a fixed frame rate, each additional
   tube makes every slot *shorter*, so the fixed ~100 µs tail becomes a *larger* fraction —
   see the table. **Ghosting scales in the wrong direction with tube count.**
There is also a second-order brightness cost: per-tube duty falls from 1/6 to 1/8 (**~25 %
dimmer per tube**) before any blanking is applied — which eats the same rail headroom the
BCD-blanking remediation below depends on.
 
**Practical consequence: fix the ghosting at six before scaling the multiplex chain.**
Eight is a strictly harder version of the same problem, not an escape from it.
*(Exception: any lamp driven **statically** rather than through the digit multiplex — the
colon pair and the ИН-15 AM/PM tubes on the MCP23017s — adds no slot and has no tail. None
of the above applies to those. Only tubes joined to the anode-multiplex rotation count.)*
 
### Physical architecture correction — 04.09.26
 
The ИН-12 tubes **do not** mount directly on the main driver board, despite the two
silkscreened `IN-12` footprints visible on it. In this actual build each **pair** of tubes
sits on a separate small **daughterboard** (hand-decorated with white "troll face" doodle
art around each socket's pin ring), connected back to the main board by a straight
multi-pin header at the top edge. Two such daughterboards carry the four tubes.
 
Within each socket's pin ring, **one pin sits alone inside a distinct circular outline**,
separated from the ring of ~8–9 others — the candidate anode pin. **Not yet meter-confirmed.**
Confirmation procedure (board dead and bled): check continuity between the *same-position*
pin on two *different* tube sockets. **No continuity → anode** (unique per tube).
**Continuity → shared cathode** (all like digits bussed in parallel to the К155ИД1).
 
Construction is **single-sided**: all copper and all through-hole solder joints are on the
back. **Any existing through-hole joint on the back is a legal tap point** — reflow the
existing blob and slide the new resistor lead in alongside the original pin. **No trace
cutting is required anywhere in this fix.**
 
### Component identification — 04.09.26
 
- **HV set-point trimmer:** the blue preset, marked **P504** (= 500 kΩ; BOM lists 470 k/500 k
  class). Sets the boost converter's rail voltage. *Identified from BOM inference plus the
  board marking, not from a schematic* — confirm with a meter (resistance from each leg to
  HV+, to ground, and to any Nano analog pin; board dead and bled) before relying on it.
- **The 100 Ω resistors beside the optos are LED-side current limiters** (~38 mA into each
  TLP627's internal LED). They are **not** anode-side resistors. **The D3–D6 pads are the
  wrong place to add anything for ghosting** — adding series resistance there only dims the
  tubes and, if anything, slows the Darlington further.
- **Voltage ceiling on this board is the TLP627's 300 V rating** — *not* the 4.7 µF
  reservoir cap (350–400 V) and *not* the boost switch (500 V class, likely IRF840). 185 V
  has margin; 250 V does not.
- Do not confuse the **1 MΩ anode bleed** (ghost suppression, anode node → ground) with the
  **12 kΩ-class anode series resistor** (current limiting, in-line). Different parts,
  different jobs; see spec §4.
### Three remediation paths, in test order — 04.09.26
 
Change **one variable at a time**, and log rail voltage at every step.
 
0. **Confirm the opto part number** stamped on the packages, and note the current trimmer
   position and measured rail voltage as the baseline.
1. **1 MΩ 0.5 W anode bleed, one tube first.** Anode node → confirmed ground. Anode-node
   capacitance ≈30 pF gives τ ≈ 30 µs (fast against a 416 µs slot); cost ≈34 mW per tube.
   **Expect an improvement, not a cure** — this removes the capacitive tail but does nothing
   about the Darlington's own conduction tail. This is the same part already budgeted for
   the SEC module's ИН-17s (spec §4).
2. **Raise one opto's LED resistor 100 Ω → 470 Ω** (≈8 mA). Pulls the Darlington out of deep
   saturation, which shortens storage time. Costs brightness on that channel. **A 5 ₽ test
   and fully reversible** — do one channel and compare against its neighbours.
3. **BCD blanking + trimmer recovery (no new parts).** The К155ИД1's input codes **10–15 are
   invalid** — no cathode is selected, the display is blanked. Writing a blanking code
   between digit slots buys *real* dead-time **without** reducing the frame rate, which is a
   different axis from the 01.09.26 refresh-rate-vs-dead-time bracketing. A ~100 µs tail
   needs ~25 % of each slot blanked → **~25 % brightness cost**, recoverable by nudging P504
   up — staying well under the TLP627's 300 V ceiling.
The likely shipped answer is **1 + 3 combined**. Path 2 is a cheap diagnostic that tells you
how much of the tail is saturation-related before committing to the blanking cost.
 
**Safety, every time:** the 4.7 µF reservoir cap stays charged after power-off. Bleed it
through ~100 kΩ for ~10 s and **verify <10 V on a meter** before touching the HV side. One
hand in your pocket.
 
### PARTIAL RETRACTION — 04.09.26: "there is no middle setting" was drawn from confounded data
 
Re-reading the stock AlexGyver ISR (`firmware/nixieClock_2_v2.5/isr.ino`, synced repo)
against the 01.09.26 bracketing shows the two test points **changed three variables at
once** and the conclusion drawn from them does not hold:
 
| Test point | Frame rate | DEAD_TIME_TICKS | NUM_INDI | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | ~400 Hz | 0 | 6 | ghosting |
| B | ~75 Hz | 182 | **4** | clean, but flicker |
 
**Nothing between A and B was ever tested, and B moved the refresh rate and the tube count
as well as the dead time.** "No middle setting to go hunting for" was a conclusion about
two extreme points, not about the space between them.
 
**The mechanism, from the stock ISR:**
 
```c
indiCounter[curIndi]++;
if (indiCounter[curIndi] >= indiDimm[curIndi])
  setPin(opts[curIndi], 0);          // anode OFF at the dimming threshold
if (indiCounter[curIndi] > 25) {     // slot ends, decoder switches
  ...                                 // ← the ghost window is between these two events
```
 
**Dead time in ticks = 26 − `indiDimm`.** The brightness control *is already* a dead-time
control — the anode is cut early and the decoder does not switch until tick 26. At full
brightness (`indiDimm` = 25) that gap is **one tick**, and one tick is nowhere near a
~100 µs tail. **Full brightness is therefore the maximum-ghosting condition by
construction.**
 
At ~400 Hz over 6 tubes the slot is ~416 µs across 25 ticks → **~16.6 µs per tick**. So:
 
| `indiDimm` | Dead time | Brightness | vs ~100 µs tail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | ~17 µs | 100 % | far too short — **ghosts** |
| 22 | ~66 µs | 88 % | marginal |
| **19** | **~116 µs** | **76 %** | **should clear it** |
| 16 | ~166 µs | 64 % | comfortable margin |
 
**Concrete prediction: ~76 % brightness clears the ghosting at the full 400 Hz frame rate
with no hardware change at all.**
 
**The exact knob: `#define INDI_BRIGHT` at the top of the main `.ino` (range 0–24).**
`changeBright()` copies it into `indiMaxBright` → `indiDimm[]`, which is the ISR threshold
above. So this is a **one-line source edit and a reflash** — not a trimmer, not a button.
Sweep **24 → 20 → 18 → 12** and watch the ghost. Dead time = 26 − `INDI_BRIGHT` ticks
(≈16.6 µs each): 24 → ~33 µs, 20 → ~100 µs, 18 → ~133 µs, 12 → ~233 µs.
 
**P504 is NOT the brightness control and must not be used for this test.** It sets the HV
rail. Turning it changes voltage, not the anode/decoder timing that causes the ghost, and
turning it up walks toward the TLP627's 300 V ceiling. It is only for recovering brightness
*after* a working `INDI_BRIGHT` is found.
 
*Check: `changeBright()` sits inside `#if (NIGHT_LIGHT == 1)`. If `NIGHT_LIGHT` is 0 in the
fork, `indiDimm[]` is being set somewhere else — find that instead. Symptom: changing
`INDI_BRIGHT` and reflashing produces no visible change.*
 
**Why B's `DEAD_TIME_TICKS` behaved so differently:** at 182 it plainly *extended* the slot
rather than stealing ticks from inside it — which is why the frame rate collapsed to 75 Hz.
**Dead time taken from inside the slot costs brightness; dead time added to the slot costs
refresh rate.** The 01.09.26 writeup treated these as the same budget. They are not, and
that is the error that made the problem look unsolvable in firmware.
 
**Immediate consequence for P2: the cheapest test has never been run, and it needs no
soldering iron, no parts and no code change** — just turn the display brightness down at
400 Hz and watch the ghosting. If it recedes as the display dims, the mechanism is confirmed
and this is a firmware fix, not a hardware one. Then cap maximum brightness at the smallest
`indiDimm` that clears it and recover the lost output on P504 (ceiling: the TLP627's 300 V).
 
*Caveat: the fork's ISR (`BobStolb/TERMINAL-06-firmware`) is rewritten — cached dim/port/mask,
6 tubes, an explicit `DEAD_TIME_TICKS` — so the variable names above are the stock v2.5
ones. Check the fork's own `isr.ino` for where the anode-off and decoder-switch actually sit
relative to each other. The 25-tick figure and the ~16.6 µs/tick that follows from it should
also be confirmed against the fork's real OCR2A, not assumed from stock.*
 
### BENCH RESULT — 05.09.26: CONFIRMED. The prediction holds; this is a firmware fix.
 
**First real bench data P2 has produced.** Two photos, same tubes, same framing, **both at
ISO 50** (fair comparison, exposure not doing the work):
 
| `INDI_BRIGHT` | Dead time | Observed |
|---|---|---|
| **24** (max) | ~33 µs | Whole cathode stack hazed — multiple digits visibly glowing at once |
| **12** | ~233 µs | Digits clean, unlit cathodes actually dark |
 
**The ghosting tracks `INDI_BRIGHT` exactly as predicted.** That closes the question the
01.09.26 bracketing got wrong: the ghost window is the gap between anode-off and
decoder-switch, the brightness constant sets that gap, and it is reachable in firmware at
the full frame rate. **No bleed resistor, no opto change, no PCB revision is required to
clear it.**
 
Consequences, immediately:
- **T1.1 is the fix.** The three remediation paths in the study are superseded as *primary*
  options; the bleed footprints on TS06-TUBE/TS06-SEC drop to **DNP insurance**, laid out
  but unpopulated (which is exactly why they were specced as footprints rather than parts).
- **The 01.09.26 "hardware fix required" conclusion is now formally wrong**, not merely
  imprecise. Recorded rather than deleted — the confounded-bracketing error is the useful
  lesson.
**Still open — the number that matters is the *highest* working value, not any working
value.** Every point of `INDI_BRIGHT` given up is brightness given up. 12 works but is
visibly dim; the job now is to bisect upward (try 18, then 20/21 if clean, 15 if not) and
find the threshold, then recover the remainder on P504 under the TLP627's 300 V ceiling.
 
**Bonus — this measures the TLP627's turn-off tail with no oscilloscope.** The threshold
value *is* the measurement: tail ≈ (26 − `INDI_BRIGHT`) × tick period. If 18 is clean and 20
is not, the tail sits between ~100 µs and ~133 µs, which would confirm the ~100 µs working
figure the whole study was sized against. The display is the instrument.
 
**Caveat that must be resolved before this result is carried to production: what were
`NUM_INDI` and the frame rate during this test?** Dead time in *ticks* is 26 − `INDI_BRIGHT`
regardless of tube count, but frame rate is 25 × `NUM_INDI` ticks. Holding ~400 Hz with six
tubes instead of four requires a ~1.5× faster tick, which shrinks each tick and therefore
demands ~1.5× more ticks of dead time for the same absolute tail — i.e. a proportionally
lower `INDI_BRIGHT` and a bigger brightness cost. **A threshold found at four tubes does not
transfer to six unchanged.** This is the tube-count scaling from the 04.09.26 refinement,
now in units that can be measured.
 
### ROOT CAUSE FOUND — 05.09.26: the prescaler rewrite silently rescaled the brightness constant by 8×
 
**The user's own observation cracked it:** stock `nixieClock_2_v2.5.ino` at `INDI_BRIGHT 24`
is *flawless* on the same hardware; the fork at 24 ghosts badly and needs 5–8 to approach
clean. Same board, same tubes, same optos — so this was never a hardware limitation.
 
**The fork's ISR header states the tick rate outright** (`firmware/nixieClock_TS06/isr.ino`):
Timer2 Fast PWM, TOP = 255, **prescaler 1 → 62.5 kHz → one tick = 16 µs**. Stock runs the
identical structure at **prescaler 8 → 7.81 kHz → one tick = 128 µs**.
 
Dead time is counted in *ticks* (`26 − INDI_BRIGHT`) in both. **The tick got 8× shorter and
nothing rescaled the brightness constant to compensate:**
 
| Build | Prescaler | Tick | `INDI_BRIGHT` | Dead time | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock v2.5 | 8 | 128 µs | 24 | **256 µs** | flawless |
| Fork | 1 | 16 µs | 24 | **32 µs** | severe ghosting |
| Fork | 1 | 16 µs | 12 | 224 µs | haze + flicker |
| Fork | 1 | 16 µs | 5–8 | 288–336 µs | near-clean |
 
**Stock is not better engineered. It is slower, and slow accidentally hides the opto's
tail.** This is a units regression introduced by the prescaler-1 rewrite, not a property of
the board.
 
### The TLP627 tail is now MEASURED — and it is ~3× the assumed figure
 
Stock is clean at **256 µs** of dead time; the fork needs **~288 µs** to approach clean.
Those agree. **The TLP627's real turn-off tail on this load is ~250–300 µs, not the ~100 µs
working figure every estimate in this project has been sized against.** Measured with no
oscilloscope, using the display as the instrument and stock firmware as the reference point.
 
**This retracts the 05.09.26 "the resistors are probably dead" call.** Run the arithmetic at
the real number: 6 tubes, 400 Hz frame → 416 µs slot; a 288 µs dead time leaves 128 µs of
on-time — **~31 % duty, ~2/3 of the brightness gone.** That is too expensive to ship.
**Hardware help is back on the table** (T1.2's 470 Ω LED resistor becomes considerably more
attractive now that the tail is known to be large), but the target is now a measured number
instead of a guess.
 
### Two cheap tests before anything else
 
**1. Turn the effects OFF and re-judge.** The fork added *"anti-poisoning and glitch across
six tubes"* (spec §5). `GLITCH_ALLOWED = 1` deliberately lights wrong digits, and the
anti-poisoning cycle sweeps cathodes — **both look exactly like haze and flicker.** The
residual seen at `INDI_BRIGHT` 5–8, and the "noticeable flicker" at 12, may be partly or
wholly deliberate effects being mistaken for ghosting. Set `GLITCH_ALLOWED = 0` and disable
the anti-poisoning cycle, then re-judge. **No conclusion about residual haze is safe until
this is ruled out.**
 
**2. Ask whether 400 Hz is actually required — nobody has tested it.** At a fixed 288 µs
tail, halving the frame rate doubles the on-time for the same ghost margin:
 
| Frame rate | Slot (6 tubes) | Dead | On | Duty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400 Hz | 416 µs | 288 µs | 128 µs | **31 %** |
| **200 Hz** | 832 µs | 288 µs | 544 µs | **65 %** |
 
**Twice the brightness at the same ghost margin.** 200 Hz is far above the 50 Hz that beat
against camera shutters and almost certainly still films clean — but that is an assumption
until it is filmed at 60 fps, which is a five-minute test.
 
**Implementation note — Timer2 has no ÷2 or ÷4.** Its prescaler options are 1, 8, 32, 64,
128, 256, 1024 only, which is exactly why the original rewrite jumped 8→1 with nothing in
between. So 200 Hz is *not* reached via the prescaler. Keep prescaler 1 and **lengthen the
slot instead: change the per-tube tick count from 26 to 52** (`if (c > 25)` → `if (c > 51)`)
and extend the brightness range to 0–50. Then 62500 / 52 / 6 = **200 Hz**, and
`INDI_BRIGHT 34` gives 18 ticks (288 µs) of dead time at **65 % duty**. Two constants. ISR
cost is unchanged (still 62.5 kHz); the changeover path runs once in 52 instead of once in
26, so marginally cheaper.

**Consequences — both of the previous two entries must be downgraded:**

### The measurement that will actually settle it
 
The new branch is the instrument: known constants, built from clean `main`, effects

(18 → 12 → 8 → 4) with `GLITCH_ENABLED`/`BURN_ENABLED` off, holding everything else fixed.
The lowest value that is still clean by eye, × 16 µs, **is** the TLP627's tail — measured on

### Brightness — correcting a framing error in both directions
 
Claude Code's headline: at `SLOT_TICKS 52`, per-tube duty over the whole frame is

**Arithmetic correct, baseline wrong.** `INDI_BRIGHT 23` at `SLOT_TICKS 26` gives only 3

### Findings from the implementation worth keeping
 
- **`SEC_BRIGHT_TRIM` is now a complete no-op** — `min(34+4, 34) = 34`. It was nearly dead
  before too (`23+2` clamped to 24 = 1 tick). **This matters at P4/P7:** §4 specifies the
  ИН-17 sustains ~105 V vs the ИН-12's ~140 V and hogs current, so per-tube brightness trim
  is how the seconds tubes get balanced against the hours. To get any trim range back,
  `INDI_BRIGHT` must sit below `MAX_BRIGHT`. **Open item, not yet decided.**
- **`BACKL` (pin 11) is `OC2A`** — the backlight PWM writes the same `OCR2A` whose compare
  match generates the multiplex interrupt. Fast PWM with TOP = 255 means one match per period
  regardless of value, so the ISR *rate* is safe, but **changing backlight brightness shifts
  the ISR's phase.** Pre-existing, harmless, but it is a variable to hold fixed during any
  ghost judgement.
- **ISR cost was never measured before and the old comment was wrong by ~2×**: measured
  **86 cy mean / 161 worst of 256** = 33.5 % CPU, against the comment's claimed "~45 cycles,
  about 18 %". Unchanged by this work (the longer slot runs the expensive changeover half as
  often, offsetting the extra branch). Build: 13506 B flash (41.2 %), 468 B RAM (22.9 %).
- **The anti-poisoning routine has no timing dependency** — it only writes `indiDigits[]`.
  The brief was wrong to list it as needing a rescale.
- Magic numbers found that were not anticipated: `applyBright()` clamped to a hardcoded 24;
  `curCount` seeded to literal `26` in `0_data.ino` (at slot 52 the first frame would have
  started mid-slot); `FLIP_SPEED[1]` crossfade steps *brightness*, so rescaling 23 → 34 made
  the fade run 48 % long.
- Bench work preserved on `bench/ghost-diagnostics-wip`; fix on `fix/multiplex-dead-time`;
  nothing pushed. `BOARD_TYPE 0` and a `newTimeFlag` hour-rollover fix carried across.
### THE DEAD_TICKS SWEEP DID NOTHING — 05.09.26 (later still)
 
Bench report: "even on dead ticks = 2 the overlap is quite severe, but no annoying flicker
though, even on 18. But the ghosting is definitely worse than before."
 
**`DEAD_TICKS` is a ceiling, not the dead-time control.** The clamp is
`curDimm = min(indiDimm[i], SLOT_TICKS - DEAD_TICKS)`, and it can only ever *lower* `curDimm`.
With `INDI_BRIGHT 34` and `SLOT_TICKS 52`:
 
| `DEAD_TICKS` | Clamp ceiling | `curDimm` | Actual dead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | 52 − 18 = 34 | min(34, 34) = **34** | 18 ticks — 288 µs |
| 2 | 52 − 2 = 50 | min(34, 50) = **34** | 18 ticks — **288 µs, identical** |
 
`INDI_BRIGHT` is the binding constraint, not the clamp. **Both settings produced exactly the
same 288 µs, which is why they looked the same.** No data was generated.
 
**The real dead-time control is `INDI_BRIGHT`**: dead time = (`SLOT_TICKS` − `INDI_BRIGHT`)
× 16 µs. `DEAD_TICKS` only exists to stop brightness from eating the margin — it is a safety
rail, and it does its job. It is not a knob to sweep.
 
### "Worse than before" is real data, and it is bad news
 
The comparison the eye is making is against the 01.09.26 dirty tree — and that configuration
had an *enormous* dead window:
 
| Build | Anode-off → next anode-on |
|---|---|
| Dirty tree (`TUBE_TICKS 208`, blank at 26, `INDI_BRIGHT 5`) | (26−5)×16 + 182×16 = **3248 µs** |
| New branch (`SLOT 52`, `INDI_BRIGHT 34`) | 18×16 = **288 µs** |
 
**11× less.** Of course it ghosts worse. **So the tail is well above 288 µs** — far beyond the
~100 µs this project has been sized against, and beyond the ~250–300 µs briefly claimed and
already withdrawn. The honest bracket right now is **>288 µs, and ≤3248 µs clears it (mostly
— even there a faint haze was reported at `INDI_BRIGHT` 5–8).**
 
**This partially vindicates the 01.09.26 conclusion I retracted.** "No middle setting"
was drawn from confounded data and the reasoning behind it was wrong, but the *verdict* may
turn out right. If the tail is ~1 ms or more, no camera-safe refresh rate survives it in
firmware alone and a hardware fix is required after all.
 
**Banked, and genuinely good:** **no flicker at 200 Hz**, confirmed by eye. The camera/flicker
criterion is reachable. The only open question is whether the tail fits inside a 200 Hz slot.
 
### New hypothesis: this may be the TUBE, not the optocoupler
 
A 1–3 ms tail is very long for a Darlington. It is entirely ordinary for **nixie deionization
time** — residual ionised gas in the envelope keeps the tube able to conduct at a reduced
striking voltage for hundreds of µs to several ms after drive is removed. That would produce
exactly this symptom and exactly this magnitude.
 
**It changes which hardware fix is right:**
- If it is the **opto**: the T1.2 LED-resistor change and an opto-stage swap help; the bleed
  resistor mostly does not.
- If it is **deionization**: the **1 MΩ anode bleed is the correct fix** (pull the anode below
  the sustaining voltage fast so the gas stops being fed), and swapping the opto buys nothing.
**Discriminator:** an opto tail is a fixed electrical property, independent of how hard the
tube was driven. Deionization scales with conduction — more current and longer on-time mean
more ionisation and slower recovery. So: **hold dead time fixed and vary on-time (or the HV
rail). If the required dead time grows with on-time or with rail voltage, it is the tube.**
`SLOT_TICKS` and `INDI_BRIGHT` being separate knobs is what makes this testable.
 
### The sweep that will actually produce the number
 
Give the sweep enough range to reach the answer. `SLOT 52` tops out at 832 µs of dead time
even at `INDI_BRIGHT 1` — probably not enough.
 
**Set `SLOT_TICKS 208`, `NUM_INDI 6`, `DEAD_TICKS 18`, effects off.** Frame rate is 50 Hz and
*will* flicker — ignore it, this is a measurement, not a candidate build. Then sweep
`INDI_BRIGHT` and record the first clean step:
 
| `INDI_BRIGHT` | Dead time |
|---|---|
| 190 | 288 µs (matches the current branch — expect the same ghosting) |
| 170 | 608 µs |
| 150 | 928 µs |
| 120 | 1408 µs |
| 90 | 1888 µs |
| 60 | 2368 µs |
| 26 | 2912 µs (≈ the old "clean" config) |
 
**The first clean step × 16 µs is the tail.** Then the arithmetic decides everything: at six
tubes, max frame rate ≈ 1/(6 × (tail + usable on-time)). If that lands under ~100 Hz, firmware
alone cannot deliver a camera-clean six-tube display on this hardware and the driver study's
hardware options become the path — with the deionization-vs-opto discriminator above deciding
*which* option.
 
### THE TAIL IS MEASURED — 05.09.26. **288 µs < tail ≤ 608 µs.**
 
Sweep run at `SLOT_TICKS 208`, `NUM_INDI 6`, effects off, 16 µs/tick:
 
| `INDI_BRIGHT` | Dead time | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 190 | 288 µs | faint ghosting, visible only when looked for |
| **170** | **608 µs** | **clean** |
 
**Design to 608 µs.** The true value is somewhere in between; one more step at `INDI_BRIGHT
180` (448 µs) would narrow it and is worth a single flash, because every µs shaved off the
tail buys frame rate directly (see the formula below).
 
Also confirmed at this setting: **50 Hz bands on camera at both 30 fps and 60 fps** but is not
visible in peripheral vision — consistent with everything the prescaler rewrite was written
to fix. And the display at 170 looks bright, with unlit cathodes properly dark.
 
### The governing formula, and it is simple
 
At `NUM_INDI` tubes, dead time D and frame rate F, per-tube duty over the whole frame is:
 
> **duty = 1/N − D × F**
 
For six tubes: **duty = 0.1667 − D × F**. Checks against measured points: at D = 608 µs and
F = 50 Hz → 13.6 %, which is exactly `170/(208×6)`. Confirmed.
 
Two things fall straight out of it:
 
**A hard ceiling.** duty hits zero at **F_max = 1/(N × D) = 1/(6 × 608 µs) = 274 Hz.** Above
that, six tubes at this tail are physically black. **400 Hz was never reachable** — not "hard",
not "dim", *impossible*. That retires the 400 Hz target permanently, whatever else happens.
 
**The whole trade, in one table** (six tubes, D = 608 µs):
 
| Frame rate | `SLOT_TICKS` | `INDI_BRIGHT` | Per-tube duty | vs current bench (13.6 %) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 Hz *(now)* | 208 | 170 | 13.6 % | — *(bands on camera)* |
| 100 Hz | 104 | 66 | 10.6 % | 78 % |
| 120 Hz | 87 | 49 | 9.4 % | 69 % |
| 137 Hz | 76 | 38 | 8.3 % | 61 % |
| 168 Hz | 62 | 24 | 6.5 % | 48 % |
| 200 Hz | 52 | 14 | 4.5 % | 33 % |
 
### The one test left: what is the lowest camera-clean frame rate?
 
Everything now reduces to a single question. **50 Hz bands. 200 Hz was reported flicker-free
by eye but has never been filmed.** The lowest F that films clean at 60 fps sets the duty, and
the duty decides whether this ships as a firmware-only fix.
 
Sweep `SLOT_TICKS` down the table above, **filming each at 30 fps and 60 fps**. Keep
`DEAD_TICKS 38` throughout — the clamp then pins brightness to `SLOT_TICKS − 38` automatically,
so `SLOT_TICKS` is the only knob that moves and the 608 µs margin is held constant by
construction.
 
- **Lands at 100–137 Hz → firmware-only fix, done.** 8–11 % duty is 60–78 % of what is on the
  bench right now and looking good; the shortfall is recoverable on P504.
- **Needs 168 Hz or more → hardware.** Below ~7 % duty the display is too dim to ship, and the
  only way to buy duty back is to shrink D.
### What the hardware options are now worth, in units
 
The formula prices them exactly. Cutting D from 608 µs to 200 µs moves 200 Hz from **4.5 % to
12.7 %** duty and lifts the ceiling from 274 Hz to 833 Hz. **That is what a bleed resistor is
buying** — not a vague "improvement", a specific number. The deionization-vs-opto
discriminator in the previous entry decides which fix delivers it; at 608 µs, deionization
remains the more likely culprit on magnitude alone.
 
### 05.09.26 (cont.) — tail narrowed, **100 Hz confirmed camera-clean**, and a fork in the road
 
**Tail bracket narrowed.** `INDI_BRIGHT 180` at `SLOT 208` (dead = 28 ticks = **448 µs**) was
*not* as clean as 170. So: **448 µs < tail ≤ 608 µs.** Design figure stays 608 µs.
 
**The big result: `SLOT_TICKS 104` → 100 Hz eliminates the banding at BOTH 30 fps and 60 fps.**
The camera criterion — the thing the entire prescaler rewrite existed to satisfy, and the
reason 400 Hz was ever targeted — **is met at 100 Hz.** The requirement was never 400 Hz; it
was never even measured before now. Combined with the 274 Hz hard ceiling from the previous
entry, the viable window is roughly **100–200 Hz**, and the bottom of that window is the
brightest point in it.
 
**But ghosting returned at `SLOT 104`.** There are exactly two explanations and they lead to
opposite conclusions:
 
**(A) `DEAD_TICKS` is still 18, not 38.** The clamp is `min(INDI_BRIGHT, SLOT_TICKS − DEAD_TICKS)`:
 
| `DEAD_TICKS` | `curDimm` at SLOT 104 | Actual dead time |
|---|---|---|
| 18 (the branch default) | min(·, 86) = 86 | 18 ticks = **288 µs** |
| 38 (what the sweep requires) | min(·, 66) = 66 | 38 ticks = **608 µs** |
 
288 µs is *precisely* the "faint ghosting, visible if you look for it" level measured at
`INDI_BRIGHT 190`. **This explanation predicts the observation exactly**, and it is the more
likely of the two: `DEAD_TICKS 38` was only specified in the instruction that came *after* the
branch was built with 18.
 
**(B) The required dead time grows with frame rate.** If the dead time genuinely was 608 µs
and it still ghosted, then dead time alone does not determine the ghost — and the variable
that changed is the **off-period between visits to the same tube** (≈17 ms at 50 Hz, ≈8 ms at
100 Hz). Residual ionisation that has not fully decayed leaves the tube primed to conduct on
the next cycle. **That is the deionization signature**, and it is the discriminator proposed
in the previous entry, run by accident.
 
**The check that separates them: read back `DEAD_TICKS`.** Then re-flash `SLOT_TICKS 104`,
`DEAD_TICKS 38`, `INDI_BRIGHT 66`.
 
- **Clean → (A), and P2 is solved in firmware.** 100 Hz, camera-clean at 30 and 60 fps,
  duty = 1/6 − 608 µs × 100 Hz = **10.6 %** — 78 % of the current bench brightness, with P504
  available for the remainder. No bleed resistors, no PCB change, `SLOT_TICKS 104` /
  `DEAD_TICKS 38` becomes the shipped configuration.
- **Still ghosting → (B), and it is hardware.** Deionization confirmed, required dead time
  scales with refresh, and no firmware setting escapes it. The driver study's bleed-resistor
  path becomes the answer, priced by the same formula: cutting D to 200 µs takes 100 Hz duty
  from 10.6 % to **14.7 %** and restores headroom to 200 Hz.
**Either way the 400 Hz target is retired and the frame-rate requirement is now a measured
100 Hz rather than an assumed 400.** That single correction makes every downstream number in
the anode-driver study easier.
 
### VERDICT — 05.09.26: **(B) confirmed. It is hardware. The bleed resistor is the fix.**
 
`SLOT_TICKS 104`, `DEAD_TICKS 38`, `INDI_BRIGHT 66` → **no flicker at 30/60 fps, noticeable
ghosting.** Dead time was held at 608 µs — the exact value that was clean at 50 Hz — and only
the frame rate changed. **Required dead time is therefore not a constant. It scales with
refresh rate, which no firmware setting can escape.**
 
The two test points isolate it cleanly:
 
| | `SLOT 208` | `SLOT 104` |
|---|---|---|
| Dead time | 608 µs | 608 µs *(held)* |
| On-time | 2720 µs | 1056 µs *(went **down**)* |
| Frame | 20 ms | 10 ms |
| **Off-period per tube** | **17.3 ms** | **8.9 ms** |
| Result | clean | ghosts |
 
On-time *decreased*, so ionisation accumulated during conduction is not the driver. **The only
quantity that moved the wrong way is the off-period** — the time each tube has to recover
before it is revisited.
 
### The mechanism, finally stated correctly
 
**The ghost was never happening in the dead window.** It happens during the *rest of the
frame*, every time another tube's cathode is driven. The cathodes are bussed: when tube B is
showing "9", the К155ИД1 pulls the shared "9" line low for *every* tube. If tube A's anode
still holds charge, tube A's "9" glows faintly too.
 
And tube A's anode node **has no discharge path**. The TLP627 can only source, never sink; the
stock board has no anode bleed. So after the opto turns off, that node floats, draining only
through opto leakage and the tube itself — a time constant in the hundreds of µs to
milliseconds. Dead time never fixed this. It only *delayed* the next cathode change, which is
why more of it helped and none of it cured.
 
**This also retires the TLP627 storage-time theory as the dominant term.** The 04.09.26
refinement was wrong about the mechanism, though right that it was not simple capacitance.
The dominant term is a **floating, undrained anode node**.
 
### Why this makes the bleed resistor exactly right
 
A 1 MΩ bleed to ground gives that node the discharge path it has never had. With ~30 pF of
anode-node capacitance, τ ≈ **30 µs** — the node collapses in ~100 µs instead of lingering for
milliseconds. It is the correct fix, it is the part already specced in §4, and it costs ~34 mW
per tube.
 
**Reversal, stated plainly:** on 05.09.26 this document said "don't solder, firmware may fix
it." Firmware has now been proven not to fix it. Soldering is the next step.
 
### Next: one resistor, one tube, tonight
 
This is a **diagnostic, not a production commitment** — which is why flying leads are
acceptable here and not in a shipped unit.
 
1. Flash `SLOT_TICKS 104` / `DEAD_TICKS 38` / `INDI_BRIGHT 66` — the config where ghosting is
   currently visible on all four tubes.
2. **Bleed the 4.7 µF reservoir cap through ~100 kΩ for ~10 s and verify <10 V on a meter.**
   One hand in your pocket.
3. Confirm the anode pin on one tube's daughterboard: continuity between the same-position pin
   on **two different** tube sockets. **No continuity → anode.** Continuity → shared cathode,
   wrong pin.
4. Fit **one 1 MΩ 0.5 W** from that anode pin to confirmed ground (continuity-check the ground
   point against the Nano's GND pin first). Reflow the existing joint and slide the lead in
   alongside — no trace cutting.
5. **Compare that tube against its three neighbours in the same frame.** That is the whole
   test — same firmware, same rail, same instant, one variable.
**If that tube goes clean and the others do not: confirmed.** Fit the rest, then **re-sweep
frame rate** — with the node drained, the 274 Hz ceiling and the whole duty table are
recomputed from a much smaller D, and 200 Hz at ~12.7 % duty comes back into range.
 
If it does not help, try **470 kΩ** (faster decay, 2.3 mA extra rail load across six tubes —
lands on P3) before concluding.
 
### Tap-point plan, 06.09.26 — proposed route and the two things that must be metered first
 
Proposal from the bench: solder the backlight LEDs first so their legs stick out of the pads,
then run a 1 MΩ from each tube's **pin 7** to the **negative LED leg** below it. Work done on
the back; the annotated image is the front-side render.
 
**Electrically sound — conditional on two measurements, both taken with the board dead and the
reservoir cap bled to <10 V.**
 
**Check 1 — is pin 7 actually the anode?** Continuity between **pin 7 of tube 1 and pin 7 of
tube 2**. **No continuity → anode** (unique per tube). **Continuity → shared cathode; stop.**
Fitting 1 MΩ from a bussed cathode to ground would put a permanent leak across the К155ИД1's
output for that digit on every tube at once.
 
**Check 2 — is the LED negative pad actually ground? This is the one that can cost hardware.**
The backlight is **D11 PWM** (spec §2 pin map). If the LEDs are wired anode-to-+5 V with their
cathodes commoned down to D11, then **the "negative" pad is the PWM switching node, not
ground** — and a 1 MΩ from a 185 V anode to it pushes **~185 µA into an Arduino pin**, through
the clamp diodes whenever D11 is not sinking. It would also make the bleed's effectiveness
vary with backlight brightness, which would look like an intermittent ghosting bug.
 
Meter it: **continuity / near-0 Ω between the LED negative pad and the Nano's GND pin.**
Near-0 Ω → real ground, the plan is good. **Open, or reading through a resistor → it is the
D11 node. Do not use it.** Fall back to the **GND pin on the 2-pin GND/5 V header** (top right
of the board) or a Nano GND pin.
 
**Practical: put the resistor body at the anode end and run the wire from its far leg to
ground.** Only a few mm then sit at 185 V; the long run is at ~0 V. Reverse that and there is a
185 V wire flapping next to glass.
 
**And fit ONE, not four.** Tube 1 gets the resistor; tubes 2–4 are the control in the same
frame. Four at once cannot distinguish "the fix works" from "the tap point was wrong."
 
### GERBER ANALYSIS, 06.09.26 — board architecture resolved, and the LED-pad ground is NOT confirmed
 
Gerbers read directly from the bench machine (`PCB/IN-12_norm/`, EasyEDA v6.3.53, 2020-06-13,
board 99.05 × 80.77 mm) and rendered/net-analysed. Findings:
 
**1. The architecture discrepancy is resolved — both observations were right.** The board ships
from China as **one panel** and is **cut along the white line** into two boards that then mate
via **P1↔P2 and P3↔P4**. The render shows the uncut panel; the photos show the post-cut tube
board. **TS06-TUBE's rationale survives intact**: the tube board is still a bought board that
cannot take added bleed footprints, and its tube pitch/outline are still fixed by someone
else's layout. Spec §3b stands.
 
**2. The top copper layer contains NO traces at all** — 142 pad flashes and a handful of stubs,
nothing else. **Every net on this board is routed on the bottom layer.** This almost certainly
explains the bench observation that the LED negative "has no traces going anywhere": inspecting
from the component side shows no traces *for any net*. Front-side inspection carries no
information on this board.
 
**3. There is no ground pour or plane anywhere, on either layer.** All copper is discrete
traces. **A pad is therefore never grounded by sitting in a pour** — it must have a real trace.
The intuition "no trace visible, so it must be the shared ground plane" is exactly inverted
here.
 
**4. The LED "−" (square) pads DO each have a trace, and they daisy-chain to each other** along
the bottom edge, with the centre LED position acting as a junction carrying five traces. So it
is a genuine shared net, not a floating pad. **The "+" and "−" pads are on separate nets**
(not shorted).
 
**5. What could NOT be established: whether that shared LED-negative net is GND or the D11
backlight PWM node.** 69 of the board's copper regions are large filled polygons carrying real
routing, which the net extraction could not model, so any claim about where the net terminates
would be unsound. **Unresolved by analysis — and it matters**: if it is the D11 side, a 1 MΩ
from a 185 V anode injects ~185 µA into an Arduino pin and the bleed's strength varies with
backlight brightness.
 
### Consequence: bus the resistors instead of trusting the LED pad
 
The question disappears if the bleed never touches that net. **Recommended:**
 
1. Fit each resistor with its **body at the tube's pin 7**, cold leg outward.
2. **Bus all the cold legs together** with a bare wire run along the tube board — this is the
   "bleed bus" from the driver study, built in wire instead of copper.
3. Run **one** wire from that bus to a **metered, known-good ground** on the main board — the
   GND pin of the 2-pin GND/5 V header, or a Nano GND pin.
One ground decision instead of four, no dependence on an unverified net, and the long run sits
at ~0 V while only the few mm at each anode are at 185 V. If the LED-negative pad *is* later
metered as true GND (continuity, near-0 Ω, to Nano GND), it becomes a convenience, not a
dependency.
 
### BLEED RESISTOR FITTED — 06.09.26: **1 MΩ produced no meaningful improvement.**
 
Resistors soldered to the anodes, connections confirmed, no firmware change. **Ghosting
essentially unchanged.** That result is informative: it falsifies the mechanism proposed in
the 05.09.26 verdict.
 
**Why the prediction failed.** The τ ≈ 30 µs figure assumed a *floating* anode node with
nothing feeding it — under that assumption 1 MΩ collapses the node in ~100 µs and the ghost
must vanish. It did not vanish, therefore **the node is not floating: something is actively
holding it up.**
 
The arithmetic says how much. For a tube to glow faintly its anode must sit near the
sustaining voltage (~120–140 V for ИН-12). Holding 140 V across a 1 MΩ bleed requires a
sustained **~140 µA** flowing in from somewhere. The only available source is the
**TLP627's off-state leakage** — and a photo-Darlington is exactly the part that has this
problem, because the input transistor's leakage is multiplied by the output transistor's gain.
Tens to hundreds of µA at 185 V, warm, is entirely ordinary for the part.
 
**So the fault is the opto after all — but its off-state LEAKAGE, not its turn-off time.**
Every previous framing (storage tail, floating node, deionization) is superseded. This one
also explains the frame-rate dependence: leakage is continuous, so the longer a tube sits
unlit, the more the bleed can win against it — which is precisely why 50 Hz was clean and
100 Hz was not, at identical dead time.
 
### Three things to do, in order
 
**1. Confirm the bleed is actually in circuit — the classic trap.** "There is continuity" is
ambiguous here: **a 1 MΩ resistor will NOT beep on a continuity tester** (those trigger below
~50 Ω). Measure in **resistance mode, from the tube's pin 7 to ground, expecting ~1 MΩ**. Open
circuit means the ground end never landed; a beep means something is shorted. Ten seconds, and
it rules out a false negative on the whole experiment.
 
**2. Measure the HV rail — P3 has still never been done.** If P504 is set well above 185 V,
everything conducts more readily and ghosting gets worse for free. **Then try turning P504
DOWN** and watch the ghost. This reverses the earlier standing caution against touching it:
lowering is safe (the 300 V TLP627 ceiling is only violated going up) and it is a free lever.
If ghosting falls as the rail falls, the ghost is confirmed as marginal conduction.
 
**3. Drop the bleed value on ONE tube and compare against its neighbours.** This directly tests
the leakage hypothesis and sizes it: required R < V_sustain / I_leak.
 
| Value | Current @185 V | Power | 6-tube rail load |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 MΩ *(fitted, failed)* | 185 µA | 34 mW | 1.1 mA |
| **470 kΩ** | 394 µA | 73 mW | 2.4 mA |
| **220 kΩ** | 841 µA | 156 mW | 5.0 mA |
| 100 kΩ | 1.85 mA | 342 mW — **needs 0.5 W** | 11 mA — likely overloads the boost stage |
 
If 1 MΩ leaves the anode at ~140 V, leakage is ≳140 µA and **470 kΩ should bring it to ~75 V —
below sustaining.** This is exactly the "treat the bleed value as a knob, not a fixed 1 MΩ"
line already in the driver study (T2.2); the ИН-17 figure was never a design value for this
board.
 
**If 220 kΩ still does not clear it**, the leakage is too large to shunt at acceptable rail
cost, and the answer becomes **replacing the anode driver stage** — study option T3.2, a fast
opto level-shifting an **MPSA92** (300 V PNP). Its off-state leakage is nanoamps against the
Darlington's microamps, roughly a thousandfold better, at which point a 1 MΩ bleed works
easily. That reinstates T3.2 as the leading hardware path, now on evidence rather than
speculation.
 
### 06.09.26 — bleed measures **0.70 MΩ, not 1 MΩ**: a pre-existing parallel path
 
Pin 7 to ground reads **700 kΩ** with a 1 MΩ resistor fitted. That implies **~2.3 MΩ already
in parallel** (1/0.7 = 1/1 + 1/2.33), i.e. the anode node was **never fully floating even on
the stock board** — some path to ground already existed. Candidate: back through the off opto
to the HV rail and down the boost stage's feedback divider (the board carries a 470 kΩ fixed
resistor plus the 500 kΩ P504 trimmer between HV+ and ground).
 
**Two consequences, and the important one is not the mystery:**
 
1. **The fitted bleed is working ~30 % HARDER than specified and still failed.** The
   leakage-too-large conclusion is therefore strengthened, not weakened. Go lower, faster:
   470 kΩ (∥2.3 M ≈ 390 k effective), then 220 kΩ (≈ 200 k effective).
2. Diagnostic worth ten seconds: **reverse the meter leads on that 700 kΩ reading.** If the
   value changes, there is a semiconductor junction in the parallel path — confirming it runs
   through the opto rather than through passive board resistance.
### The measurement that now decides everything: anode voltage under power
 
Modelling has been wrong three times (storage tail → floating node → deionization → leakage).
Stop inferring and read the node directly. **With the board running and a tube dark but
ghosting, measure DC volts from that tube's pin 7 to ground.**
 
- **~120–140 V** → confirmed: leakage is holding the anode at sustaining voltage. The bleed
  value is the right lever; keep dropping it, and T3.2 (MPSA92, nanoamp leakage) is the
  endgame if the rail cost gets unaffordable.
- **~20–40 V** → the ghost is **not** anode-voltage-driven, every model in this document is
  wrong, and the investigation has to move to the cathode side (К155ИД1 unselected-output
  behaviour).
A 10 MΩ DMM across a 700 kΩ node loads it negligibly, so the reading is trustworthy.
 
**Procedure — 185 V, do it in this order:** power OFF, **clip** the black lead to ground, then
power on, then touch only the red probe to pin 7. Never hold both leads. Meter on a 600 V DC
range before it touches anything.
 
## ACTUAL ROOT CAUSE FOUND — 06.09.26: **empty multiplex slots.** Not the optos, not the tubes.
 
Found by the Claude Code session, confirmed by a direct A/B (`MUX_SLOTS 4` clears it, 6 does
not). **This supersedes every mechanism proposed in this document** — storage tail, floating
node, deionization, opto leakage. All four were wrong.
 
**The mechanism.** In the fork's ISR the decoder is written inside the changeover guard,
together with the anode turn-on:
 
```c
if (d && anodeStates[i]) {
    PORTC = (PORTC & 0xF0) | decoderNibble[dig];   // digit onto the shared cathode bus
    *curPort |= curMaskOn;                          // and this slot's anode on
}
```
 
Stock AlexGyver rotates through exactly as many slots as it has tubes, so **a digit is never
on the cathode bus without a driven anode to take the current.** This fork rotates through
six. With no SEC module fitted, **slots 4 and 5 have no tube and no anode driver** — yet
`anodeStates[]` initialises to all-1s and `indiDigits[]` holds real seconds values, so the
guard passes and a digit is written to the bus for 21 of every 26 ticks with **no anode
sourcing current anywhere on the board.**
 
Any charge still decaying on the four real tubes' anodes then has that cathode as its only
available path, and lights it. **Stock cannot produce this condition — it has no empty slots.**
 
**This explains everything the other theories only partly covered:** why stock at
`INDI_BRIGHT 24` is flawless on the same hardware; why dead time helped but never cured (the
offending digit is driven in a slot with no anode of its own to blank); why it worsened with
frame rate (more visits per second to the empty slots); and why the 1 MΩ bleed barely moved it.
 
### The implication that matters: this is an artifact of MISSING HARDWARE
 
**The ghosting on the bench is not a property of the finished instrument.** Fit the two ИН-17s
and their two TLP627 anode drivers (D2/D13, on TS06-SEC) and slots 4 and 5 stop being empty —
every slot then has a real tube and a real anode, exactly like stock.
 
**Two consequences:**
 
1. **`MUX_SLOTS 4` is a bench workaround for absent hardware, not a shipped setting.** It must
   go back to 6 when the SEC module exists. Keep the constant and its comment block — it is
   the correct diagnostic for exactly this condition.
2. **The 06.09.26 decision to drop the camera-clean refresh rate was made under a wrong
   picture and should be REOPENED once the SEC module is fitted.** If empty slots were the
   dominant ghost source all along, a much higher refresh may be clean with all six tubes
   present. **Not a promise** — this document's predictive record is poor — but the trade that
   forced the concession may not exist. Re-run the refresh sweep before accepting flicker.
### Proper firmware fix, better than the MUX_SLOTS hack
 
The ISR should never leave a digit on the cathode bus without a driven anode. When the
changeover guard fails it currently leaves **the previous slot's digit** on `PORTC`. It should
instead write a blanking code (К155ИД1 inputs 10–15 select no cathode):
 
```c
} else {
    PORTC = (PORTC & 0xF0) | decoderNibble[10];   // no anode this slot -> blank the bus
}
```
 
With that, the rotation is safe at any `NUM_INDI` regardless of which slots are populated, and
partially-built boards stop producing phantom faults. Worth doing regardless of the ИН-17s.
 
### Open discrepancy to watch
 
The 05.09.26 sweep reported `SLOT 208 / INDI_BRIGHT 170` (608 µs dead, 50 Hz, six slots, four
tubes) as **clean**, which the empty-slot mechanism says should still have ghosted. Most likely
explanation: it was "clean enough" without a perfect reference to compare against, and
`MUX_SLOTS 4` has since provided one — a genuinely clean reference changes judgement. Worth a
direct A/B against the old setting rather than assuming.

## P3 — HV headroom, *before* adding load

tubes plus a two-lamp colon plus two ИН-15 tubes (18 cathodes between them) is a further
increase in rail load on top of what the Rev C plan already flagged — this reading is the
baseline that says whether the stock boost stage holds up as load is added, or needs the
feedback divider touched (the EMBER-06 study hit exactly this question at 200 V).
 
**Safety: the reservoir cap stays charged after power-off. Bleed it before touching the
tube side. One hand in your pocket.**
 
## P4 — SEC module on perfboard, one ИН-17 (the real work)
 
Two opto anode channels on D2 and D13 exactly as the main board drives D3–D6, one ИН-17 on
flying leads, cathodes bussed to the existing К155ИД1 outputs.
 
Produces the four numbers TS06-SEC cannot be laid out without:
 
1. **J-MAP — the digit→pin table.** Sweep 0–9 with `secCathodeMap` (its own standalone
   sketch in the repo's `firmware/secCathodeMap/` folder — it has its own `setup()`/
   `loop()` and must stay out of the main sketch's folder or compilation collides on
   duplicate definitions), write down which physical lead lights which numeral. *Never
   from a datasheet — surplus markings lie.* This is the number that, if wrong, is
   invisible until HV is on and costs a board respin.
2. **Anode resistor.** Start 12 kΩ, compare against an ИН-12 at 2 m: 15 k soft / 10 k
   bright / 8.2 k floor. Pick the value that puts ИН-17 within one perceptual step of ИН-12.
3. **Ghost check.** Fit the 1 MΩ 0.5 W anode bleed, display `88:8888`, look for a faint
   glow on the dark tube. Confirm the bleed actually kills it before it is committed to
   copper. **01.09.26: given P2's finding, treat this step as validating the same fix that
   the main board may also need — don't assume the SEC module's ghosting is a separate
   problem from P2's, it's very likely the same root cause on a second board.**
4. **Connector count.** Spec §4 now specifies XS3 as an **8-pin** JST-XH (GND, +5 V, BACKL,
   DOT, SEC_A0, SEC_A1, SDA, SCL — the Rev D SDA/SCL pair replaced the Rev C.3 AMPM_SEL
   wire); `terminal-06.html` is stated in spec Rev D to have been rewritten to match in the
   same pass — worth a quick visual confirmation on the bench rather than taking that on
   faith, since this exact discrepancy has already been wrong twice before.
Cross-check the **19.30 mm / 17.50 mm** ИН-17 envelope readings against the SEC board's
23 mm tube-centre spacing (spec §4) before finalising the layout — the single-sample
readings already in hand are sufficient for this.

## P6 — colon
 
Pair the two ИНС-1s on a bench rig (J-NEON): same 220 kΩ, same rail, side by side, judged by
eye *and* by one phone photo at fixed ISO and shutter. Brightness mismatch between the two
dots is the most visible defect on the product, and there are enough spares to select from.
 
Then the MPSA42 low-side switch on D10, 10 kΩ base resistor, **one 220 kΩ per lamp — never
shared**.
 
**Tune `DOT_BRIGHT` — this is still an outstanding bench task, not applied guidance.**
Direct inspection of the pushed firmware source (28.08.26) confirmed `DOT_BRIGHT` /

**From the CAD measurement pass:** ИНС-1 is cap-terminated on one end, not a plain two-lead

## P6a — AM/PM + idle-cycling indicator circuit (spec Rev D, runs parallel to P4–P6)
 
**Rewritten 27.08.26 to match `claude/TERMINAL-06-spec.md` §3, which supersedes the simpler
single-expander/diode-OR circuit this plan originally described here.** The authoritative
design is not a 2-lamp AM/PM pair — it's two real Soviet multimeter symbol tubes (ИН-15Б:
A, W, F, Hz, H, V, S, Ω — 8 glyphs; ИН-15А: μ, −, +, %, n, m, M, K, Р, П — 10 glyphs), with
every one of their 18 cathodes individually switchable so the pair can idle-cycle its full
symbol set on rotary position 1, not just flip between the two glyphs ('A' and 'Р') that
functionally mean AM/PM.
 
**What this needs:**
- ИН-15А (sourced, not ordered) and **ИН-15Б (a handful now secured on Meshok, 28.08.26,
  enough for one prototype unit — ten-unit-batch depth is a separate, later sourcing task,
  not a P6a blocker anymore)**
- **Two MCP23017 I²C GPIO expanders** — U3 (address 0x20, all three address pins to GND)
  drives ИН-15Б's 8 cathodes via 8 MPSA42 transistors (VT2–VT9); U4 (address 0x21, A0 to
  +5V, A1/A2 to GND) drives ИН-15А's 10 cathodes via 10 MPSA42 transistors (VT10–VT19).
  Both ride the same A4/A5 bus as the DS3231 — zero additional Nano pins for 18 switched
  cathodes, versus the single-PCF8574/2-channel design this plan used to describe.
- **18 MPSA42 (300 V)** total for the pair, one per cathode, 10 kΩ base resistor each —
  not the 2–3 this plan previously listed. Same 300 V rule as everywhere else on the HV
  side — no 2N5551.
- **One shared anode resistor per tube (8.2 kΩ, R38/R39), not per cathode** — same
  principle as any other shared-anode digit tube, since only one cathode per tube is ever
  grounded at a time.
**Bench steps, in order:**
1. Wire both expanders onto the A4/A5 bus alongside the DS3231. Confirm all three devices
   answer at their own I²C addresses (0x68 RTC, 0x20 U3, 0x21 U4) with a simple bus-scan
   sketch before building anything else on top of it.
2. One ИН-15 tube on flying leads at a time, its own shared anode resistor (bench-determine
   the value — same process as ИН-17 in P4, no known-good starting point yet since this
   tube type wasn't in the original scope).
3. Sweep every cathode on that tube through its expander one at a time, and write down which
   physical cathode lead lights which glyph — same discipline as J-MAP for the seconds tube,
   and for the same reason: this is invisible until HV is on, and wrong here means a
   respin later.
4. Confirm the two glyphs that matter functionally — ИН-15Б's 'A' and ИН-15А's 'Р' — light
   correctly and that both tubes can show any glyph independently of each other.
5. Repeat for the second tube. Idle-cycling itself (rotary position 1 sweeping through all
   18 glyphs as an ambient animation) is a firmware behaviour, not a bench step — it comes
   later, at P8, once the expander driver exists and there's a real panel to test the
   "does cycling ever land mid-animation when someone glances at the clock" question spec §3
   raises but doesn't answer yet.
**"m" indicator (HL3) — wire the easy part now, leave the disputed part open.** Whichever
way the always-on-vs-12h-mode question resolves, HL3 is a plain 5 mm amber LED with a
220 Ω series resistor (R26) off +5V/GND, no HV, no ballast — that much is decided and can be
built either way. What's **not** decided is whether it stops there (always-on, per the
current spec text) or gets a transistor gated by a diode-OR of the two expander lines
meaning "showing A" / "showing Р" (12h-mode-conditional, per what you told this chat
earlier). Cheapest way to keep this open on the bench: wire HL3 to a jumper or small switch
instead of soldering it permanently to +5V, so either behaviour can be tried once the
expander firmware exists, without having to re-open the board to add the transistor later
if the conditional answer wins. **Decide this before it goes into any board for real** —
see the Status section above.

## Tools
 
Already owned: Vogel digital calipers, soldering iron, Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro, Fusion 360.
CAD library work (see `TERMINAL-06-cad-component-library.md`) is standardising on FreeCAD
1.0+ / KiCad / OpenSCAD / Blender / Inkscape instead for its own goal (a reusable parametric
part library) — worth reconciling with Fusion 360 eventually, but not something this
prototype plan needs to track closely.
 
Needed or worth confirming:
 
- **Multimeter with a ≥300 V DC range and probes rated for it.** Non-negotiable for P3/P5.
- **A bleed resistor on a stick** (~100 kΩ 2 W on insulated leads) to discharge the
  reservoir cap. Build it before P3, not after the first surprise.
- **JST-XH crimp tool** — soldered pigtails into XH housings fail on vibration.
- **Contact cleaner** (ДЕОКСИТ-class or equivalent) — refurb step for switchgear, now
  including the rotary itself since it's in hand — worth a look regardless of it being
  new stock.
- **Phone that can shoot 60 fps with locked ISO/shutter** — the banding test in P2/P5 and
  the neon pairing in P6 both depend on a *fixed* exposure, not auto.
- Optional: any oscilloscope for the 401 Hz multiplex, the 31 kHz boost node, and the I²C
  bus once both MCP23017s are on it. **01.09.26: this stopped being purely optional for P2**
  — if a scope is available, it would directly confirm the anode-quench hypothesis by
  showing how long a tube's anode/cathode voltage actually takes to decay after switch-off,
  rather than inferring it from bracketing firmware settings.
