# EMBER-06 — six-digit nixie clock, exposed glass on a copper deck (Rev A)
 
The second model in the shop, not the second version of the first one. Where
TERMINAL-06 sinks its tubes into a trench behind a brow, EMBER-06 stands them on a
bare-metal deck and lets the glass be the object. 4× IN-14 for HH:MM, 2× IN-16 for
seconds, 4 neon lamps forming two colons, ice-blue underglow.
 
Read §9 before writing any listing copy. The whole point of this model is that it is a
peer to TERMINAL-06 at the same price, chosen on taste — not an upgrade.
 
Firmware: fork of the TERMINAL-06 tree, six substantive lines changed.
 
---
 
## 1. Design thesis
 
One sentence, and every other decision falls out of it:
 
> **TERMINAL-06 hides the tubes inside an instrument. EMBER-06 puts them on a plinth.**
 
Consequences that follow, and that you should not fight later:
 
- No brow, no trench, no rake. A low horizontal body with 55 mm of glass standing above it.
- The accent is a real metal finish, not a printed colour. **The safety-orange brow is
  TERMINAL-06's mark and must never appear on this model.**
- Round side-view glass means the customer sees the unlit cathode stack behind the lit
  digit, all the time, from every angle. That is a feature here. It was something the
  IN-12 trench deliberately hid.
- Exposed tubes collect dust and can be knocked. Accept it, say so in the listing, and
  ship a microfibre cloth.

## 2. Tubes
 
Verified against original datasheets, not from memory — surplus tube listings routinely
quote the wrong figures.
 
| | IN-14 (ИН-14) | IN-16 (ИН-16) |
|---|---|---|
| Role | HH:MM, 4 off | seconds, 2 off |
| Digit height | 18 mm | 13 mm |
| Envelope Ø | 19 mm | 13 mm |
| Overall height | 55 mm | 45 mm |
| Base | 13 wire leads, 35 mm | 13 wire leads |
| Anode supply, nominal | 170 V | 170 V |
| Maintaining drop | **145 V** | **140 V** |
| Cathode current, nominal | 2.5 mA | 2.0 mA |
| Rated life | ~5 000 h | min 5 000 h |
 
**The 5 V gap is the reason this model is buildable.** TERMINAL-06 mixes IN-12 (≈140 V)
with IN-17 (≈105 V) — 35 V apart, which is why it needs a separate anode resistor bank
and a firmware trim to stop the small tubes hogging current. IN-14 and IN-16 sit 5 V
apart. They are near-electrical twins. The seconds tubes still get their own resistors,
but the values land one or two E12 steps apart instead of a different order of magnitude,
and the perceptual match falls out almost for free.
 
Both tube types are wire-ended. There are no pins and no sockets anywhere in this design
— see §7 for why that is a deliberate trade-off and not an oversight.
 
**Neon colons.** INS-1 / IN-3 class miniature neon lamps, four off, in two vertical pairs.
A single neon run on DC glows at one electrode only, so a one-lamp "colon" is really one
dot. Use two lamps per colon position, stacked, inside a printed diffuser sleeve. They
are pennies each and the result is deterministic. Measure the strike voltage of your
actual batch — surplus neons vary more than the datasheet suggests.

## 3. Electrical
 
### 3.1 Run the rail at 200 V, not 185 V
 
This is the one genuine departure from the TERMINAL-06 electrical design, and it is
forced by the tubes.
 
At a 185 V rail an IN-14 dropping 145 V, plus ~3 V across the opto and the K155ID1, leaves
only **37 V** across the anode resistor. TERMINAL-06 had 79 V. Halving the headroom
doubles the sensitivity of tube current to rail droop: a 10 V sag moves IN-14 current by
27 % here versus 13 % there, and you will see it as the display breathing when the
multiplex duty changes.
 
At 200 V there is 52 V of headroom and the behaviour is calm. The stock boost circuit has
the margin for it — IRF840 is a 500 V part and the reservoir cap is 4.7 µF / 350 V.
 
> **Verify before you trust it:** the feedback divider on the stock board is trimmed for a
> set point near 180 V. Confirm on the bench that the regulation loop actually reaches and
> holds 200 V, and that the ADC feedback node stays under 5 V, before you commit ten
> boards. One resistor may need changing. Do not assume.
 
### 3.2 Anode resistors, at a 200 V rail
 
Assumes 3.0 V total across the TLP627 and the K155ID1 output, and 1/6 duty across six
tubes.
 
**IN-14 — 52 V across R**
 
| R | peak | avg | peak power | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 k | 5.20 mA | 0.87 mA | 0.27 W | soft |
| **8.2 k** | **6.34 mA** | **1.06 mA** | **0.33 W** | **start here** |
| 6.8 k | 7.65 mA | 1.27 mA | 0.40 W | bright |
| 5.6 k | 9.29 mA | 1.55 mA | 0.48 W | floor — don't go lower |
 
**IN-16 — 57 V across R**
 
| R | peak | avg | peak power | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 k | 4.38 mA | 0.73 mA | 0.25 W | soft |
| **11 k** | **5.18 mA** | **0.86 mA** | **0.30 W** | **start here** |
| 8.2 k | 6.95 mA | 1.16 mA | 0.40 W | bright |
| 6.8 k | 8.38 mA | 1.40 mA | 0.48 W | floor — IN-16 is a 2 mA tube |
 
Use 0.5 W parts throughout. Average dissipation is under 60 mW, but the peak matters for
thermal cycling and the physical size looks right on the deck underside.
 
The 8.2 k / 11 k pairing puts IN-16 at roughly 81 % of the IN-14 current, which tracks
the 2.0 mA / 2.5 mA nominal ratio almost exactly. Expect to need little or no firmware
trim — but keep `SEC_BRIGHT_TRIM` in the code anyway, because tube batches vary and it is
free.
 
### 3.3 Neon drive
 
Four lamps, one low-side switch, gated from D10.
 
- MPSA42 (300 V NPN), base through 10 kΩ from D10, emitter to HV ground.
- Each lamp gets its own **330 kΩ** series resistor to the 200 V rail: ~0.44 mA per lamp,
  ~1.76 mA total added rail load. Negligible against six tubes at ~1 mA average each.
- The full rail appears across the lamp until it strikes, so ignition is never marginal.
### 3.4 Underglow — one colour, and here is why
 
The reference look is ice-blue light pooling under each tube. The obvious implementation
is addressable LEDs. **Do not use them.**
 
Bit-banging WS2812B blocks interrupts for roughly 30 µs per LED. Eight LEDs is ~240 µs of
blackout. The multiplex frame is 2.49 ms at 401 Hz, so a single tube slot is 415 µs — the
LED update would swallow more than half of one tube's illuminated time, every refresh.
The result is visible flicker and a bright-dark stripe across the row. There is no clever
scheduling fix that survives a 60 fps listing video.
 
Use plain single-colour LEDs on the existing D11 PWM rail, exactly as TERMINAL-06 does
with amber. One channel, no timing conflict, no new pins — and there are no new pins
available (see §3.5).
 
> **Blue-specific caveat.** Blue LEDs are less well-behaved than amber at low duty: the
> perceived hue shifts toward violet and low-frequency flicker becomes visible sooner.
> Clamp the PWM floor at ~15 % rather than allowing zero-to-full, and diffuse hard through
> a printed collar. A blue backlight that strobes at the dim setting will be the first
> thing a reviewer mentions.
 
### 3.5 Pin map — unchanged from TERMINAL-06
 
Six tubes, same multiplex, same allocation. This is the single biggest reason EMBER-06 is
a three-week project and not a three-month one.
 
```
D2   anode, seconds TENS        D3..D6 anodes H H M M
D7   button SET                 D8  button ADJ
D9   HV boost oscillator        D10 neon colons (4 lamps via MPSA42)
D11  underglow PWM              D12 lever SW3 PROGRAM/RUN
D13  anode, seconds UNITS
A0..A3 K155ID1 BCD              A4/A5 DS3231 I2C
```
 
Every usable pin is allocated. A6/A7 on a Nano are analogue-in only. D0/D1 are the
bootloader UART. **There is no headroom for a second backlight channel, an encoder, or a
sensor.** If a future model needs one, it needs a different main board — that lesson is
already written up in the MIMI-06 study.
 
D13 still shares the onboard LED, so the seconds-units tube flashes for about a second at
boot. Cosmetic, same as TERMINAL-06.

## 4. EM06-DECK — the whole model in one board
 
TERMINAL-06's repeatability move was TS06-FASCIA, which turned 30 minutes of hand-wiring
into a two-connector plug-in. EMBER-06's equivalent is bigger: **one board is the tube
socket, the control panel, the neon and LED carrier, and the visible top surface of the
product.**
 
**176 × 72 mm, 1.6 mm FR4, ENIG finish, no soldermask and no silkscreen on the top layer.**
 
### 4.1 Why a PCB and not a metal plate
 
A laser-cut copper sheet is the "right" answer and it is the wrong answer at a batch of
ten: $15–25 per unit, a separate supplier, a lead time, a deburring step, and you still
need a tube board underneath it. The deck PCB is ~$6, dimensionally perfect, arrives with
the holes already in it, and does four jobs at once.
 
**Be honest with yourself about the finish**, because this is the one place the model can
look cheap:
 
| Finish | Looks like | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| ENIG | warm gold, stable forever | **use this** |
| Bare copper (OSP) | true copper for ~2 weeks | then tarnishes *blotchily* from handling — not the even patina of real sheet metal |
| Immersion silver | bright, then black | no |
 
Real copper patinas evenly and beautifully. A bare-copper PCB does not — it goes uneven
around wherever a thumb touched it. ENIG gives a warm metal that photographs superbly and
never changes, and "gold-finished deck" is easier listing copy than explaining patina.
If you want true copper, order OSP and lacquer the top face yourself after assembly; add
5 min per unit and expect to redo the ones you get dust in.
 
### 4.2 Top face
 
All soldering happens on the underside. **The top face carries no solder joints, no
silkscreen, and no components** — it is a continuous metal plane with holes in it.
 
The top pour ties to logic ground. Each tube's 13 leads sit on a small bolt circle, and
at 200 V the pour must be held clear of every one of them: use a **single annular moat
3 mm wide around the whole lead field** rather than per-hole clearances, which would
merge into a ragged shape anyway. The result reads as a machined bolt circle inside a
ring, which is exactly the look this model wants.
 
> **Tracking risk.** A grounded pour 3 mm from a 200 V lead across bare FR4 is fine dry and
> is not fine after two years in a humid room. Conformal-coat the top face after assembly.
> This also locks in a bare-copper finish if you went that route — one step, two problems.
 
### 4.3 Geometry
 
Tube centres, X from the left edge of the 176 mm deck:
 
| Position | X | Tube |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | 24.5 | IN-14 |
| H2 | 48.5 | IN-14 |
| colon 1 | 66.5 | 2× neon |
| M1 | 84.5 | IN-14 |
| M2 | 108.5 | IN-14 |
| colon 2 | 126.5 | 2× neon |
| S1 | 141.5 | IN-16 |
| S2 | 158.5 | IN-16 |
 
Left margin to glass edge 15.0 mm, right margin 11.0 mm. **The asymmetry is deliberate**
— same reasoning as TERMINAL-06's 20.75 / 18.7. The seconds tubes are visually lighter, so
an even margin makes the row look like it is sliding right.
 
- Tube centreline at Y = 22 mm from the front edge.
- Control centreline at Y = 56 mm — behind the glass, reached over the top.
- Control X positions **22 / 64 / 88 / 112 / 154**, carried over from TS06-FASCIA
  unchanged. The boards are different but the drilling pattern is shared, which keeps one
  jig valid across both models.
### 4.4 Controls
 
Electrically identical to TERMINAL-06 — 2 buttons, 3 levers, time-setting gated behind
the PROG lever so a customer cannot fumble into it. Physically different: brass or
knurled-barrel buttons and a smaller lever bat, to suit the warmer object.
 
| Feature | X | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Button SET | 22 mm | ⌀12.2 |
| Lever PWR | 64 mm | ⌀13.2 + anti-rotation flat, hard cut of 12 V |
| Lever LUM | 88 mm | ⌀13.2, hard cut of LED +5 V |
| Lever PROG | 112 mm | ⌀13.2, to D12 |
| Button ADJ | 154 mm | ⌀12.2 |
 
Mounting controls through a 1.6 mm PCB rather than a printed panel means the bezel nuts
land on a flat, true surface and torque evenly. It also means **you cannot re-drill a
mistake** — check plate 04 before ordering.
 
### 4.5 Connectors
 
Three keyed connectors, deliberately, so 200 V cannot be plugged into a 5 V header at 11 pm.
 
- **J1** — 8-way JST-VH: six switched anode lines, HV+, HV GND. VH's 3.96 mm pitch is the
  minimum acceptable creepage at 200 V; do not substitute XH here.
- **J2** — 10-way IDC: cathodes K0–K9.
- **J3** — 10-pin JST-XH: GND, +5 V, BACKL, NEON, SET, ADJ, PROG, LED_RAIL_IN,
  LED_RAIL_OUT, spare.
- Plus a 2-pin inline for the PWR lever in the 12 V feed, as on TERMINAL-06.
### 4.6 Cathode routing
 
IN-16 cathode for digit N goes to the same K155ID1 output the IN-14s use for digit N, so
one `digitMask` covers all six tubes. **Measure the digit → physical lead table yourself**
with the mapping jig sketch, for both tube types separately. Do not take it from a
datasheet and do not assume IN-16 follows IN-14's order. Surplus markings are not reliable
and this mistake is invisible until HV is applied.

## 5. Enclosure
 
**176 × 72 × 38 mm plinth.** Front face vertical — no rake, because there is no display
recessed behind it to angle toward the viewer. Total object height with tubes ≈ 93 mm.
 
Against TERMINAL-06's 176 × 96 × 78 wedge this is lower, deeper, and taller overall: a
completely different silhouette on a shelf, which is the point.
 
- **Body** — GRAPHITE PETG/ASA, matte, 0.20 mm, 4 walls, 20 % gyroid, no supports.
  ~7 h. Shallower than the TERMINAL-06 chassis, so the print is faster.
- **Base plate** — black PETG, debossed insignia and serial recess.
- **Diffuser collars** — 8 off, natural translucent PETG or white PLA, 0.12 mm layers,
  printed as one plate. These sit under each tube and spread the LED. Print spares; they
  are the part you will scrap most.
- **No accent-coloured part.** The deck is the accent. Resist the urge to add one.
Rear: ⌀8 DC jack, 13 × 7 mm slot aligned to the Nano's USB port, 5-slot vent array. Same
as TERMINAL-06 — reuse the geometry.
 
Feet: four cork discs. The object is light and top-heavy with 55 mm of glass on it;
silicone feet let it rock.
 
**Identity** — palette: VOID #0A0B0C, GRAPHITE #24272B, BONE #DCD8D0, CATHODE #FF9E36,
plus EMBER-only COPPER #B87333 and ICE #8FD4E8. **SIGNAL #F25610 is reserved to
TERMINAL-06 and does not appear here.**
 
Insignia: a circle bisected by a bar that breaks the outline, numeral 06 inside. The same
construction rule as TERMINAL-06's hexagon, a different primitive — so the two read as one
workshop without either looking like a revision of the other. Original mark. **No
franchise logos, names or marks anywhere on the product, packaging, listing copy or photo
props.**

## 6. Firmware
 
Fork of the TERMINAL-06 tree. Six substantive changes, all small:
 
1. `SEC_BRIGHT_TRIM` retuned for IN-16 — expect a value near 1.0, unlike the IN-17 case.
2. Neon output on D10 drives four lamps through one transistor instead of a single dot.
   Code unchanged; only the hardware behind the pin differs.
3. **Underglow PWM floor clamped at ~15 %** — new, ~4 lines. Prevents the blue hue shift
   and low-duty flicker described in §3.4.
4. Anti-poisoning sweep extended to the neon lamps so all four age evenly.
5. Transition effects retuned. The glitch effect was designed against IN-12, where the
   unlit cathodes are hidden. On round side-view glass the whole stack is visible and the
   glitch reads as noise. Use a slower crossfade; keep the seconds tubes excluded, as
   before.
6. HV set point 185 → 200 V.
Flash unchanged at roughly 13.4 kB / 394 B RAM. Timer2 stays at prescaler 1 for the
401 Hz frame — **this is not optional**, it is what keeps the listing video free of
banding.

## 7. The socket question
 
Every tube in this design is soldered directly to the deck by its leads. TERMINAL-06's
IN-12s sit in sockets and can be swapped by the customer. This is a real difference and it
goes in the honest-trade-offs column, not the ignore-it column.
 
Turned-pin sockets were considered and rejected: 66 sockets per unit is ~$3.30 and,
worse, each one adds height variance and a degree of wobble. **Tube verticality is the
signature quality risk of this model** — six wire-ended tubes standing proud on a flat
deck, where one leaning tube ruins the whole row and is obvious in every photograph.
Sockets make that harder to control, not easier.
 
So: solder direct, control the geometry with jigs, and cover the downside with service.
Offer tube replacement at $40 plus the tube, state it plainly in the listing, and note
that because the deck is one board it is a bench job rather than a rebuild. Keep two
matched spare tubes of each type from the batch for exactly this.

## 9. Positioning — read this before writing any listing copy
 
EMBER-06 is a **sibling**, not a successor. The shop is meant to read as a workshop with a
catalogue, where a customer picks by taste and pays the same either way. Two models at one
price beat a good model and a better model, because the second framing makes the cheaper
one look like a compromise and makes anyone who already bought it feel stupid.
 
| | TERMINAL-06 | EMBER-06 |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis | tubes recessed in an instrument | tubes standing on a plinth |
| Tubes | IN-12, flat front-view | IN-14 + IN-16, round side-view |
| Digit height | 18 mm | 18 mm — *identical* |
| Silhouette | 176 × 96 × 78 wedge, 12° rake | 176 × 72 × 38 plinth + 55 mm glass |
| Accent | safety-orange printed brow | bare-metal deck |
| Backlight | amber | ice blue |
| Rail | 185 V | 200 V |
| Mood | cold, industrial, cyberpunk | warm, quiet, mid-century |
| Buyer | gamer and engineer desks, mecha, industrial decor | living rooms, warm decor, gifting |
| Price | $329 | $329 |
 
### Honest trade-offs, in both directions
 
State these plainly. They are what make the two models peers rather than a ladder.
 
**EMBER-06 gives up:** customer-replaceable tubes (soldered, not socketed); dust
protection (open deck); knock resistance (55 mm of unguarded glass); a little margin
($63/h vs $70/h).
 
**TERMINAL-06 gives up:** the visible cathode stack, which is half the reason people love
IN-14s; the metal surface; the low-and-wide silhouette; a warm palette. Its trench, which
protects the tubes, is also what hides them.
 
Neither list is a defect list. They are the reasons a given customer picks one.
 
### Rules
 
- The words **"v2", "Mk II", "improved", "upgraded", "next generation"** appear nowhere —
  not in titles, tags, descriptions, photo captions or social posts.
- Cross-link as **"also available"**, never "see also the newer model".
- Both stay at $329. If one has to move, move both.
- Both are "handmade limited run of 10".
- Do not launch in the same week — but do not launch EMBER-06 as news either. It is a
  second option that was always going to exist.
- Shoot both on the same set with the same lighting family, so the listings read as one
  catalogue. Unlike MIMI-06, EMBER-06 needs no second lighting setup: the dark set that
  flatters nixie glass also flatters a graphite body and a gold deck.

## 10. Build order (≈2 h 40 min hands-on per unit)
 
Jigs made once:
 
- **J-LEAD-14** — printed comb holding IN-14's leads at final spacing and length.
- **J-LEAD-16** — the same for IN-16. **You need both.** IN-16's leads are finer and on a
  smaller circle; one comb will not serve two tube types, and freehand forming gives ten
  units with ten different tube heights.
- **J-VERT** — a printed square that drops over a tube and registers against the deck, so
  each tube is soldered plumb rather than eyeballed. This is the jig that protects the
  signature quality risk; make it first.
- **J-MAP** — measured digit → K155ID1 lead tables for *both* tube types, taped to the bench.
Steps:
 
1. Print body / base / collars — overnight
2. Heat-set inserts — 6 min
3. Form all six tubes' leads on J-LEAD-14 and J-LEAD-16 — 25 min
4. Populate deck underside: resistors, neons, MPSA42, LEDs, connectors, controls — 25 min
5. Flash and verify logic board at 200 V — 8 min
6. Solder six tubes into the deck, each set with J-VERT — 35 min *(the long pole; do this
   first in a session, never last at night)*
7. Mate deck to logic board: HV, cathodes, logic, in that order, power off — 10 min
8. Set HV 200 ± 8 V, trim seconds brightness — 10 min
9. Mechanical assembly, **gloves on from the moment the deck leaves its bag** — 15 min
10. 24 h burn-in — 5 min attended
11. Serialise, photograph, pack — 20 min

## 11. QC — every unit
 
- HV rail 200 ± 8 V at TP1/TP2 after 5 min warm
- Full power-on cathode sweep, all 60 cathodes strike cleanly
- Ghost check: display 88:88:88, blank each tube in turn, no residual glow
- IN-16s within one perceptual step of the IN-14s at 2 m in a dim room
- All four neon lamps strike, and both colons blink together
- Underglow: sweep PWM from floor to full — no visible flicker, no violet shift at minimum
- **Tube verticality: all six within 1° of plumb, checked with a square from front and
  side.** One leaning tube fails the unit. This is the check that will actually catch things.
- **Deck cosmetics: no fingerprints, no flux wicked through to the top face, no scratches.**
  Inspect under raking light, not overhead light.
- Drift < 2 s over the 24 h burn-in
- All three levers actuate; PROGRAM exit writes the RTC; both buttons click+hold
- Film 5 s at 60 fps — any banding means the Timer2 prescaler didn't take
- Serial inked, QC card signed and dated
Build log per unit: serial, tube batch for both types, measured HV, anode resistors,
trim value, drift, build date.

## 12. Batch plan
 
- **Week 0** — build unit 1 end to end. Measure both cathode maps, confirm the boost loop
  holds 200 V, pick the two resistor values, revise the deck, re-order. Do not start the
  batch before unit 1 has run 24 h.
- **Week 1** — order everything; printer runs nightly.
- **Week 2** — build in stations, ten of each operation at a time.
- **Week 3** — 48 h burn-in, shoot the ten-slot photo set on units 1 and 2, pack.
- **Week 4** — list, quantity 10, three at the founder price.
### Risks
 
- **Tube supply and envelope tint.** Worse than TERMINAL-06, not better — round glass shows
  tint variation far more than IN-12's flat face, and here six tubes sit in one exposed
  row with nothing hiding them. Buy all six tubes × ten units, plus 20 % spares, from one
  seller in one purchase. Non-negotiable.
- **Verticality.** The signature failure mode. Mitigated by J-VERT, caught by QC, but it
  will still cost you units early.
- **Deck cosmetics.** Every fingerprint on a gold plane shows in a photograph. Gloves, and
  the deck stays bagged until step 4.
- **The 200 V set point.** If the stock feedback loop will not reach it, you need a resistor
  change *before* ten boards are populated. This is a week-0 task, not a week-2 surprise.
- **Revising after unit 1.** A deck respin costs ~$20 and a week. Ten wrong decks cost the run.
