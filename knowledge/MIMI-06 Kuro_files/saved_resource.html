Why text faces are the right answer, not the fallback The manual documents “write a character at a position” and “write a string” — which reads like an ASCII-in, glyph-out font ROM, the way most of these driver ICs work. If there is no CGRAM-style “define this 5×7 pattern as character N” command, the expressions cannot be hand-drawn pixel icons. That sounds like a loss and isn't: kaomoji are what a 16-cell character display was born to do, the asset pipeline collapses from a bitmap toolchain to a string table, and the look lands closer to a 1980s Japanese appliance than a modern OLED pretending to be one. Confirm the CGRAM question with the seller anyway — it decides whether an ear-twitch is ever possible.

PLATE 02

The display, decided

Path 2 of three

Three paths were open. Path 1 — chase a genuine 128 × 32 graphic panel — survived four separate hunts and produced nothing buyable: industrial distributors price for POS terminals, Japan's auction lots are all segment-type digit tubes, one Avito lead was a plasma panel with an internal character-scan chip, and the one real GU112X16 seller sells finished clocks and won't discuss the bare part. Path 3 — substitute an OLED — keeps the pixel art and breaks the reason the object exists. Path 2 is the only one with a seller on the other end of it.

16-digit150 × 25 mm+2 / −1 mmTake it. The bezel was cut at 148 × 26 for a display that didn't exist; this one lands inside it. Face plus text on one line, 16 cells.
What arrives

Glass, board, and code

Genuine FUTABA glass, sold as bare screen, bare module, or with its own driver board. Same driver IC across all three sizes, so the program is interchangeable. Driver source is written in C for both 8051 and Arduino targets. EN solder jumper (J1) enables the power stage; 4×M2 + 2×M3 mounting.

Command set

Five commands, and that is all

Set digit count · set brightness · power on · write a character at a position · write a string from a position. A character module needs single digits of bytes per state change where a 512-byte raw bitmap frame needed hundreds — which is what makes the pin budget survivable.

Still unpriced

Three open items

Custom-glyph (CGRAM) support · price and minimum order from this seller · a bench check that bit-banged SPI really is ISR-safe next to the multiplex. None blocks drawing the face; all three block ordering ten.

PLATE 03

Why the obvious wiring breaks the clock

Timing

Rev C moved the controls onto A6/A7 resistor ladders, which leaves exactly one spare digital pin. Electrically a serial display fits. In time it does not, if driven the naive way.

QuantityValueConsequence
Timer2 multiplex ISR period16 µsthe clock's heartbeat
One anode slot416 µshow long a single tube is lit
SoftwareSerial, one byte at 19 200 baud520 µslonger than a whole digit slot — interrupts off the entire time
A 512-byte bitmap frame that way≈0.27 svisible stall, one cathode left sitting lit

The fix is bit-banged SPI on D0/D1, not SoftwareSerial on D12. SPI's clock line is self-timed by whichever side is driving it, so a write never needs a whole byte of precise interrupt-free framing — at most the instant around each clock edge. Cost: a 1 kΩ series resistor and a two-pin jumper to lift the module during flashing, because D0/D1 share the USB-serial chip and see bootloader chatter at reset. D12 stays the real spare. Roughly 50 ₽ of parts, against the new main board an earlier study called for.

Reasoning, not a bench result The claim that bit-banged SPI is gentler on the ISR than SoftwareSerial follows from how the two protocols frame bytes, and it has not been measured on this hardware. Same rule as everywhere else in this project: measure it before ten units depend on it.

PLATE 04

KURO

Palette · shell · print

The original shell was milk-white PETG with a blush brow and printed ears. It was charming and it carried a production problem in every photograph: white shows layer lines, scuffs and fingerprints that black hides, and it needed its own bright low-contrast lighting set because a cream body on a dark background reads grey. Going dark deletes both problems and one filament order.

Shell

Same wedge, harder edges

176 × 104 × 72 mm, front raked 12°, tube deck on top and inset — the ears stay but lose their round; they become skewed fins at the rear corners with a magenta gradient inside the lip, so the silhouette still reads as a face at three metres. Matte black PETG throughout, 0.20 mm, no supports. Same spool as TERMINAL-06.

Light discipline

Three emitters, one hierarchy

The VFD at ≈505 nm sits almost exactly on the eye's peak sensitivity; the nixies at ≈605 nm do not, and they run at 1.10 mA average with no headroom to give. The panel must be dimmed to the tubes, never the reverse. Magenta and cyan appear only in printed and anodised surfaces — nothing in this product emits pink.

The finding worth carrying to the spec KURO is cheaper to build than MILK, not more expensive: one filament batch across both products instead of two, no extra finishing pass on visible layer lines, and one dark photography set instead of the two the milk shell required. The colourway was chosen for looks and pays for itself in production.
The consequence nobody wanted TERMINAL-06 Rev E grows its face to 237 mm to seat the ИН-15 AM/PM annex. MIMI does not need that annex — a VFD that can spell “OKAERI” can obviously spell “AM” — so MIMI stays at 176 mm, and the two products stop sharing a fascia outline. That was a genuine economy: one panel artwork, one Rezonit order of 30 instead of two orders of 15. Tube board, TS06-SEC, the control scheme, the ladder circuit, the switchgear and the firmware base are all still common. The panel is not. Price both quantities before assuming the split is affordable.
