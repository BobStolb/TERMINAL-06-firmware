# QUADRANT-D — 2 × 2 nixie desk miniature, production spec (Rev 3)
 
The shop's **entry object**: four ИН-17 pointing at you in a square, HH over MM, read at once,
in a black block you can close a hand around. One cable, one control, no mode tree.
 
Supersedes the desk half of `claude/QUADRANT-wrist-feasibility.md` (Rev 3), which opened this
concept and is retained for the wrist sibling and the withdrawal record. **Six of Rev 3's
decisions change here and are listed in §0 rather than quietly overwritten.**
 
Deliverables: this spec · `QUADRANT-D-parts-list.xlsx` (sourced BOM, live formulas) ·
concept plates (not yet drawn — blocked, see §11.1).
 
**Rev 2 (05.09.26) — four changes, all downstream of one decision.**
 
1. **Wi-Fi is withdrawn.** A clock that asks to join a network is the wrong object. NTP goes with
   it, DS3231 becomes the only timebase, and the telemetry link moves to **USB** (§3.1, §4).
2. **Which makes the power input plain 5 V**, since the cable that carries data has to carry
   power and a PC port is not a PD source. The PD trigger and the 20 W brick are deleted and the
   "wrong charger, nothing lights" failure mode goes with them (§3.3).
3. **MCU re-picked: RP2040.** No radio in the product at all, driverless USB CDC (a CH340 driver
   prompt would kill the telemetry feature on contact), and PIO+DMA for §4.0 (§3.1).
4. **§4.0 is new and it changes the animation catalogue.** Static drive was chosen to delete the
   multiplex; what nobody costed is what it gives back — **per-cathode brightness**, and with it
   a digit-to-digit crossfade inside one tube that no multiplexed nixie clock can perform.
Also in Rev 2: the bezel-magnet configuration scheme is **dropped** and replaced by a 25 ₽ piezo
(§4 B tier); **DIAL is parked and reassigned to TERMINAL-06** (§6.4); the module bus loses its
radio and becomes wired (§6.2).
 
**Rev 3 (05.09.26) — the internals, and three things that changed while drawing them.**
 
1. **§5 is rewritten as the internals section**, because the case size is an *output* of the board
   layout, not an input to it. **One vertical board**, tubes on the front face, everything else on
   the back — the forty cathode traces never leave the board they start on and no internal
   connector carries high voltage (§5.1). Case falls out at **62 × 70 × 58 mm**.
2. **The colon is deleted** (§2.3). The 2 × 2 grouping *is* the separator — that was always the
   thesis — and removing it takes out two ИНС-1, two ballasts, a transistor, a printed holder, a
   binning step and the QC line TERMINAL-06 calls *"the most visible defect on the product"*.
3. **Knock gets a hardware fallback** (§3.2): the rear panel gains **two buttons** beside the
   encoder, and the rule is now written down — *no function is knock-only.*
4. **Rev 2's 420 g was wrong** and is corrected to ≈330 g with the arithmetic shown (§5.4). Cost
   lands at **≈4 697 ₽**.

## 0. What changed against the feasibility document, and why
 
Rev 3 costed and sketched this product before anyone read a driver datasheet. Six corrections,
all of them load-bearing.
 
| Rev 3 (02.09.26) | Rev 1 of this spec | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Drive: **2 × HV5622**, static, "3 GPIO" | **2 × HV5630** (300 V), static, **4 signals + a level shifter** | HV5622 is rated **220 V**. The rail is 185 V and eandc's ТУ for ИН-17 asks for **≥200 V supply**, so 220 V is 20–35 V of margin on a part that stands the full rail when off. This shop already refused a 160 V transistor on a 185 V rail (spec §3, MPSA42 over 2N5551). HV5630 is the same part at 300 V. |
| "3 GPIO" | **CLK · DIN · LE · BL**, plus **12 V logic** | The HV55xx/HV56xx family's logic supply is **10.8–13.2 V**, not 3.3/5 V. SPI has to be shifted up. This was missed entirely. |
| Power: **mains**, ≈1 W continuous | **USB-C PD, 12 V**, ≈2.5 W | The 12 V logic rail above makes 12 V the natural input, a PD trigger makes it one familiar cable, and it removes a mains PSU and its enclosure volume from an entry product. |
| Controls: one knob + one button on the **top deck** | **one rear encoder-with-push**, and the front face carries nothing | "No prominent control scheme" is the brief. A 2 × 2 of glass wants no hardware in front of it. |
| Cost ≈ **3 500 ₽**/unit | **≈4 700 ₽**/unit, itemised | Rev 3's "enclosure, PSU, packaging ≈900 ₽" is roughly a third of what those three groups cost on TERMINAL-06, and it costed the power supply at zero. Rebuilt line by line in the workbook: **the product is just under half of TERMINAL-06, not a third of it.** |
| Anti-poisoning "mandatory" | **mandatory, and the duty cycle is a product risk in its own right** | Static drive means 100 % duty. ИН-17's ТУ life is **7 500–9 000 h ≈ 10–12 months of always-on**. See §11.2. This is the single most important thing Rev 3 did not know. |
 
Rev 3's core findings all stand: end-view tube, window not plinth, 48 leads straight back into
one flat board, no riser, no flex, 25–35 min of tube work, and the labour argument that forced a
$700 wrist price does not exist here.

## 1. Thesis and position in the range
 
**Every nixie clock on the market is a row.** Four digits in a square is not a smaller version of
that — it is a different object. A row reads as a readout; a square reads as an *instrument face*,
and it gives the firmware a second axis nothing else in the shop has (§4).
 
| | TERMINAL-06 | **QUADRANT-D** | EMBER-06 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass | 4× ИН-12А + 2× ИН-17 + 2× ИН-15 + 2× ИНС-1 | **4× ИН-17 and nothing else** | ИН-14/ИН-16 |
| Digits | 6 | **4** | 6 |
| Controls | rotary + 2 levers + 2 buttons | **one rear encoder** | — |
| Power | mains PSU | **USB-C** | — |
| Drive | multiplexed, К155ИД1 + optos | **static, HV shift registers** | — |
| Cost / retail | 10 078 ₽ / 27 000 ₽ | **≈4 700 ₽ / 15 000 ₽** | — / $329 |
| Hands-on | 2 h 20 | **≈1 h 30** | — |
 
Its job is to be **the cheapest way for someone to own real nixie glass from this bench**, and to
be the product that carries the shop's second drive architecture on four tubes instead of six.
 
**Niche, stated deliberately:** the desk-setup audience — mechanical keyboards, a monitor arm, one
good lamp, everything USB-C. That audience buys objects that *do something on the desk*, which is
why the telemetry mode (§4, S tier) exists and why the body is 62 mm wide rather than 176.

## 2. Display and drive
 
### 2.1 Static, and this is where the shop learns it
 
`claude/TERMINAL-06-anode-driver-study.md` §4 T3.3 already wrote the verdict:
 
> HV shift-register static drive — **Rev E / product 2.** Right answer for a product line, wrong
> answer for this run. **Carry it forward to QUADRANT** and the decatron product so they don't
> inherit the multiplex from scratch.
 
This is that product. Two independent reasons, one of which is not a preference:
 
1. **Multiplex is forbidden here by the tube, and Rev 3 has a second source for it.**
   `TERMINAL-06-measurements-IN17.md` records a **4 mA DC overcurrent ceiling**; four tubes at 25 %
   duty need ~6 mA peak to average 1.5 mA, so it does not fit. A Russian catalogue entry
   (tec.org.ru) looked at first like a contradiction — it permits **3.5–7 mA in pulse** — until the
   condition is read: **скважность до 25**, i.e. that allowance holds only at a duty of ~4 %. A
   four-tube multiplex runs at 25 % duty (скважность 4), which is six times denser than the pulsed
   rating covers. **Both sources say the same thing.** (Two banks at 50 % duty would fit the DC
   figure at 3 mA peak — costed and rejected in §2.4.)
2. **Static makes the entire ghosting programme structurally impossible.** No cathode selection
   ever changes, so there is nothing to ghost. Deleted from this product before it exists:
   К155ИД1, six TLP627s, six anode bleed resistors, the multiplex ISR, `DEAD_TICKS`, the
   refresh-rate-versus-dead-time trade, the 274 Hz ceiling, and the camera-banding criterion that
   generated all of it. QUADRANT-D **cannot band on video at any frame rate.**
Also: 100 % duty is ~4–6× the light of a multiplexed tube, which matters on a 9 mm digit.
 
### 2.2 The chips
 
**2 × HV5630PG-G** — 32-channel low-voltage-serial to high-voltage-parallel converter,
open-drain, **300 V**, 100 mA/channel sink, 8 MHz clock, QFP-44, daisy-chained.
64 channels for **40 cathodes**; the spare 24 are §6's expansion budget.
 
- **HV5622 is the 220 V part of the same family and is the wrong one here.** Same footprint, same
  pinout, same price class. If a distributor stocks only the 220 V part, that is a reason to keep
  looking, not a reason to accept 35 V of margin on the shop's first static HV product.
- **Logic supply 10.8–13.2 V.** CLK/DIN/LE/BL come from a 3.3 V MCU, so a **CD4504B** hex level
  shifter (Vcc = 3.3 V logic side, Vdd = 12 V) sits between them. One 16-pin DIP/SOIC part, four
  channels used, two spare.
- **BL (blank) is global PWM brightness for free.** One pin dims all forty cathodes together,
  which is the correct behaviour for a display whose whole risk is *four tubes visibly not
  matching* (§11.3). Per-tube brightness trimming is not offered and should not be — a global dim
  cannot introduce a mismatch.
- **QFP-44 is not millable on the CNC 3040.** This is a Rezonit board with a stencil and a hotplate,
  or careful hand-work under the microscope. Budget it as a skill to acquire once, on two chips,
  rather than a per-unit cost.
- **Counterfeit risk:** the same warning the TERMINAL spec carries for the MCP23017s applies
  harder to an HV part. Buy through ChipDip or Farnell, not a marketplace listing.
### 2.3 Rail, resistors, current
 
| | Value | Working |
|---|---|---|
| HV rail | **185 V nominal**, trimmable to 200 V | Same rail as TERMINAL-06 — one rail voltage across the shop. eandc's ТУ asks ≥200 V supply and swissnixie quotes ≤170 V strike / 105 V maintain; the 300 V driver is what makes raising the trimmer free if a batch strikes reluctantly. |
| Anode resistor, per tube | **56 kΩ 0.5 W start** | (185 − 105) / 56 k = **1.43 mA**. 47 k → 1.70 mA (brighter), 68 k → 1.18 mA (softer, longer-lived). 0.5 W is for the voltage rating, not the ~0.11 W dissipated. **One per tube, never shared.** |
| Colon | **none — deleted in Rev 3** | See below. |
| Total HV load | 4 × 1.43 ≈ **5.7 mA @ 185 V ≈ 1.06 W** | |
 
**The colon is deleted, and it should have been from the start.** *HH over MM, read at once* was the
thesis in the first sentence of the feasibility document — **the 2 × 2 grouping is the separator.**
A row needs a colon because `1423` is otherwise one number; a square does not, because the eye reads
two fields before it reads four digits. Putting a colon between the rows was a row habit carried
onto a shape that does not have the problem.
 
What leaves with it: 2 × ИНС-1, two 220 kΩ ballasts, an MPSA42 and its base resistor, the printed
colon holder, the **J-NEON** pairing rig, the matched-pair binning step in the build, and the QC
line TERMINAL-06 §3 calls **"the most visible defect on the product"** — *a brightness mismatch
between the two dots of one colon.* **Deleting a whole class of visible defect from the entry
product is worth more than the 50 ₽.**
 
**The seconds tick moves onto the digits.** Per-cathode PWM (§4.0) makes a **1 Hz breathe of ~3–5 %
depth** free — the whole display inhales once a second. Nothing extra, nothing to mismatch, and it
is liveness without a blinking dot. If it reads badly on the bench, the window-wash LEDs (§5.5) are
the fallback carrier for the same pulse.
 
### 2.4 Rejected, with the reason recorded
 
- **Two-bank multiplex** (2 tubes/bank, 50 % duty, 3 mA peak — legal under the 4 mA ceiling).
  Costs two К155ИД1s, a second cathode bus, an ISR, dead-time tuning and six bleed footprints, to
  arrive at the same average current and the same brightness as static drive. **Multiplexing does
  not extend tube life** — sputtering tracks average charge, not peak — so it buys nothing on
  §11.2 either. Rejected.
- **5 × 74HC595 + 40 × MPSA42 + 40 base resistors.** Through-hole, millable, no counterfeit
  exposure, ~120 ₽ of parts. And **85 extra components and ~250 extra solder joints per unit** on
  the product whose entire argument is a short build. Rejected — but this is the honest fallback
  if HV5630 cannot be sourced, and it should be kept in the back pocket rather than forgotten.

## 3. Timebase, control, power
 
### 3.1 No radio in this product
 
**Wi-Fi is withdrawn (Rev 2).** A clock that opens a captive portal and asks to join a network
is a different kind of object from the one this shop sells, and the objection is correct: the
setup screen would be the first thing a customer ever saw. NTP goes with it.
 
| | **Rev 2 — RP2040 + DS3231 (recommended)** | Rev 1 — ESP32-C3 + NTP | Shop standard — Nano + DS3231 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radio | **none, at all** | Wi-Fi | none |
| USB to the host | **native CDC, driverless** | native CDC | CH340 bridge — **a driver install on some Windows machines** |
| Logic | 3.3 V, matches the CD4504B low side | 3.3 V | 5 V |
| Per-cathode PWM (§4.0) | **PIO + DMA, ~0 % CPU** | timer ISR, few % | timer ISR, tight on an AVR |
| Timebase | DS3231, ±2 ppm ≈ 1 min/year | RTC-less | DS3231 |
| Cost | ~300 ₽ | ~350 ₽ | ~450 ₽ with RTC |
 
**RP2040**, on three grounds: no radio anywhere in the product; **driverless USB CDC**, which
matters because the telemetry link (§4, S tier) is a consumer-facing USB connection and a CH340 driver
prompt would be the support burden that kills it; and the PIO block, which shifts the HV
registers with zero CPU — see §4.0, the capability the whole animation catalogue now rests on.
 
Against it: the shop has no RP2040 habits. That costs less than it sounds, because **static drive
means there is no AlexGyver ISR to port** — no К155ИД1, no anode multiplex, no A6/A7 ladder. The
QUADRANT firmware shares almost nothing with TERMINAL-06's except the effect *ideas*.
 
**Setting the time, with no network.** Three layers, in the order a customer meets them:
 
1. **It arrives set.** DS3231 is ±2 ppm — about a minute a year. Set on the bench at QC, and for
   most owners that is the last time anyone touches it.
2. **It sets itself whenever it is plugged into a PC.** The telemetry agent (§4) sends the host
   clock on connect. No UI, no network, and the audience this is aimed at plugs it into a PC.
3. **The encoder, for the twice-a-year case.** Press-and-hold → hours flash, turn, press →
   minutes flash, turn, press → done. That is a DST shift or a house move, not a daily interaction.
### 3.2 The only control
 
**Everything is on the rear panel and nothing is on the front.** No dial artwork, no fascia PCB, no
resistor ladder — **TERMINAL-06 §2 does not exist in this product** — and in use or in a photograph
the object has no visible interface at all.
 
| Rear panel | Function |
|---|---|
| **Rotary encoder with push** | turn = brightness · press = confirm |
| **BTN 1 — FACE** | cycle time → date → MM:SS → DUAL |
| **BTN 2 — BACK** | cancel / return · **hold = set time** (hours flash, turn, press, minutes flash, turn, press) |
| USB-C | power, and data when it is a PC |
 
**Rev 3: the two buttons are the knock's fallback, and this is now a rule rather than a nicety.**
 
> **No function is knock-only.** Every gesture the piezo triggers has a button that does the same
> thing. A knock is a shortcut, never the only path.
 
The reason is concrete: knock detection depends on the desk. A glass desk transmits beautifully, a
thick carpeted trestle may not, and the threshold that works on the bench may not work in a
customer's room. If the piezo is the only way to see the date, a bad desk turns a feature into a
fault report. With two buttons behind it, a bad desk costs the customer a party trick and nothing
else — and §11.8's bench test becomes a tuning exercise rather than a gate.
 
Two sensors, not controls, and both earn their place:
 
Two sensors, not controls, and both earn their place:
 
- **LDR** (~5 ₽) on an ADC pin, **looking up through the top deck, not forward** — it has to read
  the room, not the tube glow. Auto-dim is the primary mitigation for §11.2, not a convenience.
- **Piezo disc** (~25 ₽) bonded inside the base plate, on an ADC pin. See §4.5. One part, no hole
  in the case, and now with a button behind it.
### 3.3 Power — one plain USB-C cable, 5 V
 
**Rev 2 change: the PD trigger is deleted.** Dropping Wi-Fi puts the telemetry link on USB, which
means the cable that carries data has to carry power too — and a PC's USB port is not a PD source.
So the input is **plain 5 V**, boosted to 12 V on the board for the HV5630 logic rail and the HV
converter.
 
- ~2.5 W total → **≈560 mA at 5 V.** Fine on any USB-C port, any USB 3.x port, or any phone
  charger. Marginal only on a legacy 500 mA USB 2.0 port, where it will simply run dimmer if the
  port sags — the LDR curve already tolerates that.
- **The "wrong charger and nothing lights" failure mode from Rev 1 is gone entirely.** So is the
  20 W PD brick in the box; a plain 5 V 1.5 A supply ships instead, and most owners will use the
  PC.
**One cable, two destinations — this is the whole story:**
| Plugged into | What happens |
|---|---|
| **The 5 V supply in the box, or any phone charger / USB hub** | It is a clock. Everything in §4 works — animations, knock, date, seconds, brightness, the lot. |
| **A PC, with the same cable** | Identical, plus the telemetry faces, and the RTC sets itself. |
 
**There is never a second cable and never a wall wart alongside a data lead.** The PC is optional:
plugging it in adds features, unplugging it takes them away and the clock carries on. That is why
the heartbeat in §4 falls back to the clock after five seconds — the resting state of the product is
a clock on a charger.

## 4. What it does — the behaviour catalogue
 
Ranked as you ranked them. §4.0 is new and it is the enabling capability underneath the S tier —
without it, half of what follows is not buildable.
 
### 4.0 The capability nobody had costed: per-cathode brightness
 
Static drive was chosen to *delete* the multiplex. What was not noticed is what it gives back.
 
Each tube owns ten private cathode lines on the shift registers. Rewriting the 64-bit chain takes
**16 µs at 4 MHz**, so the display can be re-drawn hundreds of times a second — and with **binary
code modulation (8 planes per frame, not 256 slots)** a full 8-bit brightness field costs
~800 register writes a second. On RP2040 that is a PIO program fed by DMA: **0 % CPU.**
 
What that unlocks, none of which a multiplexed clock can do:
 
- **Per-tube brightness** — so a tube can fade while its neighbours hold.
- **Per-cathode brightness** — so *two digits inside one tube* can be lit at different levels.
- Global master dim still lives on the driver's BL pin, on top of everything above.
**Two honest constraints, both bench questions:**
 
1. **The bottom bits may not strike.** A nixie needs time to ionise; at 100 Hz the LSB plane is
   ~39 µs and the glow may not form at all. Expect a usable depth of **4–5 bits (16–32 levels)
   with a minimum on-time floor**, not a clean 8-bit ramp. Characterise the real curve on the
   bench before any effect is tuned to it.
2. **Never light two cathodes in one tube simultaneously.** They would share one anode resistor's
   ~1.4 mA and the nearer cathode may hog it. **Time-interleave them inside the frame instead** —
   at >100 Hz the eye integrates it identically, and the current question disappears.

### S — animations
 
The 2 × 2 is the shop's first display that is not a row, and **every effect in the existing
catalogue is defined by "shift one place right"**, which has no meaning on a square. This is new
inventory, not a re-skin. Positions: `0` HH-tens (top-left) · `1` HH-units · `2` MM-tens ·
`3` MM-units.
 
| Effect | Order / motion | Ticks | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dissolve** | per tube: old digit interleaved down while the new one comes up | ~16 @ 30 ms | **The exclusive one.** Two numerals at two *depths* in the same tube, one giving way to the other. AlexGyver's effect 1 fades the whole tube; this fades between digits. **No multiplexed nixie clock can do it**, because it needs per-cathode brightness (§4.0). If it works on the bench it is the single best thing in the listing video. |
| **Pinwheel** | 0 → 1 → 3 → 2, 90° apart | 4 @ 60 ms | The rotational order. Doubles as the sweep, see A tier. |
| **Diagonal wipe** | (0,3) then (1,2) | 2 @ 90 ms | Two ticks, not four. Reads as a shutter. |
| **Orbit** | all four digits step one place clockwise, N laps, then land | 4N + 1 | The digits are interchangeable between positions, which is a fact about a square and not about a row. |
| **Fold** | top row falls into the bottom row, new values rise out | ~6 @ 70 ms | The vertical answer to "train". A row has no vertical. |
| **Cathode-order flip** | walks the physical cathode stack via `cathodeMask` | 30–50 ms/step | Effect 3, inherited — **the only effect that is exclusively nixie**, the glow stepping through the depth of the tube instead of through arithmetic. **Blocked:** ИН-17's `cathodeMask` has never been measured, and the ИН-12 mask looks *almost* right, which is worse than looking wrong. One `secCathodeMap` session clears it, and it also serves TERMINAL-06's seconds tubes. |
 
**A property worth putting in the listing copy:** static drive means **none of these can band on
camera at any frame rate.** TERMINAL-06 spent a whole prototype gate on that problem. This product
cannot have it. Film any effect, at any shutter, with no test.
 
**Keep the glitch as inherited** — random tube, 2–6 blinks of 20–100 ms, every 30–120 s, suppressed
around transitions. It is the single most-commented-on feature in nixie clock reviews and it costs
nothing to port.
 
### S — desk telemetry, over USB (no network)
 
Four digits is exactly two two-digit numbers, and **a square reads them as two numbers where a row
reads them as one.** `67` over `54` is unambiguously CPU over GPU; `6754` in a row is not. That is
the structural argument for this feature and it is worth stating in the listing.
 
- **Link:** USB CDC at 115 200, line-based ASCII, driverless on RP2040. `V 67 54` — two values,
  top and bottom. `N 1234` — one four-digit number. `B 45` — brightness override. `T` — hand back
  to the clock. `S <unix>` — set the RTC.
- **Heartbeat:** no line for 5 s → **revert to the clock.** The clock is the resting state, always.
  Unplug the PC and it is a clock again with no interaction.
- **Agent:** a small open-source script on the shop's GitHub, beside the firmware. **Ship a script,
  not a signed installer** — an unsigned .exe collects a SmartScreen warning and a support ticket;
  the PC-setup audience runs a script happily and can read it. Windows first (LibreHardwareMonitor
  for temps), Linux second, macOS if asked.
- **Payloads worth shipping day one:** CPU °C over GPU °C · CPU % over RAM % · down over up
  Mb/s · a countdown · a pomodoro · unread count · anything the user pipes in.
- **Non-negotiable:** the clock is finished and sellable **before** the agent exists. Ship it as a
  firmware feature and a download, never as a dependency. WATCHLIST's caveat stands — the shop has
  never shipped a device the computer talks back to.
### A — the pinwheel sweep
 
The anti-poisoning cycle (0→9 three times, 10 ms a step, ~300 ms) run with a **90° phase offset per
tube**, so the glow rotates around the square. Same maintenance function, same duration, and it is
the clearest single demonstration that this is not a short row.
 
- **Schedule: both.** A full pinwheel **on the hour** as the visible event, plus a quiet one on the
  inherited 15-minute timer. Static drive is 100 % duty (§11.2), so coverage is not optional.
- **Direction carries meaning:** clockwise on the hour; counter-clockwise when a countdown or a
  timer reaches zero. Free, and it means the object has one gesture that only ever appears at the
  end of something.
### B — the bezel-magnet scheme: agreed, dropped
 
**Collars become purely cosmetic.** One printed part, ~120 ₽, no sensor, no magnet alignment, no
contacts. They still carry the best margin in the range (§6.1); they just stop trying to be a
configuration channel.
 
Recorded so it is not re-proposed from scratch: if config-by-object is ever wanted back, **the
cheap version is not a hall sensor — it is a resistor in the collar**, read as a voltage divider on
one ADC pin. Literally TERMINAL-06 §2's A6 ladder, one resistor and two contacts per collar. It
was the wrong tool that made the idea expensive, not the idea. Parked, not deleted.
 
**Better sensing, and it replaces the whole scheme with one part:**
 
| Option | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Piezo disc, knock-sensing** | ~25 ₽ | **Take this one.** See §4.5. One ADC pin, bonded inside the base, **no hole in the case and no moving part**. Reads knocks on the desk, not sound, so music and speech do not false-trigger. |
| Capacitive pad behind the shell | ~10 ₽ | Works through 2 mm of PETG; RC charge-time method needs no special silicon. Fallback if knock proves unreliable on a soft or carpeted desk. Needs humidity/drift calibration. |
| Reed switch | ~30 ₽ | Same magnet-alignment hassle as the hall sensor. No. |
| IR / ToF gesture (VL53L0X) | ~250 ₽ | Ten times the price, false-triggers on a passing hand, reads as a gimmick. No. |

### Five more, all cheap and all on-brand
 
**4.5 — Knock to answer.** Two knocks on the desk → the square shows the date for four seconds,
then returns. One knock → seconds. The piezo reads mechanical shock through the base, so it is
deaf to the room. **No button, no hole, no visible input at all** — the object appears to have no
interface and answers anyway, which is the whole brief in one part. Filming it is one shot: a hand
enters frame, knocks the desk, the digits change. Bench work: an envelope threshold plus a
~250 ms debounce, and a rejection window so the encoder's own click cannot trigger it.
 
**4.6 — Seconds on demand, as a square.** No seconds tubes and no room for them, so instead the
display **re-labels itself**: MM over SS — and with the colon deleted (§2.3), the signal that the
meaning changed is the **1 Hz breathe switching to a faster cadence**, or a single pulse of the
window wash (§5.5). In a row,
`0942` is ambiguous between a time and a date and a count. In a square, the top pair and the
bottom pair are visibly two fields, so the same four tubes can mean HH/MM, MM/SS or DD/MM without
a mode indicator anywhere. **Only a square can do this without a legend.**
 
**4.7 — DUAL, named and listed.** Formalise the above as the product's headline capability rather
than a side effect: **any two two-digit values, stacked.** Time over date · MM over SS ·
CPU over GPU · in over out · hours over minutes of a timer · day-of-month over day-of-week. It is
the one thing this shape does that no nixie clock on the market does, it costs nothing, and it is
the sentence the listing title should be built around.
 
**4.8 — Dissolve.** Called out separately from the animation table because it is not another
effect, it is a **capability no competitor's hardware has**: per-cathode brightness (§4.0) lets one
digit hand over to another *inside the same tube*, two numerals visible at two depths mid-flip. It
is the most physically nixie thing in the whole catalogue and it exists only because this product
dropped the multiplex. If the bench confirms it, lead the listing video with it.
 
**4.9 — The warm-up.** Power-on runs a slow strike through the physical cathode stack — 0→9 in
`cathodeMask` order at ~200 ms a step — so the tube visibly wakes through its own depth. Then the
unit's serial for a beat (`0003`), then its **lifetime lit hours**, then the time. Three seconds
total. Nothing else on Etsy has a boot sequence at all, it makes each unit read as a serialised
instrument rather than a craft item, and the hour meter turns §11.2's replacement quad from a
complaint into an obvious purchase at the right moment.
 
**Bonus, free: catch-up.** After a power cut the DS3231 already knows the time — so instead of
snapping to it, **wind to it**: spin the digits forward from the last displayed reading to now,
about two seconds. An outage becomes a visible, deliberate event instead of a silent jump, and it
is four lines of firmware.
 
### Parked, with reasons
 
- **Seconds tubes.** Two more tubes and a 3 × 2. *The squareness is the product* — Rev 3 of the
  feasibility document said it and §4.6 makes it unnecessary anyway.
- **Sound.** A buzzer is a returns risk on any always-on desk object.
- **A second control.** The moment there are two controls there is a control *scheme*.
- **Config-by-object.** See the B-tier note above — parked as the resistor ladder, not the hall
  sensor.
## 5. Internals, boards, and where the case size comes from
 
Protocol rule 20: *an end-view tube gets a window; a side-view tube gets an open deck.* ИН-17 seen
end-on has nothing to show but its face, so standing it proud would display a glass barrel and
forty-eight leads. Everything below follows from that one decision.
 
### 5.1 One board, and the reason is the forty cathodes
 
The obvious layout is two boards — a tube board at the front, a main board in the base. **It is
wrong here, and the number that kills it is 40.** Static drive gives every tube ten private cathode
lines; if the drivers live on the main board, forty conductors at 185 V have to cross between the
two on a ribbon. That is TERMINAL-06's cathode harness — *"mistakes invisible until HV"*, the
second-worst step in its build — reintroduced into the product that exists to not have it.
 
**So: one vertical board, perpendicular to the tube axis. Tubes on the front face, everything else
on the back face, encoder and USB-C protruding rearward.** The forty cathode traces never leave the
board they start on, there is **no internal connector carrying high voltage**, and the case gets
shallower because the tall parts sit behind the tube tails rather than under them.
 
```
   front                                                        rear
   ├─ collar (printed, swappable)
   │  ├─ window aperture, 2 mm reveal
   │  │   ┌──────── 4 × ИН-17, end-view, 2×2 at 26 × 27 mm ────────┐
   │  │   │  face setback ~3 mm │ envelope ~22 mm running back      │
   ─┼──┼───┴────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┴─┐
    │  │                                        Q-BOARD, 1.6 mm ──────┤
    │  │   front face: 48 tube holes, 4 anode R      back face: ──────┤
    │  │                                             2 × HV5630 ──────┤
    0  2                     27                   29 │ CD4504B ───────┤
                                                     │ RP2040 ────────┤
                                                     │ DS3231SN ──────┤
                                                     │ HV boost ──────┤
                                                     │ 5→12 V boost ──┤
                                                     │ cap + bleed ───┤
                                                     │ encoder ───────┤ ← through rear panel
                                                     │ 2 × button ────┤ ← through rear panel
                                                     │ USB-C ─────────┤ ← through rear panel
                                                     └── 54 ───── 58 ─┘
```
 
**Front face of the board** — 48 tube holes in four clusters, 4 anode resistors, and nothing else.
**Back face** — the two HV5630s sit directly behind the tube clusters, so a cathode trace runs
15–25 mm from its hole to its driver pin and never crosses another tube's field. At a 26 mm
horizontal pitch and a lead cluster ~12 mm across, there is a **~14 mm channel between adjacent
tubes** to route ten traces out of each — routable, though it wants a 4-layer board rather than 2.
 
**Fallback if the layout does not close:** split into Q-TUBE (tubes + both HV5630s + CD4504B) and
Q-MAIN, with **the drivers staying on the tube board regardless**, so the interconnect is 7 ways —
GND, +12 V, +3.3 V, CLK, DIN, LE, BL — plus a separate keyed 2-pin JST-VH for the 185 V, exactly
TERMINAL-06's three-keyed-families discipline. Costs one more PCB (~+150 ₽) and gains nothing else.

### 5.3 The size, derived rather than asserted
 
Every figure below is arithmetic on the working envelope **≈19.3 × ~17.5 mm face, ~22 mm deep**
(rows 1–3 of the capture sheet, one sample). **Two of the three are still single-sample calipers and
the third is a photo estimate, so treat ±2 mm as the honest band** and re-derive when §11.1 closes.
 
| Chain | Sum |
|---|---|
| **Width** — 26 mm pitch + 19.3 mm envelope = 45.3 block · +2 mm reveal each side = 49.3 aperture · +6.5 mm frame each side | **≈62 mm** |
| **Height** — 27 mm pitch + 17.5 mm envelope = 44.5 block · +2 reveal = 48.5 aperture · +7 top frame · +14 base | **≈70 mm** |
| **Depth** — 2 reveal + 3 face setback + 22 tube + 1.6 board + 25 rear components + 4 rear wall | **≈58 mm** |
 
**62 × 70 × 58 mm, front raked 15°.** Rev 2 said 66 mm deep; the single-board layout takes 8 mm out
because the tall parts stopped living under the tubes.
 
**The layout rule that falls out of it, worth keeping:** *front third is glass, middle is the board,
rear third is everything tall.* The tubes end at ~29 mm, so from there back the full 60 mm of
interior height is free — which is where the boost module, the reservoir cap and the encoder go.
Anything tall that creeps forward under the tubes hits a 12 mm ceiling.
 
### 5.4 Weight — and the 420 g figure was wrong
 
A printed PETG shell at this size is ~120 g; boards ~40 g; four tubes 28 g. **That is ~190 g, and
an entry product that feels light feels cheap** — this is the single most correctable
perceived-quality problem in the range.
 
Rev 2 claimed ≈420 g. **Checked: it does not add up.** A 3 mm steel plate at 55 × 60 mm is 78 g, for
~270 g total. To reach 420 g the plate would have to be over 10 mm.
 
**Corrected target: ≈330 g, from a 5 mm laser-cut steel plate at 58 × 62 mm (141 g).** And make it
earn its place twice: **the plate is the visible base**, bead-blasted and clear-coated, with the
printed shell sitting on it — so the mass is also the part that stops the object reading as
"3D-printed", and it is where the serial gets engraved. Flat laser cut, no machining, ~200 ₽ for ten.
If it still wants heft, lead shot in epoxy in the base voids is the cheap second stage.
 
### 5.5 Light that is not a tube — the honest answer
 
**A lit bezel is how a nixie clock ends up looking like a forty-dollar kit.** That is the real
objection, not "LEDs are tacky": an RGB halo around the glass is the visual signature of the
cheapest end of your own market, and a buyer deciding between this and a $60 AliExpress clock reads
it in the first photo. **No RGB, no addressable strip, no colour cycling, no underglow spilling
onto the desk, no visible point source.** Those are the ones to refuse.
 
But there is a version that is not that, and one argument makes it worth having:
 
> **With the colon deleted, this product has no light source that is not a digit.** No status
> channel, no acknowledgement, no heartbeat.
 
So: **two amber 595 nm LEDs — the shop's existing backlight part — firing into the window well from
the top edge, hidden, diffused, PWM'd, default very low or off.** The recess reads as a faintly warm
cavity instead of a black hole, and TERMINAL-06 §3's rule carries over verbatim: *amber only,
blue/white fights the tube glow.*
 
What they are actually for:
 
- **knock acknowledgement** — a single soft pulse says the piezo heard you;
- **"no host"** and **"wrong supply"** indication, which otherwise has nowhere to live;
- the **1 Hz seconds pulse**, if §2.3's digit-breathe reads badly on the bench;
- boot heartbeat during the warm-up.
**Two better places for light, if more is wanted later:** behind the tubes rather than in front — an
amber wash inside the case seen through a smoked rear panel, lighting the 48 leads and the two QFPs
(*the back is the datasheet* — a differentiator, where a lit bezel is a cliché); or a **lit collar
sold as an accessory variant**, so the base product stays pure and the light is opt-in revenue.
 
### 5.6 Identity
 
Printed in PETG, same process and one filament batch as everything else: **chassis · collar ·
rear panel · window well insert.** The base is the steel plate, not a printed part. Palette
unchanged — VOID #0A0B0C · GRAPHITE #24272B · SIGNAL #F25610 · CATHODE #FF9E36 · BONE #DCD8D0.

## 6. Modularity — what is worth building and what is not
 
The brainstorm asked for a module system. Most of it is a good idea; one specific part of it is
not, and the research says which.
 
### 6.1 Tier 1 — collars. Build this first, it is nearly free
 
A snap-on front bezel, one printed part, ~25 g of filament and ~8 minutes. Brass-look, orange,
bone, machined aluminium for the top of the range. **Purely cosmetic as of Rev 2** — the hall
sensor and the magnet-keyed configuration are dropped (§4, B tier), which also removes the only
alignment-critical step from the assembly. Sell at 1 200–1 800 ₽, attach rate is whatever the
photos earn, and the whole risk is a printer running overnight. **There is no cheaper margin
anywhere in this document.**
 
### 6.2 The bus: wired, because there is no radio in this product
 
Modules dock left or right on a **4-pin magnetic pogo pair** (≈100–250 ₽ a pair; 2 A per pin is
ten times what is needed), carrying **5 V · GND · TX · RX** — a half-duplex UART chain with the
clock as master. Each module takes 5 V and makes its own HV.
 
- **No high voltage on the connector.** Nothing at 185 V ever crosses a contact a customer can
  touch. That rule survives Rev 2 unchanged and is the one part of the bus that is not negotiable.
- **UART, not I²C.** A bumped magnet drops a frame and the next one re-syncs; it cannot wedge a
  bus the way a half-clocked I²C transaction can.
- **A module also works on a short cable**, not only docked — the same four wires, just longer.
**What Rev 2 gives up, stated plainly:** free placement anywhere on the desk. That needed a radio,
and there is no radio in this product. For the record, and without re-litigating it: **ESP-NOW is
not the thing you rejected** — no router, no portal, no credentials, no account, two devices
talking directly — but it is still a radio and the call is yours. Wired is the default and the
spec is written for it.

### 6.4 DIAL — the dekatron knob: parked, and it belongs to TERMINAL-06
 
The brainstormed version was: dekatron on a bearing, slip ring, potentiometer, reads how far it
was turned, displays the menu position on itself. **The idea survives. The mechanism does not, and
the tube is the reason.**
 
What ОГ-3 actually is (tube-tester.com datasheet; the dos4ever decatron clock for the drive):
 
- **Anode supply 450 V typical (420 V minimum)**, 190 V maintaining, 600–800 µA. That is a
  **second, higher HV rail** — not a tap off the 185 V nixie bus.
- **The ten counting cathodes are commoned to one pin (K1–9).** Only the index cathode K0 is
  brought out separately.
- Three guide electrodes, 40 V bias, **110–140 V transfer pulses**, up to 20 kHz.
Two consequences, one fatal to the sketch and one that improves it:
 
1. **You cannot address a position.** The glow can only be *stepped* around by guide pulses, and
   the only position you can ever read electrically is the index cathode. A potentiometer telling
   you "the knob is at 63°" is useless — there is no way to jump the glow to position 6.
2. **Which means the potentiometer and the slip ring were never needed.** Use a **rotary encoder**:
   one detent of the knob = one guide pulse = one step of the glow. The glow *follows your hand*,
   1:1, mechanically honest, and the relative-only nature of the tube exactly matches the
   relative-only nature of an encoder. Home to K0 at power-on — the same trick the dos4ever clock
   uses to resynchronise.
**So the tube does not rotate at all.** A knurled ring turns around a stationary tube. This deletes
the bearing, the slip ring, and — the part worth saying out loud — **it deletes a rotating
contact carrying 450 V**, which was a wear item, an arcing source and a safety argument you would
have had to win.
 
Supply: ОГ-3 300 ₽ × 17 at Begrant is the only decatron on the board deep enough to try twice
(WATCHLIST, B tier). Six more decatron types exist across four sellers if it works.
 
**Rev 2 verdict: parked, and the right home is TERMINAL-06, not QUADRANT.** Three reasons, and
they are all about fit rather than feasibility:
 
- **It is a control surface, and QUADRANT-D's entire brief is not having one.** A knob you turn to
  pick a menu position belongs on the instrument with six labelled rotary positions, not on the
  object whose only control is hidden round the back.
- **A 450 V rail on an entry product is the wrong risk in the wrong place.** TERMINAL-06 already
  carries mains, an enclosure with room, and a customer paying 27 000 ₽ for exactly this kind of
  content.
- **It is the only motion in the shop's catalogue that cannot be faked in firmware** — a real
  gas-discharge handoff, stepped one position per detent. That is flagship material and it should
  be sold as such, beside SCALER-06's ring, not as a 12 000 ₽ accessory to a 15 000 ₽ clock.
**Everything above stays valid wherever it lands** — stationary tube, rotating ring, encoder not
potentiometer, home to K0 at power-on, and no rotating contact at 450 V. Carry it to the
TERMINAL-06 spec when that product's next revision opens; do not re-derive it.

## 9. Build (~1 h 30 hands-on/unit, after jigs)
 
Jigs: **J-QLEAD** (four-tube lead comb — sets all 48 leads at final spacing and the four tubes
coplanar *and at equal height*, the single most valuable jig in the product) · **J-BIN** (bench rig,
four positions at 1.5 mA side by side, for binning matched quads by eye) · **J-MAP** (ИН-17
digit→pin table, needed once — it is what unblocks the cathode-order flip in §4).
**J-NEON is not needed — Rev 3 deleted the colon.**
 
**Tube handling, from the ИН-17 datasheet and from a commercial ИН-17 kit's own assembly manual:**
solder no closer than **8 mm from the glass**, bend no closer than **3 mm**; trim leads to ~15–20 mm
before insertion and flush after; straighten each lead with long-nose pliers first; tack two
*opposite* leads to hold the tube square before committing the rest; burn each cathode in at
1.5–2 mA for 1–2 minutes before final soldering. **Orientation is off the anode lead** — confirm
which convention your batch uses before drilling anything (§11.10).
 
Reflow board (2 × QFP-44 + passives) → flash and bench-light → **bin a matched quad on J-BIN** →
set tubes in J-QLEAD, solder 48 leads → set HV 185 ± 8 V, set anode resistors, tune BL curve →
print parts → fit ballast plate and inserts → assemble (gloves) → set the RTC, confirm USB
enumeration and knock threshold →
24 h burn-in → serialise, photograph, pack.
 
Rev 3's estimate of 25–35 min for the tube work stands and is the bulk of the hand time. The
static board has **no cathode harness at all** — TERMINAL-06's second-worst step does not exist
here.

## 10. QC — every unit

no stuck or skipped cathode · BL dims

| 9 | viewed face → digit plane, **re-datumed to the end face** now that end-view is settled | how deep the digit sits inside the window; the parallax the collar has to clear |
| 10–12 | the lead spacer as its own part — outer size, hole pitch, slide range | the **J-QLEAD** jig, which is the highest-value jig in the build |
| 1, 2 | two more samples each, per the min/mean/max rule | promoting either to a load-bearing `dims.*` value |
 
**One evening with a caliper, and rows 5–7 are the half-hour of it that unblocks the board.**
 
**Rev 3 — I went looking for rows 5–7 on the web instead, and they are not there.** Checked: both
Dieter's original ИН-17 datasheet scans (electrical tables and a pin list, no drawing), the ТУ
transcriptions at rudatasheet.ru, eandc.ru and tec.org.ru (mass, digit height, "glass, socketless,
flexible leads" — no envelope drawing), swissnixie ("about 15 × 20 mm"), soviet-tubes
("22 × 20 × 14 mm"), neonixie, and a commercial ИН-17 clock kit's own assembly manual, which tells
the builder to trim leads to 15–20 mm and says nothing about their spacing. **No published source
gives the lead pitch, the lead circle, or the lead diameter.** That is the answer, and it is a
useful one: the number does not exist to be looked up, so the caliper is not a shortcut being
skipped — it is the only path.
 
**What the search does give is a bound, which is enough to keep drawing.** Twelve leads inside a
~14–17.5 mm minor envelope put lead-to-lead spacing at roughly **2.5–3 mm** whichever pattern they
use (a ~11 mm circle gives 2.9 mm; two rows of six give ~2.5 mm). So each cluster is **~11–13 mm
across**, which at the 26 mm horizontal pitch leaves a **~14 mm routing channel between tubes** —
enough to fan ten cathodes out of each cluster. **The board is routable at the assumed pitch.**
That is what §5.1 is drawn on, and it is a bound, not a measurement.
 
**11.2 — Tube life at 100 % duty. The most serious commercial risk in the product.**
ИН-17's ТУ life is **7 500 h (soviet-tubes) to ≥9 000 h (rudatasheet)** — at always-on, that is
**ten to twelve months.** TERMINAL-06 never had to think about this because it multiplexes at 1/6
duty. Static drive spends the whole budget. What to do, in order:
  1. **Run at 1.2–1.4 mA, not 2 mA.** 68 kΩ anode resistors cost nothing and buy proportionally.
  2. **Make the LDR night curve aggressive** — 25 % for eight hours a day is a real ~20 % of
     lifetime charge saved.
  3. **Decide whether the hour meter (§4.9) is visible or hidden.** Visible is the honest choice
     and it makes the replacement quad an obvious purchase rather than a complaint.
  4. **Bench the actual number.** Run one tube at 1.4 mA continuously beside one at 2 mA and
     photograph both weekly. ТУ life is a minimum at rated current and community experience with
     ИН-series glass at reduced current runs far longer — but **the ТУ figure is the one a
     warranty conversation will be held at**, so design to it and be pleasantly surprised.
  5. **Say it in the listing.** "Replaceable matched tube set, 1 800 ₽" reads as engineering.
     Silence followed by a dim tube at month fourteen reads as a defect.
**11.3 — Binning.** Four tubes lit permanently, side by side, all day. Rev 3's estimate is
60–70 % yield, ~6 bought per 4 shipped. **Bin six by eye at 1.5 mA on J-BIN and find out whether
that is pessimistic** — ten tubes are on the bench, it costs an evening, and it sets the real
glass cost per unit.
 
**11.4 — Supply depth is the difference between a product and a one-off run.** 40 tubes is six or
seven units. Before this is called the shop's entry *line*, either s_svshkin and Avito Seller B
have to have more than their listings show, or a third source has to appear. **Ask both, by
message, this week.** Rev 3 flagged this and it has not moved.
 
**11.5 — HV5630 price, stock and package.** Quote it. If only the 220 V HV5622 is stocked, the
options are (a) wait, (b) run the rail at 170–175 V and accept slower striking on cold tubes, or
(c) fall back to §2.4's 74HC595 array. **Do not silently accept the 220 V part at 185 V.**
 
**11.6 — ИН-17 cathode order, unmeasured.** Blocks the cathode-order flip and §4.9's warm-up, and running the ИН-12 mask on
ИН-17 looks almost right. One `secCathodeMap` session clears it and it also serves TERMINAL-06's
seconds tubes, so the work is shared.
 
**11.7 — Is there a 3–5 V-logic 32-channel HV driver?** The HV57708-class parts may remove the
12 V logic rail and the CD4504B entirely. Twenty minutes of datasheet reading could delete two
parts from every unit forever.
 
**11.8 — Three bench questions §4 now depends on, none of them long.**
  1. **How far down does a cathode still strike?** Sweep the BCM plane depth on one tube and find
     the shortest on-time that lights reliably. It sets the usable brightness resolution and every
     effect timing downstream. Expect 4–5 bits, not 8.
  2. **Does dissolve read as a crossfade or as a fault?** Two digits time-interleaved inside one
     tube at 100–200 Hz. If the eye sees flicker or one cathode dominating, the effect is dead and
     the animation table loses its best row — better to know before the listing video is planned.
  3. **Does the piezo distinguish a desk knock from the encoder's own click?** Envelope threshold
     plus a rejection window. If it cannot, fall back to the capacitive pad (§4, B tier).
**11.10 — Which lead is the orientation key? Two conventions are documented and they are not the
same.** A commercial ИН-17 kit's manual says the **anode lead is marked with a white bar** and goes
into the marked hole. A Russian catalogue entry instead describes one lead as **«вывод обрезан
(ключ)»** — physically cut short as an index. Both cannot be true of one batch, and getting it wrong
means four tubes soldered in rotated. **Look at your own ten tubes before the footprint is drawn**,
and record which convention they use in the capture sheet. It also decides whether J-QLEAD needs a
keyway or a marked slot.
 
**11.11 — Three source disagreements on ИН-17, recorded rather than resolved.** Sustaining voltage
**≥105 V** (rudatasheet, swissnixie) against **>160 V** (tec.org.ru) — the anode resistor value in
§2.3 assumes 105 V and would need rechecking if 160 V is right. Life **≥7 500–9 000 h** against
**1 000 h minimum** on the same page — §11.2's whole risk assessment sits on the higher figure.
Digit height **9 mm** (four sources) against **~8 mm** (neonixie). **None of these changes a decision
today; all three are cheap to settle on the bench and one of them (105 vs 160 V) changes a resistor.**
 
**11.9 — The telemetry agent is a support surface, not just a feature.** Decide before launch:
which OS ships first, whether it is a script or a binary, where it lives (the shop's GitHub, beside
the firmware), and what the listing promises. **Promise nothing about it in the title.** The clock
must be complete without it.

## 12. The wireless-power question, answered
 
The company is **Etherdyne Technologies** and the product is **Ether Power**. It has not gone
anywhere — it is just still a licensing conversation rather than a part you can buy.
 
- **What it is:** 6.78 MHz resonant power. A **flexible wire loop runs around the edge of the
  desk** and a hub drives it at **up to 100 W**; devices carry flat PCB coil receivers and work
  anywhere in the field, in any orientation except perpendicular to it. Not Qi — Qi is a coil
  aligned on a pad; this is a room-scale field with a wire, which is why the coils look different.
- **The demos:** the fully cable-free desktop PC that went round in mid-2025 (Hackaday, Tom's
  Hardware), and then **CES 2026** in January, where they showed position-free multi-device power
  as a "Wire-Free Power Zone".
- **The kit is real and in stock:** the EtherPower Evaluation Kit is a hub, a loop sized for a
  2 × 4 ft desktop, **eight receivers from Rx1 (34 mm, 5 V, 100 mW) to Rx8 (130 mm, 24 V, 7 W)**,
  field-indicator LEDs and documentation. Ships from California in a few days. **No published
  price — you fill in a form and talk to them**, and the intended path is a licensing deal, which
  is exactly why it hasn't turned into a component.
- **The numbers they publish themselves:** best measured DC-to-DC efficiency **69 %** (44.3 W out
  from 64.0 W in), and the desk build reported a **~10 W idle draw with nothing being powered**.
  The eval kit is **still in SAR compliance testing**.
**Verdict for QUADRANT-D: no, and the arithmetic is short.** The clock wants ~2.5 W, which is an
Rx7/Rx8-class receiver — **a 100–130 mm coil under a 62 mm product**. Then it wants 6.78 MHz of RF
sitting beside a 31 kHz HV boost and 185 V of glass. Then it asks a customer to buy a desk hub to
avoid one USB-C cable, and burns 10 W idle to save 2.5 W of cable. Your own instinct was right for
a better reason than you gave: it isn't the charging that's silly, it's that **the loss budget of
the field is four times the load.**
 
**The hedge that costs nothing:** put a flat, unobstructed **20 mm keep-out and two 12 V input pads
in the base**, documented. If a customer ever has the zone, the module is a receiver coil and a
regulator dropped into the base — a $30 accessory later, for zero design cost now.
 
**Rev 2 note:** the other half of the wish — free placement of modules — was going to ride ESP-NOW,
and Rev 2 removed every radio from the product. The module bus is now wired (§6.2). So the honest
Rev 2 position is narrower than Rev 1's: **wired power, wired data, and one cable to the desk.**
The keep-out above is still worth leaving in the base, because it costs nothing and it is the only
part of this section that could ever become real.
