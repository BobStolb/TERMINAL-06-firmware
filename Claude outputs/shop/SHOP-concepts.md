# Shop study: the shelf, the stock, a way to sell, five concepts

Run shop-concepts, 2026-11-15. A document only.

Marks: **F** fetched today ([w] = the page, listed at the end) · **R** from the files (the repo, the owner's pages) · **I** inferred · **A** assumed.

Read another way than the brief: none of the "shopfront attempts" is a shopfront (section 4); the "Meshok stock" is mostly watched, not held. Assumed once: the seller is in Russia, where the files' board fab and tube marketplace are (A).

## 1. The verdict

1. The shop has about 35 named ideas (I) and no unit built or sold (R). One finished unit is the shortage, not ideas.
2. No earlier page was a shop. They were listing drafts for drawings that went through four revisions in three weeks (R): no item, no real photo, no way to be paid.
3. Better: sell proof before product. One built unit, a waiting list, unit 1 at auction, a home marketplace first. Foreign channels only when a payment route is confirmed.
4. Build the line on ИН-12 (560 watched, R). ИН-17 is the limit (10 held, 25 watched, R): TERMINAL-06 and QUADRANT cannot both live on it.
5. The five concepts are three builds: a lit plate with no tubes, a tube tester, and one four-digit ИН-12 core with three faces (git, sky, timer). No new board before one TERMINAL-06 runs 48 hours. Firmware demos on the old clock can start now.

**When a type runs out,** it is gone. The plan:
1. Each tube product is a numbered run, sized by its scarcest tube.
2. Keep 20 % of each run's tubes as service spares.
3. Put the volume on the deep types: ИН-12, ИН-15А, ИНС-1.
4. Keep the driver apart from the tubes, as DISP and DRV are (R). A new tube type is then a new display board, not a new product.
5. Carry the shop between runs with items that need no tubes.

Needs tubes: all of section 2; concepts G, F and A's display. Needs none: P, T and A's sensor.

## 4. The shopfront

**The earlier attempts (R).**
* Concept Plates, Open Deck and Plate Set: prices (329 USD, then 27 000 ₽), "10 units", cost tables, a banner, a listing kit (title, tags, photo list, video plan).
* The TERMINAL-06 and EMBER-06 specs: listing titles and tags written for Etsy.
* The Japanese Shelf is a buying register. The TS06 product page is a design viewer; its Order tab is about ordering boards. Not read in this run.

None of them sells. **Why:**
1. No buyable item. Every picture is a drawing.
2. The product moved under the listing: four revisions from 24.08 to 09.09, then a fifth design (R).
3. The price came from a cost table (10 078 ₽ a unit, R; the audit's count is 185 USD, A), not from a buyer.
4. No audience. The pages are private; two are shared by link (R).
5. No channel. Etsy stopped paying sellers in Russia in 2022 (R, not re-checked).
6. No proof. Brightness, pin orders and the converter are unmeasured (R).

**One better approach: proof first, then a short ladder.**

*Channel.* First sales on a home marketplace with payment and delivery built in (Avito): it works today (A). Commission with delivery: 1 to 15 %, electronics up to 10 % (F [w7]). One own page (photos, video, price, waiting-list form) is where every post links; it is not the checkout. Tindie takes 5 % [w5], Etsy about 10 % plus fixed fees [w6] (F); both pay through processors that I assume do not serve the home country (A). The flagship price needs foreign buyers (I): the main open question.

*Before the first sale:* one unit run 48 hours in its closed case (R); real photos and a 20-second video; a price; one test parcel paid and shipped through the channel; a certified 12 V adapter in the box and a printed 185 V note, "wait 10 seconds after unplugging" (R); a one-page manual; a spare-tube price.

*The price ladder* (prices A, anchors F).

| Step | Item | Price | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cheap | P, lit plate, no tubes | 39 USD | LED contribution display 34 to 43 CAD [w3]; pixel clock 46 USD [w9] |
| Tool | T, tester kit | 79 USD | A healer kit bought at 60 USD [w4] |
| Core | Four-digit ИН-12 instrument, three faces | 179 USD | ИН-12 clock kit 159 EUR, assembled 199 EUR; cased ИН-12 clock 165 USD [w1]; desk flight tracker 175 USD [w2] |
| Flagship | TERMINAL-06 | 27 000 ₽, 326 USD (R) | Above the 130 to 260 USD middle (R) |

*Kit or finished.* The plate ships finished. Tube products start as kits (working default D2 a: less safety duty on the seller, R). Finished units later, to order, at a higher price.

*Batch.* Boards in small lots; units assembled to order; tubes reserved for each order.

*Learning demand.* A waiting list with its count shown. One short video for each concept, made on the old clock. Unit 1 at auction on the home marketplace: the hammer price is the first real price. A rule fixed in advance: a run of five needs 15 sign-ups, or an auction above 1.5 times parts (A).

**The order of steps.**
1. Audit steps 1 to 5 on the bench (R). The tube rig is the first tester (T).
2. Order one rev B set and the window coupon (D1 a, R). The coupon is the first plate (P).
3. Firmware profiles on the old clock: git numbers over USB; the timer. Film them.
4. The page and the waiting list. Post the films.
5. Sell plates: a low-risk test of payment and shipping.
6. TERMINAL-06 unit 1: 48 hours, photos, auction.
7. Size the first run from the list, not from the cost table.

## 5. Five new concepts

Scores: 1 to 5, 5 is best; for effort, 5 means least work. Demand is unproven for all five.

**The shared core (I).** G, A's display and F are one device: four ИН-12 and one ИН-15А symbol tube; TERMINAL-06's 185 V supply and its K155ID1 and TLP627 drive, at the four multiplex slots the old clock already proves (20.2 % duty, R); an ATmega328P module with USB; a clock chip; the fascia connector for a face with lights and controls. A product is a face and a firmware profile. Parts 90 USD (A: between a spec-style count of 60 and an audit-style count of 125). Price 179 USD: about 50 USD left after 10 % fees and 20 USD shipping (A).

### G. The git tracker (the owner's)

* **Pitch.** The infographic's branch picture as a desk instrument: counts on tubes, branches as lit lines. It shows one repository's working state, now.
* **Buyer.** Developers with a desk setup, and gifts for them. LED contribution displays sell at 34 to 43 CAD; one sold out in minutes (F [w3], weak). They show history. One search found none that shows ahead, behind and unmerged (F).
* **Tubes.** Left pair: commits ahead. Right pair: behind. Second page: open changes, and days since the last merge. The ИН-15А marks the page with "+", "−" or "M" (A: symbol set from memory).
* **Lights.** Gold branch lines with LED filaments behind bare-laminate windows: main always lit; the feature line lit while a branch is checked out; its tail glowing while work is unmerged; a node flashing when a merge lands.
* **Data.** USB from the computer. A short script reads `git status` and sends one text line; 5 seconds of silence returns the tubes to the clock. This is QUADRANT's telemetry pattern (R). No Wi-Fi: no token on the device, no network setup.
* **Other numbers, reuse.** Yes: the line carries any two numbers, a symbol and light bits. Reused: the core, the art-face method, the display firmware and its effects.
* **Scarce parts, cost.** 4 ИН-12, 1 ИН-15А, no ИН-17: the best fit with the stock. Cost and price: the core's.
* **Hardest part.** The host script on three systems, and its support: the shop "has never shipped a device the computer talks back to" (R).
* **Cheapest test.** The profile on the old clock. Film a real merge, post it, count sign-ups. No parts.
* **Score.** Demand 3, effort 4, margin 3, stock 5.

### A. The aircraft tracker and identifier (the owner's, with sky1)

* **Pitch.** A windowsill sensor hears propeller aircraft and measures each pass from its doppler curve. A tube display counts the passes and shows the last one.
* **What the method can tell (R, the handoff).** One microphone gives: that a tonal source passed (propeller, piston, helicopter); its speed and closest distance when the whole curve is heard, their ratio more surely than either; a class guess, after training on local sound. It gives no position, no track, no direction (that needs an array), and height only as that closest distance (I). Jets have no tones: a loudness count at best. Sound cannot name an aircraft. A name comes only from an ADS-B radio receiver, matched to the curve. An aircraft with no transponder stays unnamed.
* **The product is the display.** The sensor is an open recipe first: its parts cost 240 to 350 USD (R), more than most desk flight trackers (175 to 499 USD, F [w2]); it needs setup at each site; its code is planned (9 working days, then 2 to 4 weeks of data, R), not written (I).
* **Buyer.** Plane spotters and radio hobbyists who already run a receiver. Desk trackers sell, and several were sold out (F [w2]); they read internet data. This one is local, and it hears.
* **Tubes.** Passes today; then the last pass: speed, and closest distance in hundreds of metres. **Lights.** An arc that follows the curve during a pass; one lamp "named", one "not named".
* **Law and claims.** Receive only. Rules on receiving aircraft radio, on sharing aircraft data and on a microphone at a window differ by country (A). The handoff lists a regional ban on publishing material about drone attacks and air defence, and found no rule on a private sensor (R, not verified). So: no stored audio, no upload (R). The listing never says "warning", "detector" or "safety": SCALER's "not a dosimeter" rule. Do not sell the sensor at home before a lawyer reads this (A).
* **Reuse, scarce parts, cost.** The display: as G. A sensor kit would be 450 USD (A): too dear.
* **Hardest part.** False counts. Mopeds, boats and mowers sound like piston aircraft (R).
* **Cheapest test.** The handoff's offline pipeline on recorded audio. Then a week of ADS-B-only counts on the old clock.
* **Score.** Demand 3, effort 2, margin 2, stock 5.
* **To ask sky1.** Has code run on real audio? The speed and distance error on known flyovers? False air events a day at a street window? Is the event record fixed enough to feed a display? Is a smaller computer enough?

### P. The lit plate (mine)

* **Pitch.** The fascia art as an object: a black and gold board, warm LED filaments glowing through windows in the bare laminate, on a stand, 5 V. No tubes, no high voltage.
* **Buyer.** People who like the look but not 300 USD or 185 V; gift buyers; later, clock owners who want a second face.
* **How it works, reuse.** A fascia-size board from the art faces (ten fascia families drawn, R), nine or ten filaments at 20 mA (R), a dimmer, a USB socket, a printed stand. The same window method and fab order.
* **Scarce parts.** None.
* **Cost, price.** Parts 14 USD (A: board 6, filaments 3, the rest 5). Price 39 USD. One search found no price for lit board art (F).
* **Hardest part.** Light through bare laminate is unproven, and only one face is approved (R). It is décor: it sells on looks alone.
* **Cheapest test.** The window coupon of decision D1 a. Light it. Photograph it by day and by night.
* **Score.** Demand 3, effort 5, margin 3, stock 5.

### T. The tube tester and matcher (mine)

* **Pitch.** A bench tool that lights each cathode of a nixie, measures its striking and sustaining voltage, runs a recovery cycle on a poisoned cathode, and grades tubes into matched sets.
* **Buyer.** Clock builders and tube traders. First, this shop: audit step 3 is this rig, and ten units mean about 120 tubes and lamps to test (R). Hobbyists ask for such a tool; one paid 60 USD for a healer kit (F [w4], one data point).
* **How it works, reuse.** TERMINAL-06's converter with its clamp and bleeder, made adjustable from 120 to 200 V, with a current limit. A sense resistor on the ADC. One MPSA42 switch for each lead. Plug-in cards for each tube family. Results over USB.
* **Scarce parts.** None used up: it turns stock into graded stock. The owner's sweep found these tubes 1.5 to 7.6 times dearer in Japan than at home (R). Matched, tested sets may be the best margin here, if a lawful export and payment route exists (not checked).
* **Cost, price.** Parts 35 USD (A: converter 12, switches and cards 13, the rest 10). Kit 79 USD.
* **Hardest part.** Live leads on an open socket: an interlock and a hard current limit. Honest words: recovery works sometimes; a tube that has lost its gas stays dead (F [w4]).
* **Cheapest test.** Build the step 3 rig with the measurement added. Publish the table for the shop's own tubes. Ask one hobby list who wants a kit.
* **Score.** Demand 2, effort 4, margin 3, stock 5.

### F. The focus timer (mine)

* **Pitch.** Pull a lever: 25 minutes count down on nixie tubes. Return it: a break. No app, no network.
* **Buyer.** Desk-setup people, students, gift buyers. Plastic cube timers sell at 16 to 38 USD, a better one near 60 EUR (F [w8]); the search showed no nixie timer. It sells as a nixie object with a use, not as a timer.
* **How it works.** The core, with a face that carries one lever and two buttons on the fascia connector (R). Minutes and seconds on four tubes, an ИНС-1 as the running dot, a piezo. It is also a clock.
* **Why it matters.** The one face that needs no computer and no sensor: the easiest first sale of the core.
* **Scarce parts, cost.** 4 ИН-12 and one МТ1 lever. Levers: 10 held, 20 wanted for the TERMINAL-06 run (R). It needs its own lever source. Cost and price: the core's.
* **Hardest part.** The price beside a 20 USD timer; the levers.
* **Cheapest test.** Timer firmware on the old clock, a lever on a button input, a film. The waiting list gets three boxes: git, sky, timer. The count picks the first face.
* **Score.** Demand 3, effort 4, margin 3, stock 4.

## 6. The order to do things in

Section 4's seven steps are the order. On top of them:

1. Start now only what is bench work or needs no new hardware: the tube rig (T), the window coupon (P), firmware profiles on the old clock (G, F).
2. Plainly: no new concept's board starts before one TERMINAL-06 has run 48 hours in its case. The core board comes after, sized by the waiting list.
3. A's sensor stays sky1's software work until it counts right for a week.
4. Park QUADRANT, SCALER-06, MIMI-06, EMBER-06 and Staircase until the core sells.

## 8. The owner's answers (review cards, 2026-11-15)

1. **Payment and delivery abroad: yes.** The owner's route: bulk batches go to associates in Canada (Quebec), who repack and ship to buyers in the USA. So section 4's rule "foreign channels only when a payment route is confirmed" is met in principle. Still to settle before a foreign listing: who takes the money, customs on the bulk batch, and the cost of the second leg (not checked).
2. **The drawer:** the owner will send phone photos on a separate card (posted). Counts by tube type go into section 3 when they come.
3. **The stock boards' licence: checked.** The boards are AlexGyver's NixieClock v2.2, IN-12_norm. Its repository carries the MIT licence, "Copyright (c) 2020 AlexGyver" (read 2026-11-15). MIT permits use in units for sale; the copyright line and the licence text must go with each copy (put them in the unit's papers and on the product page). Not confirmed: the board says "Based on itworkclub PCB", and one search found no page or terms of that base's author. This is a reading of the licence text, not legal advice.
4. **Aircraft: the sensor is sky1's scope.** The owner: "the sensor work and stuff is scope of sky1, this prodict just gets the combined info hower it gets to it". So concept A is the display only. It shows the combined data that sky1's system gives it (sound, radio or both). The "open recipe" question falls away: what the sensor is, costs and may claim is sky1's work. A's effort score (2) counted the wait for the sensor; the display alone is G's effort (4) once the data line is fixed (inferred).
5. **Which face first:** it waits on the tier list. The rank card went out again as a tier list, one picture per concept: `Claude outputs/shop/tier/` (`make_cards.js` draws them; sketches, not designs).
